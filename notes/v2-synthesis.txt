# V2 synthesis — what the results collectively suggest

Written 2026-08-18, after steps 001-006 closed (H7, H6, 002b, H8, H9a,
H10) with steps 007/008 in flight and the sealed block pending. Five
observations, each grounded in at least two independent results, plus
the follow-ups they generate. Incorporated into the V2 spec (section 9.5)
in the same commit.

## 1. Inference is cheap to do well and expensive to do cheaply

The exact posterior beats everything, including Continuous (H7). The
learned one-shot compiler loses to plain gradient descent (H10). Hard
commitment loses to soft mixing everywhere (V1 discrete, H8). Meanwhile
128 steps of SGD on a 24-dimensional code — the dumbest inference
procedure available — beats every sophisticated alternative at this
scale.

Hypothesis this suggests: THE CONTINUOUS MIXTURE WINS BECAUSE IT IS
SECRETLY AN APPROXIMATE POSTERIOR. Soft mixture weights can represent
"probably slot 3, maybe slot 7" where hard routes and one-shot codes
cannot, and gradient descent on them is a passable variational update.
Testable at zero cost: correlate trained per-task mixture weights against
the exact route posterior from step 001 — both already in artifacts. If
they align, the V1 headline gains a mechanism sentence: the reusable
basis wins because soft mixing implements cheap approximate Bayesian
program inference. Best available follow-up analysis; added to the spec
as step 006b.

## 2. Allocation is easy; compression is hard — and the reason is the
##    currency of the training signal

H9a: near-perfect allocation (Spearman to -1.00) from nothing but a
penalty and gradient descent. But every attempt to convert structure
into shorter descriptions failed: MDL gates (collapse or nothing),
consolidation gate v1 (fires backwards), gate v2 (barely fires), and
both envelope wins reverse under the two-part code. Pattern: gradient
descent natively optimizes nats, so anything expressible as
"prediction loss under a penalty" works; nothing in the training signal
sees bits, so all bit-savings mechanisms are post-hoc bolt-ons — and
every bolt-on so far used the wrong instrument.

## 3. Non-identifiability is the recurring villain; the design principle
##    it dictates is: functional instruments only

Identity-based instruments have failed every time they met the
overcomplete regime: posterior entropy (gate v2, never fires because
ties keep entropy high), route agreement (H7's MAP disagrees with online
routes on 39/64 tasks while outperforming), raw slot-index metrics (V1,
silently zero). Behavioral instruments have worked every time:
functional distance vs untrained baseline, BMA prediction, frozen-
library transfer. V3 design principle: nothing downstream of the library
may reference identities — descriptors, gates, and merges must be
functional. The pre-registered predictive-equivalence gate (compile when
BMA and MAP behave identically, regardless of how many routes tie) is
the direct application.

## 4. The dream falsifier passing while H10 fails means amortization is
##    sample-starved, not wrong

The compiler learned the world's program distribution (3x dream-benefit
ratio between rho 1 and rho 0 proves it) but cannot out-amortize direct
optimization on 64 tasks of experience. "Mechanism sound, data starved"
is exactly the profile that predicts cross-world scale works — the
expert-iteration loop needs thousands of tasks; a lifetime has 64.

Side idea worth keeping: DREAM-BENEFIT IS A LIBRARY-QUALITY PROBE.
Fantasy data helps only insofar as the library matches the world, so the
measured dream benefit is a self-administered, ground-truth-free measure
of vocabulary quality — potentially valuable at scale, where ground
truth is precisely what is lost.

## 5. The open problem V2 hands V3: nothing yet wins both currencies

Continuous wins nats at high recurrence; shared-residual wins nats
everywhere in mixed worlds; nothing beats the fixed architectures in
bits. Per-task compilation never could — 183 bits saved per task is
noise against a 130k-bit residual store. The arithmetic singles out
PROMOTION (merging shared residual structure across tasks into new
library operators) as the only move on the board that attacks bits at
scale, because de-duplication across tasks is where real description-
length savings live. The two-currency tension is the project's thesis
resurfacing: V1's lesson was that sharing is an investment measured in
nats; V2's lesson is that the bits ledger needs a fundamentally
different mechanism, because gradient descent speaks only one of the
two languages.

## One-sentence diagnosis

V2 has established that a learner can read the economics of its world
nearly perfectly (H9a) and exploit them online (V1; H7's mechanism), but
cannot yet write them down compactly (H8, MDL, two-part reversals) — and
the failures share one root: identity-based instruments applied to a
non-identifiable representation.

## Post-script (same day): observation 1's mechanism hypothesis is dead

006b falsified it before the sealed block, as pre-registered: mixture
weights are uncorrelated with route-posterior marginals (Spearman ~ -0.03,
chance-level sign rate) and miscalibrated by three orders of magnitude.
The corrected picture: the continuous learner never does route inference —
it solves the problem in the basis's continuous function space with
distributed, compensatory mixtures, while the exact posterior solves it
with committed routes, and the committed solution is better when found.
The inference gap is therefore not "the learner commits too early" but
"the learner's optimizer cannot reach the route-structured region of the
solution space at all." V3's wake phase should be designed manifold-first,
with route structure imposed at consolidation rather than read out of
mixtures — and any future compilation gate must construct route
hypotheses independently (posterior machinery), never by interpreting
mixture weights.
