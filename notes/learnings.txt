# What We’ve Learned So Far

This note records the project's accumulating lessons. The original eight V1-era
items below are kept, but several have since been **confirmed on sealed
confirmatory worlds** (seeds 100–129 and 200–229), and a few attractive
secondary stories were **falsified and retracted**. The later sections add the
V2 sealed results, the falsifications, the selective-sharing outcome, and the
current V3 substrate question. Where a numbered item was updated or retracted by
later evidence, it is flagged inline.

## 1. ROW is a valid testbed for reusable computation

The basic benchmark appears to work as intended.

Scratch controls show no meaningful trend in intrinsic task difficulty over the lifetime. Output scales are tightly controlled and teacher operations do not saturate. Most importantly, the oracle demonstrates that the hidden reusable structure is genuinely exploitable.

After removing the accidental teacher-(\alpha) coupling and re-running the oracle with independently learned operator scales, the positive control still passes:

* early → late zero-shot NMSE: **0.0288 → 0.00159**
* early → late examples to NMSE 0.02: **72.8 → 0**
* unseen-composition zero-shot NMSE: **0.00175**
* teacher/learner primitive distance: **0.000484**

So a learner that knows the correct factorization can progressively learn the hidden operator library and eventually solve unseen compositions essentially from the library alone.

This establishes the phenomenon we want to study:

[
\boxed{
\text{past experience with reusable computation can make future tasks dramatically cheaper to learn}
}
]

---

## 2. A learned reusable continuous basis can discover and exploit that structure

The non-oracle Continuous learner is able to discover useful reusable transformations without being told the teacher's primitive identities or programs.

After symmetric optimizer tuning, Continuous outperformed the approximately inference-compute-matched Dense-C baseline on the initial exact-reuse development worlds and adapted more accurately to unseen compositions.

However, an important caveat is now explicit: the original tuning grid was performed before the learner-(\alpha) correction, so those exact frozen configurations still need to be revalidated before confirmatory evaluation.

The qualitative result nevertheless survives the leak correction on development world 0: the independently scaled Continuous model still substantially outperforms Dense-C.

So the original result does **not** appear to have been caused by leaking the teacher's residual scale.

---

## 3. The benefit of reuse depends on how much reusable computation actually exists

This is now the most important causal result.

At exact reuse:

[
\rho=1,
]

Continuous beats Dense-C.

At no functional reuse:

[
\rho=0,
]

Dense-C wins strongly.

And the new intermediate controls show that the crossover does **not** occur as soon as any correlation exists.

On development world 0:

[
\rho=0.5
]

produces measured residual-function correlation of only:

[
0.064,
]

and Dense-C still beats Continuous by about **2,159** total Gaussian-log-loss units.

At:

[
\rho=0.75,
]

measured residual-function correlation rises to:

[
0.317,
]

but Dense-C still wins by about **1,615**.

So the current evidence looks qualitatively like:

[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\rho & \text{measured recurrence} & \text{preferred learner}\\
\hline
0 & \approx 0 & \text{Dense}\\
0.5 & 0.064 & \text{Dense}\\
0.75 & 0.317 & \text{Dense}\\
1 & 1.0 & \text{Continuous}
\end{array}
]

This suggests something stronger than:

> reuse helps when things are reusable.

Instead:

[
\boxed{
\text{reuse has to be sufficiently valuable to amortize the cost of imposing a shared representation}
}
]

There may be a genuine **specialization-to-reuse crossover**.

---

## 4. Better abstraction can appear before it becomes economically worthwhile

There is an interesting wrinkle in the intermediate-(\rho) results.

> **RETRACTED on replication (reviewer-feedback-05).** The three-regime
> picture developed in this section — abstraction quality improving at
> intermediate ρ before it wins lifetime loss — was a world-0 development
> result. It **failed replication**: at ρ = 0.25/0.5/0.75 Continuous wins
> novel transfer on only one of three worlds and has worse mean novel NMSE at
> all three points. Adopt the simpler single-crossover picture instead (weak
> recurrence → sharing is harmful bias; strong recurrence → sharing is useful
> bias). The ρ=1 learning-to-learn ratio (~6.4× vs ~2.3×) is *also* still
> primarily a world-0 result and deserves proportionate suspicion until
> replicated — both this reviewer and reviewer-02 were seduced once by a
> world-0-only effect. The falsification itself is an asset: state it
> explicitly in the report.

At both:

[
\rho=0.5
]

and:

[
\rho=0.75,
]

Continuous still loses on total online prequential loss, but already has slightly better **32-shot adaptation to fresh compositions**.

That suggests two quantities can come apart:

[
\boxed{\text{quality of the accumulated abstraction}}
]

and

[
\boxed{\text{whether that abstraction has paid for itself yet}}.
]

Continuous may already be building a representation that transfers better to future tasks while paying an up-front cost to acquire and use that representation.

That gives us a plausible three-regime picture:

### Low recurrence

Task-specific specialization is better.

### Intermediate recurrence

Reusable abstractions begin to improve transfer, but their acquisition/inference cost has not yet been amortized.

### High recurrence

Reuse becomes sufficiently valuable that it wins both future adaptation and cumulative lifetime learning cost.

This is much closer to an **economics of abstraction** than a simple architecture comparison.

---

## 5. Reusable representations can make a learner progressively better at learning

The checkpoint experiment gives preliminary evidence for the stronger learning-to-learn claim.

On exact-reuse development world 0, Continuous's 32-shot NMSE on fresh unseen compositions improves from:

[
0.0298
]

after 8 lifetime tasks to:

[
0.00467
]

after 64.

Dense-C also improves:

[
0.0367\rightarrow0.0159,
]

but substantially less.

So the important phenomenon is not merely:

> Continuous has lower average loss.

It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{the computational representation accumulated over the lifetime increasingly reduces the cost of learning new compositions}
}
]

This needs replication across development worlds before becoming a strong claim, but it is exactly the effect the larger research program cares about.

> **Updated.** This has now replicated across all ten development worlds using
> the learnable-alpha checkpoint protocol (four fixed novel compositions per
> checkpoint). Mean Continuous vs Dense-C 32-shot NMSE is 0.02282/0.02300
> after 8 tasks, 0.01401/0.01652 after 16, 0.00687/0.01098 after 32, and
> 0.00343/0.00645 after 64. Continuous wins 4/10, 8/10, 10/10, and 10/10
> paired worlds respectively. A subtler and stronger framing later emerged:
> at checkpoint 8 Dense is equal or slightly better, and the two then
> **diverge with experience** — so the reusable advantage in novel adaptation
> is *acquired*, not architectural at initialization. Stop quoting the 6.4×
> vs 2.3× ratio as the headline; the equal-start divergence is the cleaner
> story (reviewer-feedback-08).

---

## 6. Discrete reusable programs are recoverable—but inferring them online is hard

The hard discrete learner recovers an unexpectedly clean computational decomposition.

In the initial experiment it achieved roughly:

* **92.2%** exact explained-route recovery
* **96.4%** route-position recovery
* primitive matching distance around **0.00229**
* very compact retained representation
* only about **768 multiply-adds** at hardened inference

So the model can genuinely learn something close to the latent computational library.

Its weakness is **program inference**.

The original global temperature annealing schedule made this problem look worse than it actually was. Re-annealing separately for every new task improves the discrete learner's lifetime log loss substantially and greatly improves fresh-task initialization.

But even under the improved schedule, it still substantially trails Continuous online.

This gives us an important decomposition:

[
\boxed{
\text{learning a reusable computational language}
\neq
\text{inferring the correct program in that language}
}
]

The discrete system is surprisingly good at the first problem and comparatively poor at the second.

---

## 7. Continuous and discrete representations expose a real time–space–learning tradeoff

The systems now appear to occupy different points on a resource frontier.

Roughly:

[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
& \text{online adaptation} & \text{storage} & \text{inference compute}\\
\hline
\text{Dense-C} & \text{good} & \text{large} & \text{moderate}\\
\text{Continuous} & \text{best so far} & \text{small} & \text{high}\\
\text{Discrete} & \text{slower program inference} & \text{very small} & \text{very low}
\end{array}
]

Continuous representations appear easier to search/infer because the learner can move smoothly through an operation space.

Discrete representations are much cheaper once the correct program is known.

This suggests a potentially useful longer-term architecture:

[
\boxed{
\text{fast continuous inference}
\rightarrow
\text{slow discrete consolidation}
}
]

During learning, maintain a flexible continuous representation.

Once enough evidence accumulates, compress/consolidate it into cheap discrete calls, macros, or eventually loops.

That possibility is currently speculative, but it follows naturally from the empirical tradeoff.

---

## 8. The current result is partly about having the right structural prior

Several controls make the interpretation more precise.

Removing teacher-(\alpha) information does not remove the Continuous advantage.

Increasing teacher rank from 8 to 16 while keeping learner rank 8 also preserves a substantial advantage.

But changing the learner's functional family from the teacher's tanh-based operators to GELU shrinks the world-0 advantage dramatically, to only about **441 log-loss units**.

Adding an identity basis element does not materially help.

So:

[
\boxed{
\text{the result is not simply parameter leakage or inability to skip a step}
}
]

but the match between the learner's representational family and the world's hidden computational structure **does matter**.

This is scientifically important.

The current experiment demonstrates that a learner with a good structural prior can discover reusable computation.

It does **not yet** demonstrate that a sufficiently general learner can discover an arbitrary reusable factorization irrespective of how that computation is implemented.

That latter problem is much closer to the original research goal.

> **Updated by the V2 sealed block and the GELU economics.** Family alignment
> is now part of the economics, not just a caveat. With aligned tanh operators
> the advantage at high recurrence is several thousand nats; with GELU it is
> shifted downward so far that even exact reuse (ρ=1) only reaches ≈ +134
> nats. The best model is no longer ΔL = f(r) but ΔL = f(r, A, C), where A is
> alignment and C the cost of using the representation; H6 suggests alignment
> enters mostly as an additive intercept/penalty shift with some slope
> attenuation, rather than turning recurrence sensitivity off. This is the
> right answer to the "your benchmark favors the learner" objection: yes, prior
> alignment has a quantitatively large measurable price.

# The compression interpretation is becoming clearer

The basic reason reuse should eventually pay is almost information-theoretic.

Suppose a reusable abstraction (P) costs:

[
C_P
]

to learn and retain.

If every later task saves:

[
s(r)
]

units of learning cost because it can reuse (P), then after (N) relevant tasks, reuse is worthwhile when roughly:

[
N,s(r)>C_P.
]

This predicts two different ways for abstraction to become economical:

[
r\uparrow
]

—the structure is more reusable—

or:

[
N\uparrow
]

—the learner gets more opportunities to amortize it.

So the real experimental object may eventually be a phase boundary:

[
\boxed{
N^*(r)
}
]

such that:

[
N<N^*(r)
\Rightarrow
\text{specialization is cheaper},
]

while:

[
N>N^*(r)
\Rightarrow
\text{reuse is cheaper}.
]

And presumably:

[
\frac{dN^*}{dr}<0.
]

Highly reusable structure should pay for itself quickly.

Weakly reusable structure may only become worthwhile over a very long lifetime.

This suggests that the most interesting eventual experiment is not merely a one-dimensional (\rho) sweep but a **phase diagram over recurrence and lifetime length**.

---

# What we have *not* established

Several important questions remain open.

### The full recurrence curve

We currently have only sparse development evidence. We need to locate and replicate the specialization-to-reuse crossover.

Configured (\rho) should be treated as the experimental intervention; **measured functional recurrence** is probably the more meaningful explanatory variable.

### Replicated learning-to-learn

The lifetime checkpoint result is still primarily a world-0 result.

We need replicated checkpoint curves.

### A generic hypernetwork baseline

The Continuous learner still has a favorable reusable three-stage residual prior.

A generic low-rank hypernetwork remains an essential control.

If it matches Continuous, the important object may be a **continuous operation manifold**, rather than an explicit basis library.

### Training compute

Dense-C is matched approximately on **inference-forward multiply-adds**, not total training FLOPs.

Continuous evaluates and backpropagates through every basis operator while training.

Any resource-efficiency claim must keep this distinction explicit.

### Batch regime

Current online updates effectively use:

[
1\text{ current example}+1\text{ replay example}.
]

That is symmetric across models but is a highly online regime and should later be ablated.

### Teacher/student family mismatch

The GELU result suggests the current architecture still benefits substantially from knowing the broad shape of the teacher's computational language.

A more general abstraction mechanism should discover functional equivalences rather than requiring architectural alignment.

### Dynamic restructuring

We have no evidence yet that merge/split, copy-on-write, or self-refactoring are necessary.

The fixed continuous basis may already capture much of the useful structure.

### Higher-order computation

We have not tested whether the learner can invent:

* repeated calls,
* macros,
* variable-depth execution,
* loops,
* branching,
* parameterized subroutines.

These are where reusable neural computation starts becoming a genuine computational language rather than simply a factorized function approximator.

---

# The broader conceptual lesson

The original question was:

> Does compositional reuse help?

That is increasingly looking like the wrong question.

Information theory already gives us strong reason to expect that sufficiently repeated shared structure should be cheaper to encode once and reuse.

The more interesting questions are now:

> **When does abstraction become economically worthwhile?**

> **What representation of reusable computation minimizes lifetime learning cost?**

> **Can the learner discover that representation without being given the correct factorization?**

> **Can it subsequently change and enrich its own computational language?**

So the project has evolved from:

[
\text{Does reuse work?}
]

toward:

[
\boxed{
\text{Can neural learning discover the representation that best exploits the algorithmic mutual information in its experience?}
}
]

And ultimately:

[
\boxed{
\text{Can a neural learner progressively reorganize its own computation so that future experience becomes cheaper to learn?}
}
]

That remains the larger research target.

# V2: what the sealed blocks confirmed and falsified

V2 ran the development program through two independent sealed confirmatory
blocks (V1 seeds 100–129 and V2 seeds 200–229) with frozen configs. The
results both **confirmed** the core phenomena and **falsified** several
attractive secondary stories — and the falsifications made the project more,
not less, coherent.

## 9. The economics of abstraction is a confirmed, quantitatively stable law

The dose-response ΔL = L_Dense − L_Reusable is approximately linear in
**measured** functional recurrence r, and the coefficients replicated across two
independent sealed blocks:

* development: ΔL ≈ 5906 r − 2477, R² ≈ 0.97
* sealed V2 Family A: slope 6,194 nats/unit recurrence on seeds 200–229
  (interval 4,000–7,500), pooled crossing 0.450 (0.40–0.60), per-world crossing
  mean 0.483 (0.42–0.58) with 30/30 worlds crossing, R² = 0.926 in measured
  coordinates.

This is no longer "a reusable architecture wins." It is a replicated,
quantitatively stable relationship: the value of sharing rises smoothly with
actual reusable functional structure, and the crossover is where that return
amortizes the cost/bias of sharing. Measured recurrence beats configured ρ as
the explanatory coordinate (R² rises from 0.646 to 0.974 on the 64-task
development curve), so report effects against measured recurrence.

## 10. The amortization story is "early amortization + stationary bias," not "wait long enough"

The crossover ρ*(N) moves from N=16 to N=32 but then **largely stops moving**
to N=64. So this is not simply "sharing always wins given enough lifetime." It
is early amortization of the shared-representation cost plus a largely
stationary representational bias. H5a (the crossover declines with N) is only
partially supported: 8/10 worlds decline from 16→64 tasks, but the mean is not
monotone (it edges up from 32→64). Report this as mixed, not as a general law.

## 11. Several attractive secondary hypotheses were falsified — and that is an asset

* **Mixtures as Bayesian route beliefs: falsified.** The continuous mixture
  weights are essentially uncorrelated with the exact route posterior (mean
  Spearman ≈ −0.03) and their entropy is wildly different (H_mix ∼ 1.4 vs
  H_posterior ∼ 0.001). Continuous exploits a continuous function space that
  contains solutions *not interpretable* as uncertainty over discrete programs.
  This explains why consolidation can't simply harden mixtures, why operator
  identity becomes ambiguous, and why hypernetworks become competitive at
  partial recurrence.
* **Transfer-before-amortization: falsified** (see the retraction on §4).
* **Strict amortization: weakened** (§10).
* **Within-lifetime amortized compiler (H10): failed.** Direct optimization
  beats the learned compiler within a 64-task lifetime; the compiler is
  sample-starved, not wrong in principle (dreaming helps more at ρ=1 than
  ρ=0). This is where cross-world meta-training becomes justified.

## 12. Three representational regimes, supported independently

The V1/V2 results together suggest a continuum rather than a single "reusable
basis":

* **Weak recurrence** → task-specific/specialized; Dense wins.
* **Partial recurrence** → a continuous function manifold is best; the generic
  hypernetwork becomes competitive and can even beat explicit slots at ρ=0.9.
* **Exact/near-exact recurrence** → discrete-like reusable primitives
  crystallize; explicit slots beat the hypernetwork; operator recovery improves
  sharply; frozen recomposition works.

The hypernetwork gives an independent instrument: at ρ=0.9 it beats Continuous
in 2/3 worlds; at ρ=1 Continuous wins all three. Across all ten development
worlds Continuous beats the tuned hypernetwork 10/10 on both lifetime loss and
novel 32-shot NMSE, while the hypernetwork beats Dense-C 10/10 on lifetime
loss — so a generic continuous operator manifold is useful, but the explicit
slot basis retains an exact-reuse advantage the hypernetwork does not explain
away.

## 13. Discreteness itself is not expensive; program inference is

The exact posterior over the discrete library's final learned operators scores
L ≈ −174,844 — *better* than Continuous (−170,967) — while the online discrete
learner's prequential loss is far worse. So the representation was good and the
inference algorithm was bad. This sharpens the §6 decomposition into a clean
separation: **representation learning** vs **program inference**, and it says
there is potentially a better-than-continuous discrete solution if we can infer
it efficiently.

# Selective sharing works — but cannot yet be encoded compactly

## 14. The learner can discover where sharing belongs, primitive by primitive

The shared-parent + residual model (H9) allocates specialization correctly:
on homogeneous worlds the residual magnitude decreases monotonically with
recurrence, and on mixed worlds per-primitive residual mass is inversely
related to actual primitive recurrence in 10/10 worlds. The hybrid beats both
fixed architectures on prediction cost in 10/10 mixed worlds (~+7,153 nats).
This is arguably the first result directly approaching the original
self-organizing-computation thesis: the model discovers how much sharing each
computation deserves. It **replicated 30/30 on the sealed V2 block**.

## 15. Allocation is solved; compression is not

But the shared-residual learner carries ~130,624 retained task bits, so under
actual two-part description length it **loses in every tested cell** (0/30 on
the sealed block, as pre-registered). This is the clearest handoff from V2 to
V3: the learner knows where to specialize but has no incentive/mechanism to
encode that specialization compactly. The optimizer sees prediction loss; it
does not see retained bits. Raw prequential wins can be reversed by
description-length accounting — always report both currencies before
"chooses its own sharing" style claims.

# V3: the current substrate question

## 16. The central V3 problem is now crisp

> Can a neural learner preserve the predictive benefit of flexible continuous
> specialization while progressively converting repeated structure into a
> shorter shared code?

Success is defined around **information migration**: D_task ↓, D_shared ↑,
D_total ↓ with held-out behavior constant, and then L_future,promoted <
L_future,unpromoted. If V3 gets both predictive and description-length wins,
that is qualitatively different from H9.

## 17. Zero information must equal the computational identity

The variational toy result was decisive: a Gaussian code spends ~79% of the
used-task cost just to say "do nothing," while a gated code spends ~0. This is
a constitutional requirement for the eventual architecture — **zero information
must correspond to the computational identity operation** (Δ=0; halt; no
write; no fork). Complexity should be explicit departures from a default, not
precision around a random variable. The Gaussian parameterization is
consequently the wrong primary PROMOTE substrate.

## 18. Task state contains three distinct currencies

REFERENCE, ARGUMENT, and INNOVATION should not share one generic regularizer:
D(reference) ∼ log₂ K, D(argument) modest, D(Δ) potentially huge. The
Gaussian prior treated all of these as floating-point coordinates deviating
from zero, which is conceptually wrong. This points toward a **typed
description language** (CALL / CALL(α) / INNOVATE(R) / PROMOTE→j_new, then
later MACRO/REPEAT/IF) before sophisticated program syntax — the
self-programming architecture may require typed semantics before it requires
control flow.

## 19. P-A (Gaussian variational wake) is falsified; gate before PROMOTE

The Gaussian variational wake was falsified 0/3 on the two-part objective, with
three independent strikes: wrong null-state semantics (q=p ⇏ Δ=0), the shared
prior kills routes (the cheap reuse channel) via symmetry-breaking, and the
rate–distortion curve has no hidden sweet spot. Decision: run the
preregistered gated experiment (P-I) **before any PROMOTE development**; if it
passes and preserves family residual structure, use gated innovation; if it
fails, fall back to the original H9 shared-residual learner (not Gaussian);
keep Gaussian only as a control. The H9 post-hoc result (≈89% of dense bits
under the strict matched-behavior margin) eliminates the "just a dumb int8
code" alternative — better coding alone recovers only ~11%, so the learner
actually needs to change the representation, which makes PROMOTE better
motivated and gives it a stronger causal test.

# Durable cross-cutting scientific findings

These are replicated experimental results that transcend any single numbered
hypothesis. They are the findings most likely to survive into the paper and into
future work, independent of how V3 turns out.

## A. The advantage is robust to every nuisance axis tested

On the exact-reuse development set, Continuous's lifetime-loss advantage is
10/10 under no replay, canonical (1:1) replay, 1:4 heavy replay, and reverse
task order — with bootstrap intervals [2,511–3,803] that exclude zero in every
condition. Reverse order has no consistent absolute penalty for either
architecture (mean reverse-minus-forward loss is −57 Continuous, −299 Dense-C,
both spanning zero), so the advantage is not a curriculum artifact. A second
independent model initialization reproduces it 10/10 (mean 3,081 vs 3,698
canonical). Opaque-ID reassignment is an exact invariant (every metric and final
tensor bitwise-identical after remapping). The one boundary: novel 32-shot
transfer is **not** robust without replay (6/10, interval spanning zero) but is
10/10 under canonical/strong replay and reverse order — distinguish the
replay-sensitive retained-transfer endpoint from the replay-robust cumulative
lifetime-cost result.

## B. Forward transfer is real, measurable, and grows with task index

Same-architecture fresh-task baselines establish explicit forward transfer: mean
per-task fresh-minus-lifetime Gaussian log loss is +366.8 (Continuous) and +211.6
(Dense-C), positive in all ten world means (97.5% / 93.75% of individual tasks).
Transfer **increases with task index in every world** for both models (slopes
+4.35 and +3.77 log-loss units per task). Continuous's extra transfer over
Dense-C is positive 10/10 at +155.2 [145.5, 165.3]. Route similarity is a strong
post-hoc explanatory variable: Continuous mean transfer rises from 26 (no
matching route positions) to 268 (one of three) to 402 (two of three); Dense-C
from −34 to 115 to 242. Teacher routes are used only for this analysis, never
training or prediction.

## C. The reusable advantage in novel adaptation is acquired, not architectural

At checkpoint 8 Dense-C is equal or slightly better at 32-shot novel adaptation;
the two then **diverge with experience**, and by 64 tasks Continuous is ~2×
better. This equal-start divergence is a stronger learning-to-learn claim than
any gain ratio — the advantage is acquired over the lifetime, not present at
initialization. Progressive operator recovery parallels it: from 8 to 64 tasks,
mean one-to-one primitive functional distance falls from 0.00786 to 0.00211
(Continuous) and 0.00745 to 0.00131 (Discrete), 10/10 worlds. Matched slots
also generalize to untrained future programs (future-program teacher-route NMSE
falls 0.0300→0.0126 Continuous, 0.0280→0.0106 Discrete between checkpoints 8
and 32).

## D. Measured recurrence is the right explanatory coordinate, but per-world crossing does not collapse

Re-coordinating the 64-task mean effect curve by measured residual-function
correlation makes it much more nearly linear: linear-fit R² rises from 0.646
(configured ρ) to 0.974 (measured recurrence), and residual RMSE falls from
1,329 to 361 log-loss units — so much of the apparent "elbow" is a coordinate
artifact. But H5b's tighter-alignment prediction fails: at 64 tasks per-world
crossover SD is 0.0173 in configured ρ versus 0.0381 in measured recurrence
(ranges 0.0482 vs 0.1308). Report this as mixed: the mean curve smooths in
measured coordinates, but the cross-world crossing does not collapse onto one
frontier. Do not claim a universal phase boundary at a precise ρ*.

## E. Mixture representations are distributed/compensatory, not slot-identical

A teacher route through Hungarian-matched individual slots is **not** an oracle
upper bound: Continuous's learned mixture route is better on completed tasks in
all ten worlds at every checkpoint (at 64 tasks mean NMSE 0.00191 vs 0.00937 for
the matched-slot teacher route). So the slot-to-primitive diagnostic is
basis-dependent — report progressive functional matching as evidence of
operator-quality improvement, not as proof that Continuous has uniquely
recovered the teacher's discrete representation. Discrete's learned hard route
and matched-slot teacher route do converge (0.00456 vs 0.00484, learned better
only 6/10), consistent with discrete routing recovering a cleaner symbolic
structure.

## F. Retention is a solved, measured resource frontier — distinct from lifetime loss

Across all ten development worlds, int8 quantization degradation is negligible
(mean 0.6–3.2e-6, worst individual task ≤1.4e-4), so retained-description
measurements are trustworthy. The retained-bits ordering
(Discrete 26,208 < Continuous 29,248 < Hypernetwork 33,928 < Dense-24 56,448 <
Dense-C 66,688) **diverges from the lifetime-loss ordering**, which is the
resource-frontier result: storage efficiency, online learning efficiency, and
inference compute are distinct objectives, and no single representation
dominates on all three. This is what justifies the weighted objective
J = L_preq + λD + μC as measurement rather than decoration.

## G. The amortization crossover moves early then stops — a mixed, not clean, law

Truncating existing ρ lifetimes gives mean configured-ρ crossovers of 0.869 (16
tasks), 0.822 (32), 0.826 (64). The 16→64 crossover declines in 8/10 worlds,
but the mean is **not monotone** (it edges up from 32→64). So "early
amortization + stationary representational bias" is closer to the truth than
"sharing always wins given enough lifetime," but H5a is only partially supported.
This is a durable negative result: do not claim a monotone economic law of
reuse amortization.

# V3 testbed: the validity-gate failure and the functional-substitutability gate

## 20. Spare shared capacity absorbs recurrence into routes, not residuals

The original V3 testbed assumed recurrent structure would sit patiently in
task-local residuals until sleep discovered it. It doesn't. When unused shared
capacity exists, gradient descent puts the recurring family structure **directly
into the shared basis and addresses it through the route/reference channel**.
So there was nothing for residual-clustering PROMOTE to factor. This is a "very
good failure" — the validity gate did its job — but it is not yet MDL-driven
promotion: the slots were preallocated and already paid for, and the optimizer
wasn't charged 192 vs 17,712 bits. It establishes that unused shared capacity
gets spontaneously used for recurrent cross-task structure, which is more
interesting than residual clustering but is a different claim than "promotion
creates new shared capacity at a cost."

## 21. The saturated-library / delayed-family testbed redesign

The cleaner V3 testbed forces the abstraction to be *born*: Phase A saturates
the existing library (K=6, verify each slot is load-bearing by ablation), then
Phase B introduces a hidden family component with no shared slot for it, so it
must appear as residuals R_τ ≈ A + ε_τ. Then PROMOTE literally creates
K=6→7 by setting P_7 = A. The accounting is explicit:
D_before = D(L_6) + Σ D(R_i) vs D_after = D(L_6) + D(P_7) + Σ[D(ref_7) + D(ε_i)],
promote iff D_after < D_before at matched prediction. An oracle gate J*(K+1) <
J*(K) must be established first so the world guarantees there is actually
something for PROMOTE to discover. This is the V3 analogue of validating that
V1's teacher structure exists before testing learners.

## 22. Parameter averaging is dead; functional substitutability is the gate

Parameter means captured only ~12% of residual value despite 1.000 family
recovery — the gauge-freedom / non-identifiability failure is now clean
evidence. The decisive object is the **functional** fit
A_f* = argmin_A Σ E_z[|R_τ(z) − A(z)|²], and the operational gate is
**family-specific functional substitution must recover a substantial fraction of
the full residual's value and outperform both zeroing and a single global
abstraction.** The gate now passes: residuals are load-bearing (0.0127→0.0224
when removed), family functional fit recovers 53.4% of behavioral value, global
recovers only 24.0% (29.4-point family advantage, 3/3 worlds). Leave-one-out
functional fitting (A_{f,−τ} substituted into held-out τ) is the remaining
prerequisite and doubles as a clean prospective-gate estimator. 53% capture is
probably enough — the economic question is whether the shared part saves enough
repeated private bits to pay for itself; real abstractions leave ε_τ behind.

## 23. PROMOTE redefined; the hierarchy of "shared structure" notions

PROMOTE is no longer "factor repeated residuals." It is **allocate shared
representational capacity to recurring innovation** — the structural event is
private computational capacity → shared computational capacity, which generalizes
to creating operators, low-rank directions, parameterized families, macros, or
memory entries. This also yields a two-level structural-learning theory: cheap
inner adaptation reorganizes existing capacity when it can express the recurrence;
expensive outer restructuring (PROMOTE) grows the language only when existing
capacity cannot absorb recurrence economically — so PROMOTE should fire when
(recurrence exists) + (existing capacity cannot absorb it economically). The
project has climbed a ladder of increasingly strong notions of "shared structure"
because every weaker criterion failed in a different way:

parameter similarity < statistical clustering < recoverable latent identity <
functional similarity < cross-task substitutability < prospective learning value.

That ladder is itself a methodological contribution. The learned abstraction will
be a reusable object in the **learner's own computational language** (not a
recovered teacher module), which is exactly the LLM-relevant problem.

# Current bottom line

The cleanest statement the evidence currently supports is:

> ROW provides **sealed-confirmatory** evidence (two independent blocks,
> parameter replication inside every frozen interval) that learned reusable
> neural representations reduce future learning cost when latent functional
> recurrence is sufficiently strong, with a quantitatively stable
> specialization-to-reuse crossover governed by measured recurrence, prior
> alignment, and cost of use. The learner can discover where sharing belongs
> (primitive by primitive, 30/30) but cannot yet encode that allocation
> compactly under a two-part code (0/30) — allocation is solved, compression
> is not. Discreteness itself is not expensive; program inference is. The
> current open question is whether repeated continuous specialization can be
> promoted into a cheaper shared representation while preserving its
> predictive advantage — and that requires a representation whose zero state
> is the computational identity.

The next step is **not more architectural complexity**. It is the V3 promotion
experiment on a correctly-coded (gated or H9-fallback) wake substrate, with
refusal controls, under both predictive and description-length accounting. The
immediate prerequisite is the **saturated-library testbed** with the
functional-substitutability validity gate (family fit > global > zero,
leave-one-out confirmed) and the oracle J*(K+1) < J*(K) established, so that
PROMOTE has something real to discover — not a world where spare shared capacity
silently absorbs the recurrence into routes.
