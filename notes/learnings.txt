# What We’ve Learned So Far

This note records the project's accumulating lessons. The original eight V1-era
items below are kept, but several have since been **confirmed on sealed
confirmatory worlds** (seeds 100–129 and 200–229), and a few attractive
secondary stories were **falsified and retracted**. The later sections add the
V2 sealed results, the falsifications, the selective-sharing outcome, and the
current V3 substrate question. Where a numbered item was updated or retracted by
later evidence, it is flagged inline.

## 1. ROW is a valid testbed for reusable computation

The basic benchmark appears to work as intended.

Scratch controls show no meaningful trend in intrinsic task difficulty over the lifetime. Output scales are tightly controlled and teacher operations do not saturate. Most importantly, the oracle demonstrates that the hidden reusable structure is genuinely exploitable.

After removing the accidental teacher-(\alpha) coupling and re-running the oracle with independently learned operator scales, the positive control still passes:

* early → late zero-shot NMSE: **0.0288 → 0.00159**
* early → late examples to NMSE 0.02: **72.8 → 0**
* unseen-composition zero-shot NMSE: **0.00175**
* teacher/learner primitive distance: **0.000484**

So a learner that knows the correct factorization can progressively learn the hidden operator library and eventually solve unseen compositions essentially from the library alone.

This establishes the phenomenon we want to study:

[
\boxed{
\text{past experience with reusable computation can make future tasks dramatically cheaper to learn}
}
]

---

## 2. A learned reusable continuous basis can discover and exploit that structure

The non-oracle Continuous learner is able to discover useful reusable transformations without being told the teacher's primitive identities or programs.

After symmetric optimizer tuning, Continuous outperformed the approximately inference-compute-matched Dense-C baseline on the initial exact-reuse development worlds and adapted more accurately to unseen compositions.

However, an important caveat is now explicit: the original tuning grid was performed before the learner-(\alpha) correction, so those exact frozen configurations still need to be revalidated before confirmatory evaluation.

The qualitative result nevertheless survives the leak correction on development world 0: the independently scaled Continuous model still substantially outperforms Dense-C.

So the original result does **not** appear to have been caused by leaking the teacher's residual scale.

---

## 3. The benefit of reuse depends on how much reusable computation actually exists

This is now the most important causal result.

At exact reuse:

[
\rho=1,
]

Continuous beats Dense-C.

At no functional reuse:

[
\rho=0,
]

Dense-C wins strongly.

And the new intermediate controls show that the crossover does **not** occur as soon as any correlation exists.

On development world 0:

[
\rho=0.5
]

produces measured residual-function correlation of only:

[
0.064,
]

and Dense-C still beats Continuous by about **2,159** total Gaussian-log-loss units.

At:

[
\rho=0.75,
]

measured residual-function correlation rises to:

[
0.317,
]

but Dense-C still wins by about **1,615**.

So the current evidence looks qualitatively like:

[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\rho & \text{measured recurrence} & \text{preferred learner}\\
\hline
0 & \approx 0 & \text{Dense}\\
0.5 & 0.064 & \text{Dense}\\
0.75 & 0.317 & \text{Dense}\\
1 & 1.0 & \text{Continuous}
\end{array}
]

This suggests something stronger than:

> reuse helps when things are reusable.

Instead:

[
\boxed{
\text{reuse has to be sufficiently valuable to amortize the cost of imposing a shared representation}
}
]

There may be a genuine **specialization-to-reuse crossover**.

---

## 4. Better abstraction can appear before it becomes economically worthwhile

There is an interesting wrinkle in the intermediate-(\rho) results.

> **RETRACTED on replication (reviewer-feedback-05).** The three-regime
> picture developed in this section — abstraction quality improving at
> intermediate ρ before it wins lifetime loss — was a world-0 development
> result. It **failed replication**: at ρ = 0.25/0.5/0.75 Continuous wins
> novel transfer on only one of three worlds and has worse mean novel NMSE at
> all three points. Adopt the simpler single-crossover picture instead (weak
> recurrence → sharing is harmful bias; strong recurrence → sharing is useful
> bias). The ρ=1 learning-to-learn ratio (~6.4× vs ~2.3×) is *also* still
> primarily a world-0 result and deserves proportionate suspicion until
> replicated — both this reviewer and reviewer-02 were seduced once by a
> world-0-only effect. The falsification itself is an asset: state it
> explicitly in the report.

At both:

[
\rho=0.5
]

and:

[
\rho=0.75,
]

Continuous still loses on total online prequential loss, but already has slightly better **32-shot adaptation to fresh compositions**.

That suggests two quantities can come apart:

[
\boxed{\text{quality of the accumulated abstraction}}
]

and

[
\boxed{\text{whether that abstraction has paid for itself yet}}.
]

Continuous may already be building a representation that transfers better to future tasks while paying an up-front cost to acquire and use that representation.

That gives us a plausible three-regime picture:

### Low recurrence

Task-specific specialization is better.

### Intermediate recurrence

Reusable abstractions begin to improve transfer, but their acquisition/inference cost has not yet been amortized.

### High recurrence

Reuse becomes sufficiently valuable that it wins both future adaptation and cumulative lifetime learning cost.

This is much closer to an **economics of abstraction** than a simple architecture comparison.

---

## 5. Reusable representations can make a learner progressively better at learning

The checkpoint experiment gives preliminary evidence for the stronger learning-to-learn claim.

On exact-reuse development world 0, Continuous's 32-shot NMSE on fresh unseen compositions improves from:

[
0.0298
]

after 8 lifetime tasks to:

[
0.00467
]

after 64.

Dense-C also improves:

[
0.0367\rightarrow0.0159,
]

but substantially less.

So the important phenomenon is not merely:

> Continuous has lower average loss.

It is:

[
\boxed{
\text{the computational representation accumulated over the lifetime increasingly reduces the cost of learning new compositions}
}
]

This needs replication across development worlds before becoming a strong claim, but it is exactly the effect the larger research program cares about.

> **Updated.** This has now replicated across all ten development worlds using
> the learnable-alpha checkpoint protocol (four fixed novel compositions per
> checkpoint). Mean Continuous vs Dense-C 32-shot NMSE is 0.02282/0.02300
> after 8 tasks, 0.01401/0.01652 after 16, 0.00687/0.01098 after 32, and
> 0.00343/0.00645 after 64. Continuous wins 4/10, 8/10, 10/10, and 10/10
> paired worlds respectively. A subtler and stronger framing later emerged:
> at checkpoint 8 Dense is equal or slightly better, and the two then
> **diverge with experience** — so the reusable advantage in novel adaptation
> is *acquired*, not architectural at initialization. Stop quoting the 6.4×
> vs 2.3× ratio as the headline; the equal-start divergence is the cleaner
> story (reviewer-feedback-08).

---

## 6. Discrete reusable programs are recoverable—but inferring them online is hard

The hard discrete learner recovers an unexpectedly clean computational decomposition.

In the initial experiment it achieved roughly:

* **92.2%** exact explained-route recovery
* **96.4%** route-position recovery
* primitive matching distance around **0.00229**
* very compact retained representation
* only about **768 multiply-adds** at hardened inference

So the model can genuinely learn something close to the latent computational library.

Its weakness is **program inference**.

The original global temperature annealing schedule made this problem look worse than it actually was. Re-annealing separately for every new task improves the discrete learner's lifetime log loss substantially and greatly improves fresh-task initialization.

But even under the improved schedule, it still substantially trails Continuous online.

This gives us an important decomposition:

[
\boxed{
\text{learning a reusable computational language}
\neq
\text{inferring the correct program in that language}
}
]

The discrete system is surprisingly good at the first problem and comparatively poor at the second.

---

## 7. Continuous and discrete representations expose a real time–space–learning tradeoff

The systems now appear to occupy different points on a resource frontier.

Roughly:

[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
& \text{online adaptation} & \text{storage} & \text{inference compute}\\
\hline
\text{Dense-C} & \text{good} & \text{large} & \text{moderate}\\
\text{Continuous} & \text{best so far} & \text{small} & \text{high}\\
\text{Discrete} & \text{slower program inference} & \text{very small} & \text{very low}
\end{array}
]

Continuous representations appear easier to search/infer because the learner can move smoothly through an operation space.

Discrete representations are much cheaper once the correct program is known.

This suggests a potentially useful longer-term architecture:

[
\boxed{
\text{fast continuous inference}
\rightarrow
\text{slow discrete consolidation}
}
]

During learning, maintain a flexible continuous representation.

Once enough evidence accumulates, compress/consolidate it into cheap discrete calls, macros, or eventually loops.

That possibility is currently speculative, but it follows naturally from the empirical tradeoff.

---

## 8. The current result is partly about having the right structural prior

Several controls make the interpretation more precise.

Removing teacher-(\alpha) information does not remove the Continuous advantage.

Increasing teacher rank from 8 to 16 while keeping learner rank 8 also preserves a substantial advantage.

But changing the learner's functional family from the teacher's tanh-based operators to GELU shrinks the world-0 advantage dramatically, to only about **441 log-loss units**.

Adding an identity basis element does not materially help.

So:

[
\boxed{
\text{the result is not simply parameter leakage or inability to skip a step}
}
]

but the match between the learner's representational family and the world's hidden computational structure **does matter**.

This is scientifically important.

The current experiment demonstrates that a learner with a good structural prior can discover reusable computation.

It does **not yet** demonstrate that a sufficiently general learner can discover an arbitrary reusable factorization irrespective of how that computation is implemented.

That latter problem is much closer to the original research goal.

> **Updated by the V2 sealed block and the GELU economics.** Family alignment
> is now part of the economics, not just a caveat. With aligned tanh operators
> the advantage at high recurrence is several thousand nats; with GELU it is
> shifted downward so far that even exact reuse (ρ=1) only reaches ≈ +134
> nats. The best model is no longer ΔL = f(r) but ΔL = f(r, A, C), where A is
> alignment and C the cost of using the representation; H6 suggests alignment
> enters mostly as an additive intercept/penalty shift with some slope
> attenuation, rather than turning recurrence sensitivity off. This is the
> right answer to the "your benchmark favors the learner" objection: yes, prior
> alignment has a quantitatively large measurable price.

---

# The compression interpretation is becoming clearer

The basic reason reuse should eventually pay is almost information-theoretic.

Suppose a reusable abstraction (P) costs:

[
C_P
]

to learn and retain.

If every later task saves:

[
s(r)
]

units of learning cost because it can reuse (P), then after (N) relevant tasks, reuse is worthwhile when roughly:

[
N,s(r)>C_P.
]

This predicts two different ways for abstraction to become economical:

[
r\uparrow
]

—the structure is more reusable—

or:

[
N\uparrow
]

—the learner gets more opportunities to amortize it.

So the real experimental object may eventually be a phase boundary:

[
\boxed{
N^*(r)
}
]

such that:

[
N<N^*(r)
\Rightarrow
\text{specialization is cheaper},
]

while:

[
N>N^*(r)
\Rightarrow
\text{reuse is cheaper}.
]

And presumably:

[
\frac{dN^*}{dr}<0.
]

Highly reusable structure should pay for itself quickly.

Weakly reusable structure may only become worthwhile over a very long lifetime.

This suggests that the most interesting eventual experiment is not merely a one-dimensional (\rho) sweep but a **phase diagram over recurrence and lifetime length**.

---

# What we have *not* established

Several important questions remain open.

### The full recurrence curve

We currently have only sparse development evidence. We need to locate and replicate the specialization-to-reuse crossover.

Configured (\rho) should be treated as the experimental intervention; **measured functional recurrence** is probably the more meaningful explanatory variable.

### Replicated learning-to-learn

The lifetime checkpoint result is still primarily a world-0 result.

We need replicated checkpoint curves.

### A generic hypernetwork baseline

The Continuous learner still has a favorable reusable three-stage residual prior.

A generic low-rank hypernetwork remains an essential control.

If it matches Continuous, the important object may be a **continuous operation manifold**, rather than an explicit basis library.

### Training compute

Dense-C is matched approximately on **inference-forward multiply-adds**, not total training FLOPs.

Continuous evaluates and backpropagates through every basis operator while training.

Any resource-efficiency claim must keep this distinction explicit.

### Batch regime

Current online updates effectively use:

[
1\text{ current example}+1\text{ replay example}.
]

That is symmetric across models but is a highly online regime and should later be ablated.

### Teacher/student family mismatch

The GELU result suggests the current architecture still benefits substantially from knowing the broad shape of the teacher's computational language.

A more general abstraction mechanism should discover functional equivalences rather than requiring architectural alignment.

### Dynamic restructuring

We have no evidence yet that merge/split, copy-on-write, or self-refactoring are necessary.

The fixed continuous basis may already capture much of the useful structure.

### Higher-order computation

We have not tested whether the learner can invent:

* repeated calls,
* macros,
* variable-depth execution,
* loops,
* branching,
* parameterized subroutines.

These are where reusable neural computation starts becoming a genuine computational language rather than simply a factorized function approximator.

---

# The broader conceptual lesson

The original question was:

> Does compositional reuse help?

That is increasingly looking like the wrong question.

Information theory already gives us strong reason to expect that sufficiently repeated shared structure should be cheaper to encode once and reuse.

The more interesting questions are now:

> **When does abstraction become economically worthwhile?**

> **What representation of reusable computation minimizes lifetime learning cost?**

> **Can the learner discover that representation without being given the correct factorization?**

> **Can it subsequently change and enrich its own computational language?**

So the project has evolved from:

[
\text{Does reuse work?}
]

toward:

[
\boxed{
\text{Can neural learning discover the representation that best exploits the algorithmic mutual information in its experience?}
}
]

And ultimately:

[
\boxed{
\text{Can a neural learner progressively reorganize its own computation so that future experience becomes cheaper to learn?}
}
]

That remains the larger research target.

# V2: what the sealed blocks confirmed and falsified

V2 ran the development program through two independent sealed confirmatory
blocks (V1 seeds 100–129 and V2 seeds 200–229) with frozen configs. The
results both **confirmed** the core phenomena and **falsified** several
attractive secondary stories — and the falsifications made the project more,
not less, coherent.

## 9. The economics of abstraction is a confirmed, quantitatively stable law

The dose-response ΔL = L_Dense − L_Reusable is approximately linear in
**measured** functional recurrence r, and the coefficients replicated across two
independent sealed blocks:

* development: ΔL ≈ 5906 r − 2477, R² ≈ 0.97
* sealed V2 Family A: slope 6,194 nats/unit recurrence on seeds 200–229
  (interval 4,000–7,500), pooled crossing 0.450 (0.40–0.60), per-world crossing
  mean 0.483 (0.42–0.58) with 30/30 worlds crossing, R² = 0.926 in measured
  coordinates.

This is no longer "a reusable architecture wins." It is a replicated,
quantitatively stable relationship: the value of sharing rises smoothly with
actual reusable functional structure, and the crossover is where that return
amortizes the cost/bias of sharing. Measured recurrence beats configured ρ as
the explanatory coordinate (R² rises from 0.646 to 0.974 on the 64-task
development curve), so report effects against measured recurrence.

## 10. The amortization story is "early amortization + stationary bias," not "wait long enough"

The crossover ρ*(N) moves from N=16 to N=32 but then **largely stops moving**
to N=64. So this is not simply "sharing always wins given enough lifetime." It
is early amortization of the shared-representation cost plus a largely
stationary representational bias. H5a (the crossover declines with N) is only
partially supported: 8/10 worlds decline from 16→64 tasks, but the mean is not
monotone (it edges up from 32→64). Report this as mixed, not as a general law.

## 11. Several attractive secondary hypotheses were falsified — and that is an asset

* **Mixtures as Bayesian route beliefs: falsified.** The continuous mixture
  weights are essentially uncorrelated with the exact route posterior (mean
  Spearman ≈ −0.03) and their entropy is wildly different (H_mix ∼ 1.4 vs
  H_posterior ∼ 0.001). Continuous exploits a continuous function space that
  contains solutions *not interpretable* as uncertainty over discrete programs.
  This explains why consolidation can't simply harden mixtures, why operator
  identity becomes ambiguous, and why hypernetworks become competitive at
  partial recurrence.
* **Transfer-before-amortization: falsified** (see the retraction on §4).
* **Strict amortization: weakened** (§10).
* **Within-lifetime amortized compiler (H10): failed.** Direct optimization
  beats the learned compiler within a 64-task lifetime; the compiler is
  sample-starved, not wrong in principle (dreaming helps more at ρ=1 than
  ρ=0). This is where cross-world meta-training becomes justified.

## 12. Three representational regimes, supported independently

The V1/V2 results together suggest a continuum rather than a single "reusable
basis":

* **Weak recurrence** → task-specific/specialized; Dense wins.
* **Partial recurrence** → a continuous function manifold is best; the generic
  hypernetwork becomes competitive and can even beat explicit slots at ρ=0.9.
* **Exact/near-exact recurrence** → discrete-like reusable primitives
  crystallize; explicit slots beat the hypernetwork; operator recovery improves
  sharply; frozen recomposition works.

The hypernetwork gives an independent instrument: at ρ=0.9 it beats Continuous
in 2/3 worlds; at ρ=1 Continuous wins all three. Across all ten development
worlds Continuous beats the tuned hypernetwork 10/10 on both lifetime loss and
novel 32-shot NMSE, while the hypernetwork beats Dense-C 10/10 on lifetime
loss — so a generic continuous operator manifold is useful, but the explicit
slot basis retains an exact-reuse advantage the hypernetwork does not explain
away.

## 13. Discreteness itself is not expensive; program inference is

The exact posterior over the discrete library's final learned operators scores
L ≈ −174,844 — *better* than Continuous (−170,967) — while the online discrete
learner's prequential loss is far worse. So the representation was good and the
inference algorithm was bad. This sharpens the §6 decomposition into a clean
separation: **representation learning** vs **program inference**, and it says
there is potentially a better-than-continuous discrete solution if we can infer
it efficiently.

# Selective sharing works — but cannot yet be encoded compactly

## 14. The learner can discover where sharing belongs, primitive by primitive

The shared-parent + residual model (H9) allocates specialization correctly:
on homogeneous worlds the residual magnitude decreases monotonically with
recurrence, and on mixed worlds per-primitive residual mass is inversely
related to actual primitive recurrence in 10/10 worlds. The hybrid beats both
fixed architectures on prediction cost in 10/10 mixed worlds (~+7,153 nats).
This is arguably the first result directly approaching the original
self-organizing-computation thesis: the model discovers how much sharing each
computation deserves. It **replicated 30/30 on the sealed V2 block**.

## 15. Allocation is solved; compression is not

But the shared-residual learner carries ~130,624 retained task bits, so under
actual two-part description length it **loses in every tested cell** (0/30 on
the sealed block, as pre-registered). This is the clearest handoff from V2 to
V3: the learner knows where to specialize but has no incentive/mechanism to
encode that specialization compactly. The optimizer sees prediction loss; it
does not see retained bits. Raw prequential wins can be reversed by
description-length accounting — always report both currencies before
"chooses its own sharing" style claims.

# V3: the current substrate question

## 16. The central V3 problem is now crisp

> Can a neural learner preserve the predictive benefit of flexible continuous
> specialization while progressively converting repeated structure into a
> shorter shared code?

Success is defined around **information migration**: D_task ↓, D_shared ↑,
D_total ↓ with held-out behavior constant, and then L_future,promoted <
L_future,unpromoted. If V3 gets both predictive and description-length wins,
that is qualitatively different from H9.

## 17. Zero information must equal the computational identity

The variational toy result was decisive: a Gaussian code spends ~79% of the
used-task cost just to say "do nothing," while a gated code spends ~0. This is
a constitutional requirement for the eventual architecture — **zero information
must correspond to the computational identity operation** (Δ=0; halt; no
write; no fork). Complexity should be explicit departures from a default, not
precision around a random variable. The Gaussian parameterization is
consequently the wrong primary PROMOTE substrate.

## 18. Task state contains three distinct currencies

REFERENCE, ARGUMENT, and INNOVATION should not share one generic regularizer:
D(reference) ∼ log₂ K, D(argument) modest, D(Δ) potentially huge. The
Gaussian prior treated all of these as floating-point coordinates deviating
from zero, which is conceptually wrong. This points toward a **typed
description language** (CALL / CALL(α) / INNOVATE(R) / PROMOTE→j_new, then
later MACRO/REPEAT/IF) before sophisticated program syntax — the
self-programming architecture may require typed semantics before it requires
control flow.

## 19. P-A (Gaussian variational wake) is falsified; gate before PROMOTE

The Gaussian variational wake was falsified 0/3 on the two-part objective, with
three independent strikes: wrong null-state semantics (q=p ⇏ Δ=0), the shared
prior kills routes (the cheap reuse channel) via symmetry-breaking, and the
rate–distortion curve has no hidden sweet spot. Decision: run the
preregistered gated experiment (P-I) **before any PROMOTE development**; if it
passes and preserves family residual structure, use gated innovation; if it
fails, fall back to the original H9 shared-residual learner (not Gaussian);
keep Gaussian only as a control. The H9 post-hoc result (≈89% of dense bits
under the strict matched-behavior margin) eliminates the "just a dumb int8
code" alternative — better coding alone recovers only ~11%, so the learner
actually needs to change the representation, which makes PROMOTE better
motivated and gives it a stronger causal test.

# Durable cross-cutting scientific findings

These are replicated experimental results that transcend any single numbered
hypothesis. They are the findings most likely to survive into the paper and into
future work, independent of how V3 turns out.

## A. The advantage is robust to every nuisance axis tested

On the exact-reuse development set, Continuous's lifetime-loss advantage is
10/10 under no replay, canonical (1:1) replay, 1:4 heavy replay, and reverse
task order — with bootstrap intervals [2,511–3,803] that exclude zero in every
condition. Reverse order has no consistent absolute penalty for either
architecture (mean reverse-minus-forward loss is −57 Continuous, −299 Dense-C,
both spanning zero), so the advantage is not a curriculum artifact. A second
independent model initialization reproduces it 10/10 (mean 3,081 vs 3,698
canonical). Opaque-ID reassignment is an exact invariant (every metric and final
tensor bitwise-identical after remapping). The one boundary: novel 32-shot
transfer is **not** robust without replay (6/10, interval spanning zero) but is
10/10 under canonical/strong replay and reverse order — distinguish the
replay-sensitive retained-transfer endpoint from the replay-robust cumulative
lifetime-cost result.

## B. Forward transfer is real, measurable, and grows with task index

Same-architecture fresh-task baselines establish explicit forward transfer: mean
per-task fresh-minus-lifetime Gaussian log loss is +366.8 (Continuous) and +211.6
(Dense-C), positive in all ten world means (97.5% / 93.75% of individual tasks).
Transfer **increases with task index in every world** for both models (slopes
+4.35 and +3.77 log-loss units per task). Continuous's extra transfer over
Dense-C is positive 10/10 at +155.2 [145.5, 165.3]. Route similarity is a strong
post-hoc explanatory variable: Continuous mean transfer rises from 26 (no
matching route positions) to 268 (one of three) to 402 (two of three); Dense-C
from −34 to 115 to 242. Teacher routes are used only for this analysis, never
training or prediction.

## C. The reusable advantage in novel adaptation is acquired, not architectural

At checkpoint 8 Dense-C is equal or slightly better at 32-shot novel adaptation;
the two then **diverge with experience**, and by 64 tasks Continuous is ~2×
better. This equal-start divergence is a stronger learning-to-learn claim than
any gain ratio — the advantage is acquired over the lifetime, not present at
initialization. Progressive operator recovery parallels it: from 8 to 64 tasks,
mean one-to-one primitive functional distance falls from 0.00786 to 0.00211
(Continuous) and 0.00745 to 0.00131 (Discrete), 10/10 worlds. Matched slots
also generalize to untrained future programs (future-program teacher-route NMSE
falls 0.0300→0.0126 Continuous, 0.0280→0.0106 Discrete between checkpoints 8
and 32).

## D. Measured recurrence is the right explanatory coordinate, but per-world crossing does not collapse

Re-coordinating the 64-task mean effect curve by measured residual-function
correlation makes it much more nearly linear: linear-fit R² rises from 0.646
(configured ρ) to 0.974 (measured recurrence), and residual RMSE falls from
1,329 to 361 log-loss units — so much of the apparent "elbow" is a coordinate
artifact. But H5b's tighter-alignment prediction fails: at 64 tasks per-world
crossover SD is 0.0173 in configured ρ versus 0.0381 in measured recurrence
(ranges 0.0482 vs 0.1308). Report this as mixed: the mean curve smooths in
measured coordinates, but the cross-world crossing does not collapse onto one
frontier. Do not claim a universal phase boundary at a precise ρ*.

## E. Mixture representations are distributed/compensatory, not slot-identical

A teacher route through Hungarian-matched individual slots is **not** an oracle
upper bound: Continuous's learned mixture route is better on completed tasks in
all ten worlds at every checkpoint (at 64 tasks mean NMSE 0.00191 vs 0.00937 for
the matched-slot teacher route). So the slot-to-primitive diagnostic is
basis-dependent — report progressive functional matching as evidence of
operator-quality improvement, not as proof that Continuous has uniquely
recovered the teacher's discrete representation. Discrete's learned hard route
and matched-slot teacher route do converge (0.00456 vs 0.00484, learned better
only 6/10), consistent with discrete routing recovering a cleaner symbolic
structure.

## F. Retention is a solved, measured resource frontier — distinct from lifetime loss

Across all ten development worlds, int8 quantization degradation is negligible
(mean 0.6–3.2e-6, worst individual task ≤1.4e-4), so retained-description
measurements are trustworthy. The retained-bits ordering
(Discrete 26,208 < Continuous 29,248 < Hypernetwork 33,928 < Dense-24 56,448 <
Dense-C 66,688) **diverges from the lifetime-loss ordering**, which is the
resource-frontier result: storage efficiency, online learning efficiency, and
inference compute are distinct objectives, and no single representation
dominates on all three. This is what justifies the weighted objective
J = L_preq + λD + μC as measurement rather than decoration.

## G. The amortization crossover moves early then stops — a mixed, not clean, law

Truncating existing ρ lifetimes gives mean configured-ρ crossovers of 0.869 (16
tasks), 0.822 (32), 0.826 (64). The 16→64 crossover declines in 8/10 worlds,
but the mean is **not monotone** (it edges up from 32→64). So "early
amortization + stationary representational bias" is closer to the truth than
"sharing always wins given enough lifetime," but H5a is only partially supported.
This is a durable negative result: do not claim a monotone economic law of
reuse amortization.

# V3 testbed: the validity-gate failure and the functional-substitutability gate

## 20. Spare shared capacity absorbs recurrence into routes, not residuals

The original V3 testbed assumed recurrent structure would sit patiently in
task-local residuals until sleep discovered it. It doesn't. When unused shared
capacity exists, gradient descent puts the recurring family structure **directly
into the shared basis and addresses it through the route/reference channel**.
So there was nothing for residual-clustering PROMOTE to factor. This is a "very
good failure" — the validity gate did its job — but it is not yet MDL-driven
promotion: the slots were preallocated and already paid for, and the optimizer
wasn't charged 192 vs 17,712 bits. It establishes that unused shared capacity
gets spontaneously used for recurrent cross-task structure, which is more
interesting than residual clustering but is a different claim than "promotion
creates new shared capacity at a cost."

## 21. The saturated-library / delayed-family testbed redesign

The cleaner V3 testbed forces the abstraction to be *born*: Phase A saturates
the existing library (K=6, verify each slot is load-bearing by ablation), then
Phase B introduces a hidden family component with no shared slot for it, so it
must appear as residuals R_τ ≈ A + ε_τ. Then PROMOTE literally creates
K=6→7 by setting P_7 = A. The accounting is explicit:
D_before = D(L_6) + Σ D(R_i) vs D_after = D(L_6) + D(P_7) + Σ[D(ref_7) + D(ε_i)],
promote iff D_after < D_before at matched prediction. An oracle gate J*(K+1) <
J*(K) must be established first so the world guarantees there is actually
something for PROMOTE to discover. This is the V3 analogue of validating that
V1's teacher structure exists before testing learners.

## 22. Parameter averaging is dead; functional substitutability is the gate

Parameter means captured only ~12% of residual value despite 1.000 family
recovery — the gauge-freedom / non-identifiability failure is now clean
evidence. The decisive object is the **functional** fit
A_f* = argmin_A Σ E_z[|R_τ(z) − A(z)|²], and the operational gate is
**family-specific functional substitution must recover a substantial fraction of
the full residual's value and outperform both zeroing and a single global
abstraction.** The gate now passes: residuals are load-bearing (0.0127→0.0224
when removed), family functional fit recovers 53.4% of behavioral value, global
recovers only 24.0% (29.4-point family advantage, 3/3 worlds). Leave-one-out
functional fitting (A_{f,−τ} substituted into held-out τ) is the remaining
prerequisite and doubles as a clean prospective-gate estimator. 53% capture is
probably enough — the economic question is whether the shared part saves enough
repeated private bits to pay for itself; real abstractions leave ε_τ behind.

## 23. PROMOTE redefined; the hierarchy of "shared structure" notions

PROMOTE is no longer "factor repeated residuals." It is **allocate shared
representational capacity to recurring innovation** — the structural event is
private computational capacity → shared computational capacity, which generalizes
to creating operators, low-rank directions, parameterized families, macros, or
memory entries. This also yields a two-level structural-learning theory: cheap
inner adaptation reorganizes existing capacity when it can express the recurrence;
expensive outer restructuring (PROMOTE) grows the language only when existing
capacity cannot absorb recurrence economically — so PROMOTE should fire when
(recurrence exists) + (existing capacity cannot absorb it economically). The
project has climbed a ladder of increasingly strong notions of "shared structure"
because every weaker criterion failed in a different way:

parameter similarity < statistical clustering < recoverable latent identity <
functional similarity < cross-task substitutability < prospective learning value.

That ladder is itself a methodological contribution. The learned abstraction will
be a reusable object in the **learner's own computational language** (not a
recovered teacher module), which is exactly the LLM-relevant problem.

# V3: abstraction birth demonstrated on development worlds

## 24. The abstraction-birth phenomenon is now demonstrated

V3 has demonstrated, on development worlds, the phenomenon it was designed to
find: repeated private computation was converted into newly created shared
computation (K=6→7), and the resulting abstraction subsequently made new
learning cheaper. The conjunction is qualitatively different from anything in
V1/V2:

* **Structural migration (M2a):** D_task ↓, D_shared ↑, D_total ↓ — roughly a
  70% task-bit reduction and ~57,000-nat two-part-code gain.
* **Economic dominance (M2b):** J_promoted < J_unpromoted, and prediction also
  improves (+982 lifetime nats) — "smaller AND better," not the usual
  compression fidelity tradeoff.
* **Prospective learning (M3):** on genuinely unseen related tasks,
  L_adaptation ↓, with 8/8 future tasks independently selecting reuse — so the
  new abstractions are part of the learner's hypothesis space for subsequent
  learning, not merely post-hoc codecs.

This is the first result in the project that is a miniature of the full
mechanism rather than merely evidence consistent with it: experience → repeated
local solutions → functional recurrence detection → new persistent shared
computation → references from tasks → cheaper future learning. The
information flow is now correct. Treat worlds 3–9 as an internal holdout (no
more mechanism changes unless something is broken) and freeze ambitious
preregistered intervals, not merely "positive."

## 25. Abstraction may pay twice: compression and denoising

The surprising simultaneous D↓ *and* L↓ (compression usually costs fidelity)
has a leading hypothesis: promotion acts as a **regularizer** — many noisy
private residuals are replaced by a better-estimated shared family function, so
pooling across tasks denoises. This is testable by comparing the promoted A
against the median private residual on a large oracle evaluation set; if A is
closer to the teacher family function, promotion is denoising repeated
estimates. If confirmed, abstraction pays twice: once through amortized
description length, once through improved estimation from pooled evidence.

## 26. Refusal is the weakest link — but abstraction formation may be speculative

Structured worlds promote 5.0 / refuse 4.3; structureless 3.3 / 6.3 —
discrimination, not a clean criterion. Do not tune refusal away (the danger of
polishing until the control reaches zero, after four world redesigns). Instead
measure what false abstractions are: reuse count, future-nat savings, lifetime
net value. The cleaner usage separation (31.3/40 structured vs 15.0/40
structureless) hints that creation can be noisy while valuable abstractions
survive repeated reuse — i.e., abstraction formation should be **speculative,
with retention decided later** (a CREATE/DELETE lifecycle). The "5 abstractions
for 2 families" overproduction may organically motivate MERGE once pairwise
functional equivalence among promoted abstractions is diagnosed — do not add
MERGE before confirmation, just diagnose it.

## 27. The development result is 10/10 and the control separation has flipped

Across all ten development worlds, every sign holds 10/10: ΔL = +1,350 nats,
ΔJ_two-part = +55,697 nats, D_total ↓ 63.6%. Crucially the **decision balance
flips** — structured worlds promote more than they refuse, while structureless
controls refuse more than they promote — which is a much better behavioral
signature than demanding zero false promotions. A rational promoter does not need
perfect specificity on every local decision; it needs to allocate substantially
more persistent capacity when recurrence is real. Reuse-rate separates more
strongly than library-size (2.48× vs 1.83×), and reuse is closer to economic
relevance, so prefer **reuse-rate discrimination** over library-size (which is
contaminated by fragmentation: ~5.3 abstractions for two latent families). Keep
the original absolute-refusal requirement permanently recorded as **falsified on
development**, and register the graded discrimination result separately — do not
let a sealed pass of the graded criterion retroactively become "H11 including
refusal passed."

## 28. The load-bearing pre-seal audit: representation advantage ≠ extra training

The one audit considered load-bearing before sealing is a matched-compute /
sham-reuse control: give the unpromoted learner the same adaptation checkpoints
and optimization-step budget but no access to promoted abstractions. If the
+1,350-nat prediction gain survives, it is a representation advantage; if not,
it is an accidental extra-training advantage. Search FLOPs need not be equal for
the primary J (μ=0), but you must know whether the prediction improvement comes
from better representation versus extra SGD steps. The model-selection benefit
from having a library is itself legitimate; extra SGD for free is the thing to
exclude.

## 29. The defensible definition of a reusable abstraction, forced by four failed testbeds

The failed worlds forced a much richer criterion than "find something similar."
PROMOTE should mean: find private computation that is **load-bearing + cross-
task substitutable + more specific than a generic shared approximation +
prospectively useful on data it was not fitted to**, then allocate shared
capacity to it. That four-part definition is a more serious definition of a
reusable abstraction than where V3 began, and is itself a durable conceptual
output of the research program. The expected sealed-replication pattern: D_total
and two-part gain replicate strongly, lifetime prediction noisier, M3 (future
learning) largest variance, refusal positive but drifting, library size may
wander because fragmentation is not yet controlled.

# V4 roadmap: the lifecycle of abstractions (contingent on V3 sealing)

## 30. Abstraction creation is speculative; survival is decided by reuse

V3's ~5 abstractions for 2 real families (and some false births in the
structureless control) is not a bug to tune away — it implies the right model is
speculative birth + evidence-based survival. The objective is not P(false
birth)=0 but P(false abstraction survives)≈0, which is far more realistic and
directly motivates a CREATE/DELETE lifecycle (V4). The revised V4 progression
starts with RETAIN/DELETE (V4.1), not MERGE — give every abstraction a running
account V_t(A) = [S_t(realized) + Ŝ_future] / [D(retain) + C(retrieval)] and
DELETE when V_t<0; compare PROMOTE+DELETE vs frozen PROMOTE-only. The desired
result table: structured 5.4 births → 2.3 survive → 2.1 useful; control 3.0 →
0.4 → 0.1. Abstraction-survival becomes a new instrument: N_reuse, cumulative
savings S(A), survival time T_life — false births → low reuse → short life vs
real → high reuse → retained (selection over neural abstractions). The guiding
principle: **invent hypotheses cheaply, then make persistence expensive.**

## 31. The grand architecture emerges from measured failures, not design

A durable methodological principle is now clear across V1–V4: each structural
operation is forced by a measured failure of the simpler system, not added
arbitrarily. Repeated private bits ⇒ PROMOTE; fragmented births ⇒ MERGE;
false/stale births ⇒ DELETE; shared-state interference ⇒ FORK. The
continual-computational-memory lifecycle (reuse → innovate → PROMOTE →
MERGE/FORK/DELETE) is earned rung by rung. The cleanest scientific progression
is V3 birth → V4 life/death/reorganization → V5 abstractions over abstractions
(macros, loops, recursion) — premature recursion would amplify fragmentation
and search debt, so macros are deferred to V5. The constitutional rule carried
everywhere: **structural edits are justified by substitution, never identity**
(MERGE asks "can one replacement serve all dependents of both?" — fit C* over
all dependents, compare A+B vs C vs global vs delete).

## 32. Hysteresis is the sharpest theoretical prediction — but it has a lag confound

If C_create > C_retain (creating an abstraction costs more than keeping one),
then r_create > r_delete, producing a hysteresis loop — strong evidence that
abstractions are investments. But there is a nasty confound: any slowly-adapting
dynamical system shows apparent hysteresis simply because it **lags behind** a
changing environment. The decisive test is to sweep the recurrence-change rate
v and check whether r_create − r_delete → 0 as v → 0 (fake, adaptation lag) or
stays > 0 (genuine economic hysteresis). **Preregister this.**

## 33. Abstractions are real options; frequency is not value

DELETE should not ask "has A been used recently?" but "what is the expected
value of keeping the option to reuse A?" — V_retain(A) = P(return) ×
value-if-returned / D_retain. This gives the A→B→A (temporary dormancy) vs
A→B→B… (permanent disappearance) experiment: a good lifecycle learner should
retain A through short expected dormancy but eventually delete it if the regime
is gone. Long-term memory itself becomes an economic decision. Separately,
frequency ≠ value: an abstraction used twice may save 10,000 nats/use while one
used 100 times saves 5 — retain on N_reuse × S_reuse, not N. The library
manages economic value, not popularity.

## 34. Lifecycle management resembles a garbage collector — and may force stable handles

The architecture maps onto systems concepts: PROMOTE = allocation, references =
pointers, realized reuse = reachability, DELETE = garbage collection, MERGE =
deduplication, FORK = copy-on-write. These solve the same underlying economics
(store reusable structure while controlling space + mutation + aliasing).
Generational GC (new/speculative abstractions monitored aggressively, long-lived
ones require stronger evidence to delete) naturally generates hysteresis.
Lifecycle may also force emergence of a **stable addressing / symbol table** —
indirection (task → abstraction ID → current implementation) so MERGE/DELETE can
reorganize without rewriting every reference. Every edit pays a **migration
cost** (reference rewrites, validation, fitting) that must be logged or lifecycle
operations look artificially attractive. Chained edits risk **cascading
distortion** — validate every dependent task's final behavior against stable
checkpoints (a semantic regression suite / unit tests for learned abstractions).
Track **abstraction lineage** metadata from day one (birth_time, parents,
dependents, reuse_count, savings, merge_history) — once MERGE/FORK/DELETE fire
it will be impossible to reconstruct why the library reached a state otherwise.
V4's (library state, candidate edit, ΔJ, outcome) decision dataset quietly
collects the training set for a later learned restructuring policy (V6).

WHAT THE FOUR FAILED TESTBEDS TAUGHT, which may matter longer than the result

Getting a world where "promotion" means what it says took four attempts, and
each failure killed a different plausible-sounding criterion:

1. Overcomplete basis. Recurring structure never reached the residuals at
   all; the learner spent two spare slots on the two hidden families and
   addressed them through routes. Verified causally (swapping a task's route
   for the wrong family's mean costs +0.00344 more NMSE than the right one;
   single-slot ablation is family-differential up to 140x). Gradient descent
   performs promotion-like absorption on its own WHEN FREE CAPACITY EXISTS —
   but note the correction: those slots were preallocated and already paid
   for, so this is not evidence of MDL-driven promotion, only that unused
   capacity gets used.
2. Saturated library, perturbation families. Structure moved into residuals
   and families became recoverable (0.861), but substituting family means
   barely beat zeroing every residual (0.02618 vs 0.02661). Promotion was
   deletion in a costume, and the promoter could not refuse.
3. New-primitive families, varying position. Family recovery went to 1.000
   and residuals became load-bearing, yet a family's mean parameters
   captured only 9% of its members' behavioral value, because averaging
   across step placements mixes incompatible functions.
4. Gauge freedom. Even at a fixed position, PARAMETER means captured 11.9%
   where a FUNCTIONAL fit captured 53.4%. Two tasks computing the same rank-2
   innovation under different rotations of (U, V) have a mean that is not
   their function. This was predicted in review 17 and landed exactly there.

THE LADDER. Notions of "shared structure" are ordered, and every rung below
the top certified a world where promotion was really something else:

    parameter similarity < clustering < recoverable latent identity
      < functional similarity < CROSS-TASK SUBSTITUTABILITY
      < prospective value

The gate that works is a four-way LEAVE-ONE-OUT comparison — private
residual, family abstraction, global abstraction, zero — with the
abstraction fitted without the task it is substituted into. It asks three
questions at once: is the private computation load-bearing, is it
compressible into one shared function, and is that function family-specific
rather than generic? On the frozen testbed: 51.3% family capture against
22.0% global, +29.3 points, 3/3, with an on-trajectory probe agreeing with a
common-domain probe. Any future promotion claim should carry this table.

TWO OTHER THINGS WORTH KEEPING

Promotion has two halves and the second is easy to forget. Before library
REUSE was implemented, promoted and unpromoted learners had IDENTICAL
prequential loss: bits migrated and nothing learned faster. Compression
without a reuse path is storage optimization. H11.3 exists to catch exactly
that, and it caught it.

Refusal is graded, not absolute, and the absolute form is FALSIFIED. The
promoter does not decline to promote in structureless worlds; it builds a
smaller library there (3.0 against 6.2 abstractions, 1.80x fewer tasks
reusing). At lambda = ln 2 a private rank-2 residual costs ~1,098 nats and
buys ~215, so any behavior-preserving grouping pays and a retrospective-only
criterion is a vector quantizer. Only the prospective term — must this
candidate beat a single global abstraction on members it was not fitted on —
produces discrimination at all.

WHAT V3 DOES NOT SHOW. The world is constructed: saturated six-slot library,
a genuinely new hidden primitive at task 16 at a fixed position, two hidden
families. The claim is that promotion works when structure is load-bearing,
compressible, and family-specific — not that those conditions arise unaided.
The learned abstraction is not the teacher's primitive; it is the best shared
explanation of how this learner compensated for a computation it could not
express, which is the more realistic object and the one an LLM would present.
And promotion is compute-expensive: that cost is logged, never charged
(mu = 0), leaving the search-cost economics to V4.

================================================================================
V4.1 GATE: RETIRE FAILS — THE ABSTRACTIONS ARE DISTINCT, NOT REDUNDANT
================================================================================

The V4.1 compaction/RETIRE gate ran on worlds 0–2 and failed. The
interpretation changed in a useful way: V3's extra abstractions are not
duplicates. They are distinct learned implementations that each carry real
behavioral contribution, even though the teacher world has only two hidden
families.

The normalized substitution result is decisive: substitution costs 0.86–1.60×
the abstraction's own contribution, 99–100% of ordered pairs fail even a 10%
contribution-relative tolerance, and exact behavioral-cover compaction has
negative net value in all three worlds (compacting to one abstraction is net
−1,180, −2,393, and −1,377 nats on worlds 0–2). RETIRE cannot improve this
library by simply redirecting references. The earlier "V3 fragmented two
concepts into 4–6 redundant copies" story is unsupported.

This required a correction to the tolerance metric: the original V4.1
substitution test divided by total output variance, but an abstraction
contributes only ~0.2% of it, so every abstraction substituted for every other
and the oracle "compacted" 4–6 abstractions to 1. Two symptoms identify this
class of error immediately: the causal control goes DEGENERATE (random, usage,
and functional retirement score identically), and the NULL EDIT passes
(deleting an abstraction outright scores a smaller deviation than replacing
it). Both are now hard guards in audit_lifecycle_oracle.py. A functional
tolerance must be normalized against the quantity whose loss it licenses,
never against total output scale.

The failure distinguishes three operations that were conflated:
(1) RETIRE/DELETE — genuine obsolescence (A→∅); requires a nonstationary world;
(2) REDUNDANCY ELIMINATION — A→B, redirect and discard; V3's library has
almost none of this; (3) SYNTHETIC MERGE — A+B→C+ε_A+ε_B, neither replaces
the other but a fitted C captures their shared component. This is now the
interesting problem and the V4.2 question.

The gate also sharpens several V4 design points: the right primitive is
REHOME+RETIRE (dependents(A)→B, A→∅ — the computation isn't forgotten, an
unnecessary implementation is eliminated); approximate substitutability is
neither transitive nor symmetric, so the library is a substitution graph, not
equivalence classes; V4.1 is a covering problem (find the cheapest surviving
subset that behaviorally covers all dependents); H14b's survival-fraction
prediction may be the wrong endpoint (2/6 vs 1/3 gives identical .33 survival
rate while behavior differs) — prefer reuse density and value density; and
"birth is locally rational but globally redundant" is the precise phenomenon
(each PROMOTE decision makes sense locally, yet after several births the
library collectively contains redundancy that only sleep can detect jointly).

The revised V4 ordering is now empirically justified: V4.1 attempted RETIRE
(gate failure, recorded), V4.2 SYNTHETIC FACTORIZATION (can several distinct
abstractions be replaced by a shared parent + small differences?), V4.3
COPY-ON-WRITE/FORK, V4.4 true RETIRE + hysteresis with nonstationarity. The
stationary V3 world contains no genuine obsolescence by construction.

The most exciting direction from the failure: V4.2 may be the first real test
of abstraction over abstractions. If the 4–6 distinct abstractions are
different instantiations of a common family A_i(z) ≈ A(z; α_i), the right
operation is not MERGE but anti-unification — {A_i} → A(·; α_i), multiple
discrete abstractions → parameterized abstraction. A cheap functional-PCA
diagnostic on pairwise differences A_i(z)−A_j(z) tests whether low-rank
structure exists before building anything. This may synthesize V2 and V3:
partial recurrence favored continuous manifolds, exact recurrence favored
discrete atoms, and V4 may discover several atoms → shared parameterized
manifold, making representation learning capable of changing granularity and
type (private residual → atom → parameterized family).

H14 stands as written and awaits a nonstationary testbed. The retraction
strengthens the project: the scientific discipline (correct normalization killed
a +2,928-nat artifact) makes eventual positive results believable, and the
surviving conclusion is richer than the one it replaced.


================================================================================
V4.2 DIAGNOSTIC: LOW-RANK STRUCTURE EXISTS — BUT THE GATES ARE NOT YET PASSED
================================================================================

The functional-PCA diagnostic on pairwise differences A_i(z)−A_j(z) ran and
found encouraging low-rank structure: R² at rank 2 reaches 67–83% of total
abstraction variation across the three development worlds. The operation is
renamed from MERGE to **FACTORIZE / PARAMETERIZE** — the model is
A_i(z) ≈ C(z) + Σ α_ij B_j(z) + E_i(z) (shared center, small reusable
functional basis, cheap per-abstraction arguments, small remaining innovation),
which is richer than A_1,A_2→C and makes the V2 connection explicit.

But three caveats apply. First, the isotropic null was essential: with only m
abstractions, the empirical variation has rank at most m−1, so "rank 4 explains
everything" is arithmetic. The meaningful result is the excess over a matched
null: +15.7, +26.2, +27.3 points at rank 2. This says the abstraction variation
is substantially more low-dimensional than generic variation with the same
finite-sample geometry, but it does not yet say D_factorized < D_atoms at
matched behavior. Proxy bits are only a proposal-stage diagnostic.

Second, the economic gate (Gate 2) has not been passed. It requires:
D_after = D(C) + Σ D(B_j) + Σ [D(α_i) + D(E_i) + D(references)] + D_select,
scored as J = L + λD against D_before = Σ D(A_i), with real held-out Gaussian
loss. A nasty loophole: if E_i has the same expressive capacity as the
original A_i, the factorization is structurally meaningless (C+Bα_i becomes
decorative while E_i ≈ A_i). Measure D(E_i)/D(A_i) and require D(E_i) ≪ D(A_i)
with substantial shared behavioral capture — this is the V4.2 analogue of V3's
global/family/zero control. Also add an **independent compression baseline**
(each abstraction gets its own rank-2 approximation and is charged
independently); if the shared C+Bα model wins, the gain is cross-abstraction
reuse, not individual overparameterization. Existence ≠ representation ≠
discovery: behavioral oracle factorization must become compact realizable
neural factorization, which must be discoverable by the learner.

Third, the decisive test is **leave-one-abstraction-out**: fit C,B_{1:k} on
A_1..A_{m-1}, take held-out A_m, infer only its small argument α_m from
proposal probes, and compare learn-full / independent-compressed / family /
family+E / global-only. If a new abstraction → learn a few arguments instead
of a whole operator works, V4 has learned a reusable **space of computations**
— Gate 3, the prospective parameterization analogue of V3's H11.3.

The broader insight: V4's abstractions aren't garbage, they're **useful but
over-specific**. The failure mode is not "bad concept → DELETE" but "several
useful specifics → discover the general thing they instantiate." V3 showed
repeated task-local computations can become a reusable object; V4 may show
repeated reusable objects can themselves become instances of a more general
reusable schema. If the fully priced factorization and leave-one-abstraction-out
test pass, that is more interesting than the original garbage-collection story,
and it unifies V2 (partial recurrence → continuous manifolds) and V3 (strong
recurrence → discrete atoms) by showing representation type is fluid: private →
atom → parameterized family.

The V4.1 RETIRE dormancy control also failed, correctly: when the regime returns
the learner simply PROMOTE(A'), so the dormant abstraction has little option
value. This is a world failure, not a retention-policy failure. Final-step
deletion is inherently a null edit under the objective. True RETIRE requires a
world with **retrieval continuity** — the returning regime must make reusing
dormant A materially cheaper than relearning/promoting A' (reacquisition cost
C_reacquire = L_extra + λD_temporary + C_search). V4.4 must build that world.


================================================================================
V4.2 ECONOMIC GATE: FACTORIZATION FAILS — PRIVATE COMPRESSION DOMINATES
================================================================================

The V4.2 factorization economic gate (Gate 2) failed on development worlds
0–9. The functional-PCA existence gate (Gate 1) passed: low-rank structure
exists beyond the finite-m isotropic null (R² excess +15.7/+26.2/+27.3 at rank
2 on worlds 0–2). But when the factorization is fully priced with real
held-out Gaussian loss and actual retained bits, the matched-budget
private-compression baseline wins 9/10 worlds. The prospective parameterization
test (Gate 3) also failed: leave-one-abstraction-out recovered only 7.5% of
the held-out abstraction's behavioral contribution through the inferred
argument α_m. This is a real falsification, not a tuning issue.

The decisive new control is the **matched-budget private-compression
frontier**: give each abstraction its own rank-2 approximation and charge
independently. If the shared C+Bα_i model cannot beat that, the gain is not
cross-abstraction reuse but each abstraction being individually
overparameterized. At the canonical V3 scale, the cheapest representation
change is "compress each abstraction better," not "share more between
abstractions." This is a positive statement about V3's library: its abstractions
are not mutually redundant, not obsolete, and not profitably factorable once
private compression is fair.

This produces a new constitutional methodological rule:

[
\boxed{
\textbf{Every sharing claim needs a matched-budget non-sharing alternative.}
}
]

alongside V3's "structural claims require functional substitutability." A
complex structural edit only earns its place if it beats every simpler
counterfactual at the same resource budget. The edit economy is ordered by
structural ambition: KEEP < COMPRESS < SHARE/FACTORIZE < CREATE/FORK. COMPRESS(A)
— lower precision, lower rank, pruning, simpler realization of the same
function — is a missing primitive the architecture needs, and it sits below
sharing because the experiment showed private compression dominates at this
scale.

V4 is now reframed. The original premise (successful abstraction birth ⇒
library maintenance problem) is falsified for the canonical V3 regime. The
revised question: **under what conditions does the optimal representation cease
to be a static library of independently compressed abstractions and begin to
require lifecycle operations?** The V3 regime is one corner of the phase
diagram (small library + stationary recurrence + cheap reacquisition + no
retrieval pressure ⇒ KEEP + privately compress is near-optimal). Lifecycle
pressure should appear only when one or more of these grow: library scale,
environmental turnover, reacquisition cost, interference, retrieval cost.

The next step is an **opportunity census**: keep the V3 learner frozen, generate
libraries under different regimes, run offline structural oracles only, and
classify which edit (KEEP, COMPRESS, FACTORIZE, RETIRE, RETAIN, FORK) the
oracle would choose. A small phase diagram (N∈{64,128,256} lifetime, F∈{2,4,8}
families, q∈{0,low,high} turnover, g∈{8,16,32,64} return-gap) on worlds 0–2
could map the phase boundaries. There may be a **lifecycle phase transition** —
none of the lifecycle operations matter until library scale exceeds some M*,
meaning lifecycle complexity itself should be amortized (a small library
doesn't need GC, deduplication, hierarchy, or retrieval indexing). Measuring
M*_retrieval, M*_merge, M*_retention would be an excellent V4 contribution.

The failed spec is preserved rather than rewritten: V4 original lifecycle
specification — H14 redundancy gate failed; H14 obsolescence world failed to
instantiate retention value; H15 factorization existence gate passed but
matched-budget economic gate failed 9/10 and prospective acquisition recovered
only 7.5%. The successor direction: "V4: When Does a Library Need a Lifecycle?"
— studying the phase boundaries of self-restructuring, why and when a neural
computational language needs an economy rather than merely a library.


================================================================================
SCALE-EXPERIMENT CORRECTION: HOLD m CONSTANT, NOT N
================================================================================

The first F sweep was not a true library-scale sweep. With lifetime N fixed,
increasing F simultaneously decreased the evidence available to establish each
reusable family (N_family/F ↓). By F=8 the learner was asked to build a larger
vocabulary while starving every candidate abstraction of the repetitions needed
to be born. The falling library size is expected, not informative about scale.

The correct coordinates are F (breadth of latent vocabulary) and
m = N_recurring/F (support/repetitions per family), with N ≈ N_base + Fm. To
isolate scale while holding family evidence fixed, hold m constant and vary F
(e.g., (2,16)/(4,16)/(8,16)/(16,16)). To isolate evidence per abstraction, hold
F constant and vary m (e.g., (8,8)/(8,16)/(8,32)). Scale lifetimes as N ∝ F to
keep m constant — do not increase scale by sacrificing support per concept.

The quantity to track is emergent library size M(F,m) = number of promoted
abstractions. Holding m constant tests whether M(F,m₀) ≈ cF over some range.
Only once M actually grows can lifecycle machinery be asked to pay. F isn't
the resource pressure; the realized live library size M is.

This reveals two nested amortization thresholds: abstraction-birth
(m > m*_promote, V3's level) and higher-order factorization
(M > M*_factorize). Examples amortize an abstraction; abstractions amortize a
schema; eventually schemas amortize macros — the recursive economic structure
hoped for. The revised V4 centerpiece is **The Economics of Abstraction
Granularity**: as a learned library grows, when does it become cheaper to
represent reusable computations independently, compress them locally, or factor
them into a higher-order parameterized family?

The current result establishes the first curve point: small library ⇒ COMPRESS
(local precision reduction dominates cross-object restructuring). The
interesting question is whether increasing M produces a J_COMPRESS(M) vs
J_FACTORIZE(M) crossing: COMPRESS gets per-object savings, FACTORIZE pays a
fixed shared cost but amortizes it over more related abstractions, so
M < M* ⇒ COMPRESS wins and M > M* ⇒ FACTORIZE wins. Finding that M* would be an
excellent V4 result.

Two confounds to control. (1) Learning duration: longer lifetimes change both
scale and training history, so compare broad (F=16, m=16) vs narrow (F=2,
m=128) at the same total recurring-task count — if factorization wins only in
the broad case, the pressure is genuinely number of reusable objects, not
longer training. (2) Family relatedness: independently sampled primitives
won't share factorable structure, so for clean factorization tests introduce a
controlled meta-family recurrence parameter r_meta (A_f = C + Bα_f + ε_f). The
higher-order phase diagram becomes (M, r_meta) → COMPRESS vs FACTORIZE — the
precise analogue of V1's recurrence experiment one abstraction level higher
(V1: r_task → private vs shared operator; V4: r_meta → independent atoms vs
parameterized operator family; same economics, recursively applied).


================================================================================
ECONOMIC SURVIVAL FILTER: RETAIN(A) ⟺ V(A) > λD(A)
================================================================================

The first genuinely compelling V4 lifecycle mechanism is the economic survival
filter. The conceptual correction: the filter distinguishes **economically
worthwhile abstractions from uneconomical ones**, not "real" from "false." The
η=0 control already contains the counterexample — one abstraction worth 2,302
nats against a ~1,098-nat storage cost. If an abstraction saves more predictive
loss than it costs to encode, it deserves to survive regardless of teacher
ontology. The rule is RETAIN(A) ⟺ V(A) > λD(A), where V(A) is the marginal
behavioral value of having A.

New constitutional rule: **structural value is always marginal value against the
cheapest legitimate alternative.** V(A) must be measured against the best
admissible without-A counterfactual (substitutable abstraction, remaining private
innovation, normal task adaptation, architecture fallback), not raw ablation.
This project has been burned repeatedly by wrong counterfactuals; the correct
V(A) = J(best available representation without A) − J(current representation
with A), excluding A's own storage charge from the first behavioral term.

Do NOT modify PROMOTE. Birth (does it capture recurring behavior and
generalize?) and persistence (is the reuse worth its storage?) are separate
tests. The pipeline is PROMOTE → probationary abstraction → observe actual
reuse → economic survival test (RETAIN if value > code cost, RETIRE otherwise).
This preserves V3 unchanged and gives V4 the causal comparison PROMOTE-only vs
PROMOTE + economic survival.

The online problem is the whole experiment. The post-hoc oracle knows V_T(A)
with complete dependent-set knowledge; the learner at sleep t has only V̂_t(A).
The V4R question: can realized early usefulness forecast whether an abstraction
will ultimately repay its code cost? Before any online deletion, run a
historical forecasting audit: reconstruct V̂_t(A) at each sleep t∈{24,32,48,64}
from existing trajectories, compare to V_T(A) − λD(A), and measure sign
accuracy, ranking correlation, false-retirement rate, false-retention rate, and
economic regret R_t = J(decisions from V̂_t) − J(clairvoyant decisions from
V_T). This is runnable from saved ledgers/checkpoints before another expensive
lifetime sweep.

Scrutinize realized_savings_bits. The per-reuse savings ŝ_{τ,A} = cost
without A − cost with A must use the matched alternative, not "error after
zeroing A." The online estimate is V̂_t(A) = Σ ŝ_{τ,A} + V̂_future(A).

The threshold must remain parameter-free: T_A = λD(A) per abstraction (≈1,098
nats today), not a tuned constant. When abstraction size changes, the criterion
scales automatically. The early-evidence problem needs PROBATION, not a fitted
threshold: at birth V̂(A) ≈ 0 so immediate filtering kills everything;
distinguish "not yet validated" from "validated and uneconomic" — enter
PROBATION at birth, become eligible after the existing grace/evidence
condition, then survive iff V̂_t(A) ≥ λD(A). Avoid confidence-bound machinery
until a simple fixed grace rule fails.

The falsifier: the online rule could make the post-hoc library look cleaner
while hurting lifetime performance. The main criterion is J_survival <
J_PROMOTE-only, not "library size decreases." Score ΔD, ΔL_lifetime, ΔJ, future
adaptation, and old-task behavior. Distinguish false retirement (eventually
V_T > λD but deleted) from false retention (eventually V_T < λD but survived) —
false retirement is probably more costly because it can cause reacquisition,
and that asymmetry may produce the real-options/hysteresis story organically.

This gives RETAIN a non-tautological meaning: an abstraction survives because
its demonstrated marginal contribution exceeds its description cost. Dormancy
becomes V̂_t(A) ↓ and returning regimes cause V̂_t(A) ↑ or reacquisition cost
if deleted, so real-options retention extends an already-working economic
survival rule rather than being invented for the dormancy world.

The acceptance ladder is now: structure exists → structure transfers →
structure has marginal value → marginal value exceeds representation cost.
V3 discovered the epistemic criteria (functional recurrence → substitutability
→ prospective transfer); V4 adds the economic criterion (useful abstraction
⇒ worth storing). A function can be recurrent, substitutable, prospectively
generalizable, and still have economic value < code cost — exactly what was
measured.

Next steps: do NOT touch factorization or scale experiments yet. (1) Freeze
V(A) > λD(A). (2) Verify V(A) uses the strongest without-A counterfactual.
(3) Retrospectively measure V̂_t(A) at each sleep using only history. (4)
Measure online-estimator regret against the final-value oracle. (5) If early
estimate carries signal, implement PROBATION → RETAIN/RETIRE with threshold
unchanged. (6) Compare against frozen PROMOTE-only on development worlds. (7)
Only then revisit the dormancy crossover. Do NOT require the control survivor
rate to go to zero (the 2,302-nat control abstraction is why): the desired
result is that abstractions survive iff their measured usefulness repays their
own description cost, regardless of where they came from.


================================================================================
V4 SYNTHESIS: THE ECONOMY OVER REPRESENTATIONAL TRANSFORMATIONS
================================================================================

V4 taught not "how to delete abstractions" but what kind of economy a
self-organizing neural library has to obey. The real picture: first compress
what you already have; only restructure when a more structural representation
beats the best simpler alternative. Once the library keeps changing, an
abstraction's value stops being independent of the rest of the system.

ELEVEN FINDINGS:

1. V3's library was better than expected. The 4–6 abstractions for two hidden
   families were mostly behaviorally distinct (A_i ≉ A_j) once substitutability
   was measured contribution-relative, not total-variance-relative. V3
   discovered distinct useful computations, not sloppy duplicates.

2. Apparent higher-order structure ≠ economically reusable higher-order
   structure. The abstractions lie on low-dimensional functional geometry
   (rank-2 family beats isotropic null), but matched-budget private COMPRESS
   beats shared FACTORIZE in essentially every cell. Geometric regularity ≠
   economically reusable computation; representations lying near a manifold does
   not mean the right thing is to name the manifold.

3. The main slack was numerical, not structural. Abstractions stored at ~8
   bits/scalar had a behavioral coding frontier of ~1–2 bits/scalar with no
   penalty around 6 bits. COMPRESS (lower precision, lower rank, pruning) was a
   huge cheap opportunity that won across the reachable census. Architectural
   principle: local coding efficiency should be exhausted before global
   structural complexity is introduced.

4. The correct counterfactual rule (strongest methodological discovery). A
   structural operation earns credit only relative to the cheapest behaviorally
   legitimate alternative the learner would actually take: FACTORIZE vs best
   private compression (not current representation), RETAIN vs
   delete-and-relearn/re-promote (not delete-and-nothing). Four or five
   corrections all had the same cause: wrong counterfactual.

5. The main positive V4 result: a clean unfitted retention law RETAIN(A) ⟺
   H_R·s̄ > λD(A), with H_R* = λD(A)/s̄. Measured s̄ ≈ 64.1 nats/use and
   λD(A) ≈ 1098 nats predicted H_R* ≈ 17.1 returning tasks; empirical crossing
   was 17.9 (within ~5%). Not fitted.

6. Retention and promotion are the same economic law in opposite temporal
   directions: birth asks N_observed·s > D(A), retention asks N_future·s >
   D(A). Not fundamentally separate mechanisms — the same amortization decision
   applied to different horizons. This simplifies the grand theory.

7. Dormancy itself was not the important variable. Across gaps 4–16,
   per-return-task savings were ~constant (~64 nats/task). What changed was the
   number of future opportunities remaining. Correct variable is H_R (expected
   remaining reuse), not g (time since last use). Recency is merely evidence
   about P(future reuse), not the value function itself.

8. Retention becomes path-dependent in a living library. With frozen later
   promotion the law holds, but when the library reopens, deleting A can cause
   A' to be promoted (saving 0 bits) or change library count differently. V(A)
   is not a property of A alone but V(A | L_t, H_t, π), and the proper decision
   becomes Q(L_t, e_t) = E[J_future | L_t, e_t].

9. A learned library is a dynamical system, not a bag of modules. Today's
   deletion causes tomorrow's promotion; today's fork prevents tomorrow's
   residual; today's factorization changes which future abstraction looks
   worthwhile. Library operations interact through the future learning
   trajectory. Per-object garbage collection is eventually insufficient; the
   true problem is sequential structural decision-making.

10. Lifecycle machinery was not worthwhile at current scale. The V4R
    opportunity census was overwhelmingly negative (COMPRESS wins, FACTORIZE
    loses, RETIRE has no useful canonical opportunity, FORK has no oracle
    opportunity, RETAIN works only when future opportunity is instantiated
    carefully). Even tripling lifetime and growing the library toward ~16
    abstractions did not produce a factorization crossing. Small stationary
    neural libraries may simply not need sophisticated lifecycle machinery;
    architecture complexity itself needs to be amortized.

11. The missing pressures that should eventually create a lifecycle economy:
    scale (large enough M), meta-recurrence (enough related abstractions that
    shared schema beats independent COMPRESS), nonstationarity, reacquisition
    cost, retrieval cost. Without these, KEEP/COMPRESS is rational.

THE RECURSIVE ECONOMIC LAW. At every level N×s > C: tasks→abstraction
(N_task·s_task > D(A)), future tasks→retention (H_R·s_reuse > D(A)),
abstractions→schema (N_atoms·s_schema > D(S)), programs→macro
(N_sequences·s_macro > D(M)). Abstraction, memory, hierarchy, macros, and
continual learning may all be manifestations of one prospective compression
principle: pay a fixed representation cost when expected repeated savings
exceed it.

THE NECESSARY COMPLICATION. The simple law works when candidate decisions are
independent. In a self-modifying library, e_t changes L_{t+1}, which changes
later-edit economics. The objective becomes π* = argmin_π E[L_lifetime +
λD_lifetime + C_search + C_retrieval], where π is a policy over representation
edits. Eventually π_φ(e_t | L_t, H_t) can be learned from structural-edit
trajectories — V4 shows why such a policy is needed.

THE METHODOLOGICAL CONSTITUTION (accumulated across V3/V4):
- functional substitutability, not parameter identity
- matched-budget alternatives, not weak baselines
- correct behavioral counterfactual, not convenient ablation
- validate opportunity before tuning the operator
- don't confuse geometry with economic reuse
- don't interpret a mid-lifetime intervention as paired after trajectories
  diverge
- never trust a validation guard that can pass on zero observations

PROGRAM SUMMARY. V1 (why share?) found the economic relationship between
recurrence and reuse. V2 (where should sharing occur?) could discover where
recurrent computation belongs but couldn't encode it compactly. V3 (can a new
abstraction be born?) showed repeated private computation becomes shared,
reducing retained representation and making later tasks cheaper. V4 (when
should that library restructure?) — not automatically; most restructuring
disappears against the correct simpler alternative; compress locally first,
retain according to expected amortization, recognize structural decisions
become path-dependent when the library keeps learning.

THE REVISED ULTIMATE GOAL. The grand picture transforms from "build a library
and add lifecycle operations" to "learn an economy over representational
transformations." The system should ask "what is the cheapest representation
available right now?" Every operation (KEEP, COMPRESS, PROMOTE, FACTORIZE,
FORK, DELETE, MACRO) must earn its existence economically. The final system is
not a neural library learner but a self-refactoring neural computational
system that continually asks where information should live, what form it
should take, whether it is worth naming, how precisely it should be encoded,
and whether keeping that representation will make future learning cheaper. V4
didn't give MERGE or DELETE — it gave the rules those operations will
eventually have to obey.


================================================================================
V5 PROPOSAL: LEARNING THE REPRESENTATION ECONOMY (NOT YET EXECUTED)
================================================================================

V5 should stop asking "does operation X work?" and ask: can the system choose
the right representation transformation for the economics it faces? PROMOTE,
COMPRESS, RETAIN, FACTORIZE, FORK, DELETE are candidate actions in a
representation economy. Charter: given a stream of experience and an evolving
computational library, can a learner determine what form information should
take (private, compressed, shared, parameterized, retained, or replaced) so as
to minimize prospective lifetime cost?

V5.0 — CODING-FRONTIER AUDIT (prerequisite). V4 found 8-bit storage is 4–8×
above the behavioral minimum. Every future structural comparison must use
D*(R) = min over behavior-preserving encodings of D(R), not arbitrary
D_8bit(R). New constitutional rule: compare representation classes after each
has been locally compressed to its own rate-distortion frontier.

V5.1 — AMORTIZATION INVARIANCE (H19). The V4 retention result H* = λD(A)/s̄
predicted 17.1, observed 17.9. Now deliberately change D(A) (store at
2,3,4,6,8 bits/scalar) and s̄ independently, predict H*(D) = λD/s̄ before
running, and check H* ∝ D and H* ∝ 1/s. H19: N*·s ≈ C across independently
manipulated code cost, reuse value, and horizon, with no fitted threshold.
This is the cheapest and potentially most theoretically valuable experiment.

V5.2 — HIERARCHICAL RECURRENCE BENCHMARK. Create a continuous meta-recurrence
knob A_f(z) = C(z) + r_meta·B(z;α_f) + √(1−r_meta²)·ε_f(z) controlling how
much abstractions belong to a common operator family. Simultaneously control
library scale M while holding task support per abstraction fixed. Map (M,
r_meta) → best representation (COMPRESS vs FACTORIZE). Predict M small ⇒
COMPRESS even at decent meta-recurrence, but M↑ and r_meta↑ eventually produce
FACTORIZE. The experiment discovers M*(r_meta) — V1 one abstraction level
higher (V1: task recurrence → sharing threshold; V5: abstraction recurrence →
schema threshold). H20: at sufficiently high abstraction-level recurrence and
support, FACTORIZE overtakes matched-budget COMPRESS.

V5.3 — PROSPECTIVE SCHEMA REUSE (H21). Learn a family A(z;α), then present a
new member not used to fit it. Compare full acquisition (learn entirely new
A_new) vs family acquisition (keep A(·;α) fixed, learn only α_new). The
higher-order abstraction claim: a new family member requires few arguments
rather than a whole new operator. Measure prequential cost, samples to
criterion, retained bits, held-out behavior, always against matched-budget
independently compressed operator. If the family wins, we've moved from
reusable computations to reusable spaces of computations.

V5.4 — ECONOMIC EDIT SELECTION (H22). At every consolidation point generate
candidate edits {KEEP, COMPRESS, PROMOTE, FACTORIZE, RETAIN, RETIRE} (later
add FORK), estimate ΔJ(e) for each, choose e* = argmax_e ΔJ(e). Initially
not learned — use hand-designed estimators based on V1–V4. The experiment
asks whether one common economic objective can correctly choose different
edits across regimes (low recurrence→KEEP, repeated innovation→PROMOTE,
bloated atoms→COMPRESS, related atoms→FACTORIZE, future return→RETAIN,
obsolete→RETIRE).

V5.5 — STRUCTURAL REGRET (H23). V4's path-dependence means myopic edit value
is insufficient. Define π* = argmin_π J(whole future structural trajectory),
then compare myopic (argmin_e J_{t+1}), short-horizon rollout Q_h(L_t,e),
and clairvoyant oracle. Predict myopic ≈ oracle in stationary worlds but
rollout < myopic structural regret under library evolution/nonstationarity,
establishing that representation management requires planning.

V5.6 — LEARNED REFACTORING POLICY (H24). The logged (L_t, H_t, e, ΔJ, outcome)
tuples are training data. Train q_φ(e | L_t, H_t) or Q̂_φ(L_t, e) to predict
valuable structural edits. Meta-train across many synthetic economies and test
on unseen conditions. The claim: the system learned how to restructure itself
rather than executing hand-written rules — the first point where "train the
optimizer with the model" becomes experimentally tractable.

MACROS/LOOPS HELD FOR V6. V4 showed structural compressibility isn't
sufficient — a macro must beat COMPRESS and every simpler counterfactual. Once
V5 works, a macro is just another candidate edit (MACRO) with fixed creation
cost and repeated execution savings; the theory carries over. V6 =
compositional abstraction.

DEEPER HYPOTHESIS. A natural order of compression opportunities (KEEP →
COMPRESS → PROMOTE → FACTORIZE → MACRO) emerges not by programming but because
each successive operation has larger fixed structural cost requiring more
recurrence to amortize. Increasing recurrence/scale should cause discrete
transitions in the optimal representation — the computational language's
structure emerges from economic phase transitions.

UPGRADED THEORY. From N×s > C to Choose R = argmin_R [C(R) + E[future cost |
R]], and because edits alter future learning (R_{t+1} = U(R_t, e_t)), the
endpoint becomes π* = argmin_π E[Σ_t (L_t + λD_t + μC_t)] — learn a policy for
continually choosing the cheapest useful representation of accumulated
computation.

EXECUTION ORDER. (1) Finish V3/V4 coding-frontier audit. (2) Run H19. (3)
Build hierarchical/meta-recurrence benchmark with support per family
controlled. (4) Run oracles until COMPRESS→FACTORIZE phase boundary exists.
(5) Test prospective family-member acquisition. (6) Construct the edit market.
(7) Measure myopic vs rollout structural regret. (8) Train the edit-value/
refactoring policy. (9) If that works, V6 gets MACRO/LOOP/recursive
composition.

SIX PREREGISTERED HYPOTHESES (a ladder, not all in one version):
- H19: code-cost invariance (thresholds move as C/s predicts)
- H20: higher-order amortization (FACTORIZE overtakes COMPRESS at high r_meta
  and M)
- H21: prospective schema reuse (new family member needs arguments, not a
  whole operator)
- H22: economic edit selection (one scoring rule selects correct edit across
  regimes)
- H23: structural planning (finite-horizon rollout beats myopic under library
  evolution)
- H24: learned restructuring (trained policy reduces structural regret on
  held-out economics)


================================================================================
LONG-TERM VISION: PROCEDURAL GAME-WORLD PRETRAINING AND ARC-AGI (NOT EXECUTED)
================================================================================

If the project reaches the endpoint where perception is library computation and
arbitrary programs can be assembled from learned library elements, the natural
next step is to pretrain not on answers but on worlds. ARC-AGI-2 emphasizes
compositional reasoning from few examples; ARC-AGI-3 is explicitly interactive
(agent enters novel environments without instructions, must explore, infer
goals/rules, build a world model, and act over time). The current direction of
ARC makes this vision more plausible than it would have sounded a few years ago.

CORE ARCHITECTURE. observation → perceptual programs → workspace W → reasoning
program → W' → planning program → action. The perception/reasoning/planning
distinction does not exist architecturally — they are all programs over state.
FindObjects→GroupByColor→InferSymmetry→ApplyTransform solves an ARC-like visual
problem; FindObjects→PredictCollision→Simulate→ChooseAction solves a game. Same
computational language.

REASONING EMERGES AS PROGRAMS. If hypothesize→simulate→compare→backtrack recurs
enough (N·s > C_macro), the theory says TEST_HYPOTHESIS becomes economical.
Repeated simulate→evaluate→choose becomes PLAN. Repeated try→fail→restore→
try-alternative becomes SEARCH. This is the recursive amortization law applied
to thought itself — reasoning and planning become learned reusable programs
not because we label them "reasoning" but because they recur across enough
worlds to earn a name.

NOT ORDINARY GAMES. Training on Minecraft/chess/Atari risks accumulating
game-specific policies. Instead: a procedural universe of millions of generated
microworlds whose rules are composed from latent primitives (objects, movability,
collisions, transformations, keys/locks, gravity, symmetry, copying, containment,
teleportation, resource consumption, ordering, counting, spatial/temporal
relations, hidden state, goals, adversaries, stochasticity, combined
compositionally). Each game has two programs to infer: world program
P_world(s_t,a_t)→s_{t+1} and goal program G:s→{0,1}.

ARC-AGI-2 AS DEGENERATE CASE. Treat (x_1,y_1)...(x_n,y_n) as observations of
unknown P(x)=y, synthesize P̂ from the learned computational language (not a
hard-coded DSL), run ŷ=P̂(x_test). ARC-AGI-1/2 is program induction over grids;
ARC-AGI-3 adds the interactive/agentic dimension.

THREE LEARNING TIMESCALES. Slow: computational language across millions of
worlds (L_t→L_{t+1}). Medium: world/task program P_τ synthesized from the
library for one episode. Fast: workspace W_t during individual reasoning. This
maps onto the slow library / medium program / fast workspace architecture.

PERCEPTION AS TASK-DIRECTED LIBRARY COMPUTATION. Different tasks require
different perceptual decompositions (ConnectedComponents, FindRepeatedTiles,
FindInsideOutside, TreatBackgroundAsObject). Perception is stronger than a
fixed embedding — the system learns how to look at the problem.

PLANNING COMPILES FROM SEARCH. Planning starts as brute-force search over
P̂_world; repeated useful planning structures compress into macros (10^5 search
steps → NavigateAroundObstacle(x)). Reasoning starts as expensive search and
gradually compiles into reusable computation, falling back to search on novel
situations — wake/sleep at the level of thought.

GAMES TEACH PRIORS NOT SOLUTIONS. Reusable priors: objects persist, boundaries
matter, symmetry often matters, transformations compose, goals can be inferred,
unsuccessful hypotheses should be abandoned. The library is {SEGMENT, COUNT,
COMPARE, ROTATE, TRANSLATE, TRACK, SIMULATE, SEARCH, ...} but at evaluation
time the program is new — that's fluid intelligence.

EXPLORATION AS REASONING PROGRAM. Actions chosen for information not reward
(ChooseExperiment to reduce uncertainty over candidate programs). Repeated
active-learning strategies become library programs. Interactive worlds
exercise this; static ARC does not.

NINE-STAGE CURRICULUM BY PROGRAM STRUCTURE. (1) primitive induction, (2)
composition A∘B∘C, (3) conditional IF(cond,A,B), (4) iteration REPEAT(A,until
C), (5) hidden state, (6) partial observability, (7) information gathering,
(8) long-horizon planning, (9) new abstractions the library can't yet express.

PROGRAM-STRUCTURE TRAIN/TEST SPLITS. Train on A+B and B+C, test on A+C or
A∘B∘C or A(α_unseen). Train on depth d≤4, test d=5,6. Train on IF and REPEAT
separately, test novel nesting REPEAT(IF(...)). This tests whether the library
really became a language.

THE GOLD-STANDARD GENERALIZATION TEST. A never-seen game with novel perceptual
presentation, novel rules, no textual instructions, and a small interaction
budget, where every rule is expressible by compositions of known library
computations or close enough for a cheap new abstraction. The model
perceives→experiments→infers the program→plans→solves without changing slow
pretrained weights.

ARC AS OUT-OF-DISTRIBUTION TEST. Train on the procedural world curriculum, not
on ARC directly. Then test zero-shot/few-shot: ARC-AGI-1 (transfer?),
ARC-AGI-2 (deeper interacting rules?), ARC-AGI-3 (interactive rule/goal
inference and planning?).

KEY RISK: THE CONTROLLER BECOMES THE REAL INTELLIGENCE. A hand-built search
procedure that takes library primitives and solves everything merely moves
intelligence from the neural model into the program synthesizer. Trajectory
must be: handwritten search → learned search heuristics → learned proposal
model q_φ(P|D,L) → learned restructuring/planning policy.

THE POSITIVE FEEDBACK LOOP. Solving problems improves the language used to
solve later problems: successful programs → find recurrent computation →
COMPRESS/PROMOTE/PARAMETERIZE/MACRO → better computational language → future
programs cheaper to discover.

THE GRAND META-LEARNING OBJECTIVE. J = Σ_t cost-to-understand-and-solve G_t +
λD(L_T), not merely Σ_t L(G_t). The dream result is dC_solve/dt < 0 even though
games remain novel — the system is learning how to learn worlds. The games are
not the goal; they are the ecology that causes perception, experimentation,
abstraction, reasoning, planning, memory, and program synthesis to become
economically useful reusable computations.


================================================================================
THE CORE TRIANGLE: COMPOSITION + PROGRAM SYNTHESIS + MDL
================================================================================

The strongest formulation: intelligence is search over compositional programs,
guided by compression, using a learned computational language. Almost
everything else in the project is machinery serving these three.

COMPOSITION gives combinatorial generality. A small vocabulary generates
|possible programs| >> |library primitives| via A∘B, C(A(x),B(x)),
IF(P(x),A(x),B(x)), REPEAT(A,until P). An ARC problem need not be seen before
if its solution is a novel composition of learned computations. This is where
OOD generalization comes from.

PROGRAM SYNTHESIS turns the vocabulary into fluid intelligence. Given a novel
task: P* = argmin_P L(D|P) + λD(P) — find the smallest program built from what
you know that explains the observations. The task is novel but the language
isn't; that's the distinction between memorization and genuine compositional
reasoning.

MDL makes the whole thing improve over time. Without it the library degenerates
into A_1...A_10^9 (memorization with modules). With it, pressure discovers the
smallest reusable vocabulary, and recurring subprograms get promoted to macros
(N·s > D(M)), which may parameterize into schemas. The language becomes
progressively more powerful.

THE POSITIVE FEEDBACK ENGINE. program synthesis → successful programs →
compression → better language → easier program synthesis. Initially a task may
require search over 10^6 candidate programs; after PROMOTE, the same solution is
A∘M (one step instead of four). Recurring search strategies themselves become
reusable.

PERCEPTION, REASONING, AND PLANNING ARE ALL P_i: W→W. FindConnectedComponents
(perceptual), CompareSizes (reasoning), SimulateMove (planning) are all
transformations over workspace state, so programs mix them freely without
architectural borders — a general computational language.

REASONING ITSELF CAN BE COMPILED. GenerateHypothesis→Test→Reject→Backtrack
appears across thousands of domains; MDL notices the computation is reusable and
HypothesisSearch gets promoted. Reasoning strategies become learned program
abstractions, not built modules. Plan emerges from repeated
simulate→evaluate→branch→select. Rather than building a reasoning module,
reasoning becomes compiled experience about useful computation.

SYSTEM 1 / SYSTEM 2 EMERGES COMPUTATIONALLY. Novel problem: no compact
compiled solution, so perform expensive program search/synthesis (slow
reasoning = search). Familiar structure: the useful program is already
compressed into the library, so CALL(M) solves it cheaply (fast intuition =
compiled program). Repeated reasoning literally becomes intuition.

PRINCIPLED ABSTRACTION. An abstraction is a computation whose reuse savings
justify giving it a shorter name (N·s > C), not something humans find
conceptually meaningful. Works for perceptual objects, transformations, causal
rules, planning routines, reasoning heuristics, macros, schemas — same
mechanism.

COMPOSITION GIVES RECURSION. primitives → operators → macros → schemas →
larger programs → new abstractions. Abstraction over abstraction over
abstraction — the system resembles a self-growing programming language.

THE ULTIMATE ARCHITECTURE. L = learned computational language containing
perceptual operators, transformation operators, memory operations, world-model
operations, search procedures, reasoning strategies, planning procedures,
macros, control structures. Given a new problem: (1) perceive — construct a
useful representation using programs in L; (2) synthesize — search for
P ∈ Programs(L) that explains/solves the task; (3) execute — run P over
workspace/memory; (4) learn — keep temporary innovations if needed; (5)
consolidate — find recurring computation deserving a shorter description,
update L→L'. Repeat forever.

ARC AS CLEAN TEST. The prior is p(P|L); the objective is P* = argmin_P
[-log p(D|P) + λL_L(P)] — Bayesian/MDL program induction. The crucial measure
is not whether the exact transformation was seen during training but whether the
system can construct it from pieces. The dream result: train on a procedural
game ecology, freeze the slow library, give ARC-like tasks, observe
perceptual decomposition → candidate synthesis → testing → shortest consistent
program wins → execution, where the solved program never appeared during
training — genuine compositional generalization.

THE SYNTHESIS. The neural-network machinery supplies flexible differentiable
computations, but what gets beyond ordinary deep learning is: learn the
primitives, compose them into programs, and continually compress successful
programs into a better language. If that loop works recursively, the library
stops looking like neural modules and starts looking like a learned programming
language whose compiler, standard library, and optimizer are all being
improved by the model's own experience.


================================================================================
THE CONCEPTUAL HEART: PROSPECTIVE PARSIMONY AND THE LANGUAGE OF THOUGHT
================================================================================

Intelligence is the continual invention and testing of simpler ways of
generating experience. Not merely compressing observations after the fact — the
system actively proposes new explanatory/computational organizations, runs them
forward, asks what they predict, and keeps the ones that make the world and
future problem-solving cheaper. This is the deeper story connecting MDL,
composition, PROMOTE, program synthesis, dreaming, and reusable primitives.

PROGRAMS AS HYPOTHESES ABOUT HOW TO LOOK AT THE PROBLEM. Program synthesis is
fundamentally abductive: P* = argmin_P [L(D|P) + L(P)]. Different candidate
programs are different ways of seeing the same input, not merely different
answers. Find a compact program under which what seemed complicated becomes
simple.

ABSTRACTION AS DISCOVERING A COORDINATE SYSTEM. If A makes T_i = A + δ_i
with tiny δ_i, then L(A) + Σ L(δ_i) << Σ L(T_i). A concept is a learned
representational transformation that makes a class of future explanations
shorter. The observations didn't change; the language did. (Momentum, object
permanence, recursion, symmetry, conservation, functions, variables.)

ACTIVE PRIMITIVE HYPOTHESIS. Go beyond detecting repeated computation (PROMOTE)
to asking: what new primitive, if it existed, would make these experiences much
easier to explain? This is program-language learning — hypothesizing X(α) that
explains several programs as instances of a deeper operation, not just literal
macro extraction.

RUN THE HYPOTHESIS FORWARD. A candidate program H should be executed:
s_{t+1}^(H) = H(s_t, a_t), then prediction ↔ actual observation. Reasoning
becomes hypothesize→simulate→compare→revise. This is theorizing.

DREAMING AS A TECHNICAL CONCEPT. Running programs without immediate external
input: internal state → P → ŝ_{t+1} → P → ŝ_{t+2} → ... Used for prediction,
planning, discrimination (dream H_1 and H_2, find where they disagree, choose
the experiment that reveals which is right), compression (test candidate
abstractions against old experience), and program discovery (generate synthetic
trajectories, notice deeper regularities). Dreaming is using the learned
computational language as a generative simulator for hypothesis evaluation and
invention.

REASONING AS SEARCH THROUGH PROGRAM SPACE. Maintain candidate programs P_1...P_k
each scored S(P) = L(P) + L(D|P) + C_execution(P). Reasoning moves P→P' through
substitute primitive, add condition, compose, introduce latent object, bind
variable, generalize constant to parameter, factor subprogram, recurse, fork
hypothesis. Thought is movement through program space.

PROSPECTIVE PARSIMONY. Naïve min L(P) prefers uselessly simple theories. The
actual tradeoff is L(P) + L(D|P) and ultimately L(P) + L(prediction errors) +
C_reasoning + E[C_future learning | P]. A more complicated abstraction can be
worth creating because it makes vast amounts of future computation cheaper.
This is prospective parsimony, not merely retrospective compression.

PERCEPTION AND REASONING ARE COUPLED. (R*, P*) = argmin_{R,P} [L(R) + L(P) +
L(D|P,R)]. The model reasons about how it should perceive. The correct
decomposition (pixels vs objects vs rows vs tiles vs symmetry groups) is
whichever yields the shortest successful program. In ARC the hard part is often
realizing "those aren't twenty-seven cells, those are three objects," after
which reasoning collapses.

INSIGHT = discovering a representation in which search collapses. Before
insight L(D|L) >> 0; after inventing A, L(D|L∪{A}) << L(D|L). The problem
suddenly becomes easy. This operationalizes the "ohhh, that's what is going
on" phenomenology.

THEORY = compact executable explanation. Distinguish pattern recognition
(resemblance), abstraction (reusable object), and theory (generative: H +
s_t → ŝ_{t+1}). A theory is testable because it produces consequences, not
merely compressed memory.

ACTIVE PROGRAM INDUCTION. Two candidate programs making different predictions
under action a* let the agent select a* = argmax_a expected discrimination
(P_1, P_2), act, observe, eliminate a theory. The emergent sequence:
observe→hypothesize→simulate→design experiment→observe→revise. This is
science-like behavior, much richer than classical synthesis from static
examples.

TWO MODES: COMPILATION AND INVENTION. Compilation mode: search programs
expressible by L. Invention mode: allow L→L'. The system should be able to say
"my language itself is making this problem unnecessarily difficult" and invent
a better one.

SEARCH DIFFICULTY AS EVIDENCE FOR ABSTRACTION. If many tasks are solvable but
require extremely long searches, that is evidence the language is poorly
matched. Abstraction discovery should minimize D(L) + E[C_synthesis | L],
creating an abstraction when it makes future programs easier to find, not merely
when it saves program bits. Two languages can encode the same functions while
having radically different search complexity.

GOOD PRIMITIVES RESHAPE THE SEARCH LANDSCAPE. Under L_1 a program is
A∘B∘C∘D∘E∘F; under L_2 it's M∘F. The second isn't merely shorter — its
solution is vastly easier to discover. The objective needs representation cost
+ execution cost + synthesis/search cost. This is learned algorithmic priors.

REASONING STRATEGIES AS META-PRIMITIVES. BackwardChain, TryExtremeCase,
SplitProblem, FindInvariant, SimulateAhead, SearchForCounterexample emerge
because across the task ecology they reduce future solution cost enough to be
promoted. The library begins containing not merely knowledge of the world but
ways of thinking.

INVENTING REPRESENTATIONS HUMANS DIDN'T PROVIDE. The system may discover
A: R^d→R^k that looks meaningless to us but makes a huge family of worlds have
simple dynamics z_{t+1} = F(z_t, a_t). This is "find latent variables under
which the world has simple laws." Objects might emerge because objects are
economical; other representations could emerge too.

TWO COUPLED SEARCHES. Search 1 (reasoning): P* = argmin_{P∈Programs(L)} J(P).
Search 2 (abstraction/invention): L* = argmin_L [D(L) + E_T C_solve(T|L)]. They
bootstrap each other: L→programs→experience→better L→better programs.

WAKE/SLEEP REINTERPRETED. WAKE: use L_t, search programs, test hypotheses,
simulate futures, act, accumulate reasoning trajectories. SLEEP: ask why
reasoning was expensive, what computations recurring, what variables made
prediction simple, what distinctions irrelevant, what new primitive would
shorten many programs, which existing primitives to parameterize/compress/merge/
discard; then L_t→L_{t+1}. This is periodic self-refactoring of the model's
ontology and computational language, much more than memory consolidation.

DREAMING BRIDGES WAKE AND SLEEP. Take candidate primitive A, generate A(x_1),
A(x_2),..., compose with others, ask what behaviors it implies, where it fails,
whether it's equivalent to another, whether two can be unified, whether a
candidate macro generalizes outside its suggesting trajectories, what
hypothetical worlds would distinguish competing abstractions. Some dreams test
theories against replayed data, some generate counterfactual data, some search
the abstraction space itself — offline program-space exploration using learned
world models.

THE OPERATIONAL LOOP. Observe → Propose program/interpretation → Execute forward
→ Predict/plan/test → Compare with reality → Revise program → Accumulate
reasoning trajectories → Invent/compress computational primitives → Change the
language → Reason more cheaply next time.

THE ULTIMATE OBJECTIVE. min_{L,π} D(L) + E_T [C_perceive + C_synthesize +
C_reason + C_act + L_error]. The model minimizes the total cost of making the
world intelligible and acting successfully in it, including the cost of the
language it has invented for thinking. Parsimony becomes the force that drives
ontology formation, abstraction, theory creation, compilation of reasoning, and
ultimately the evolution of the model's own language of thought.


================================================================================
HYPOTHESIS-DRIVEN PROGRAM INDUCTION: THE TEN QUESTIONS (NOT EXECUTED)
================================================================================

The missing centerpiece is hypothesis-driven program induction in a learned
language. The system should say "maybe this situation is generated by this
program, under this way of representing it; what follows if that's true?",
execute the hypothesis forward, compare with reality, modify/reject, and change
its own vocabulary if the current vocabulary makes too many problems difficult.
DreamCoder is an ancestor but works with symbolic DSLs supplied by the
researcher; we push further by learning the computational vocabulary itself and
allowing it to restructure by prospective usefulness. Ten questions:

Q1: WHAT IS A PROGRAM? Minimal substrate to give the learner (CALL, COMPOSE,
BRANCH, BIND, READ, WRITE, ITERATE, HALT — fixed computational mechanics) vs
what it discovers (OBJECT, SYMMETRY, PLAN, COLLISION, COUNT, SEARCH — learned
vocabulary). V6 experiment: compositional closure — can neural operators compose
into programs deeper than training depth? Error exploding at depth 5 breaks the
AGI story.

Q2: HYPOTHESIZING A PROGRAM. Maintain candidates H_t = {P_1...P_k} scored
S(P) = L(P|L) + L(D|P) + αC_synth(P) + βC_execute(P). Reasoning searches around
hypotheses by modifying candidates. The candidate is executable (P(s,a)=?), so a
theory is a compact executable hypothesis that predicts consequences.

Q3: DISCOVERING A DIFFERENT WAY OF LOOKING AT THINGS. The deepest problem. 40
colored cells = 40 cells / 4 objects / 2 tiles / 1 symmetry group — radically
different program complexity. Jointly infer (R*,P*) = argmin_{R,P} [L(R) +
L(P|R) + L(D|P,R)]. Perception is part of program synthesis. Experiment:
construct worlds where multiple valid decompositions exist but only one yields
a short predictive program; test whether MDL pressure causes the system to
invent the useful perceptual operator.

Q4: INVENTION VS EXTRACTION. PROMOTE notices recurring computation; scientific
abstraction invents A(α) explaining P_1=A(α_1)+ε_1, P_2=A(α_2)+ε_2, etc., even
though no program contained the generic A. This is anti-unification/
generalization (Stitch compresses, Babble does equivalence-aware anti-unification);
our neural version must discover in functional space. Sleep gets mutation
operators: EXTRACT, GENERALIZE, PARAMETERIZE, FACTOR, MACRO.

Q5: DREAMING = counterfactual execution without external observation (internal
state → P → ŝ_{t+1} → ...). Three uses: planning, theory testing, abstraction
discovery. Dreamer shows learned world models support imagined rollouts, but our
transition model is a synthesized program from learned primitives, not a
monolithic network. CRITICAL: dreams are not evidence. P generating dream D_P
and evaluating P against D_P is self-confirmation. Constitutional rule: dreams
may propose, train search, expose consequences; external or held-out evidence
must adjudicate hypotheses.

Q6: THEORIZING → EXPERIMENTATION. If P_1 and P_2 both explain observations but
P_1(s,a) ≠ P_2(s,a), choose a* = argmax_a E[discrimination among H]. This is
active program induction. Benchmark: two hidden programs indistinguishable
under passive observation, one experiment separates them — test observe→
hypothesize→dream both→find disagreement→experiment (scientific reasoning in
miniature).

Q7: REASONING STRATEGIES AS PRIMITIVES. If propose→simulate→compare→backtrack
recurs, MDL promotes it to BACKWARD_CHAIN. Also FIND_INVARIANT,
TRY_COUNTEREXAMPLE, DIVIDE_AND_CONQUER, SIMULATE_AHEAD. Reasoning strategies are
compressed regularities in successful search. Brutal test: after promotion, do
unseen task families require fewer search expansions (C_search^before /
C_search^after)? If a reasoning abstraction doesn't reduce prospective search on
novel problems, don't call it reasoning. Be paranoid about measuring actual
causal reuse — library-learning research found tool reuse rare, gains attributed
to self-correction.

Q8: SEARCH LENGTH MATTERS, NOT JUST DESCRIPTION LENGTH. Two languages can both
express P, but under L_1 it's A∘B∘C∘D∘E and under L_2 it's M. Not only
L(P|L_2) < L(P|L_1) but C_synth(P|L_2) << C_synth(P|L_1). Good abstractions make
solutions findable, not just short. Replace MDL with J = L(D|P) + λD*(P,L) +
αC_synth + βC_execute + γC_learn. An abstraction may be worth creating even if
it barely reduces storage because it reshapes the search landscape.

Q9: PREVENTING THE SYNTHESIZER FROM BECOMING THE INTELLIGENCE. Biggest risk. If
we write a sophisticated MCTS around a modest library, we've hand-coded
intelligence into the search engine. Decompose: library quality vs proposal
quality vs search compute. Development order: oracle synthesis → brute-force/beam
→ learned proposer → learned search strategy, freezing everything else at each
stage. Eventually search traces train q_φ(P|D,L), giving two compilations:
breadth (q_φ) and depth (L).

Q10: WHEN IS THE LANGUAGE WRONG (NOT THE PROGRAM)? If a task needs 10^7 search
expansions, the conclusion could be "search harder" or "think in a different
language." Signal: C_synthesis(T|L) remains systematically large across related
tasks. Then sleep searches L' = argmin_L' [D*(L') + E_T C_solve(T|L')]. Insight
is a representation/language edit that causes the search problem to collapse
(10^6 candidates → 12).

TWO NESTED SEARCHES. Inner (reasoning): P* = argmin_{P∈Programs(L)} J(P). Outer
(abstraction): L* = argmin_L [D*(L) + E_T C_solve(T|L)]. They bootstrap:
L→P→experience→L'→P'→... The inner improves the outer by generating useful
trajectories; the outer improves the inner by changing its language. That loop
may be the actual research thesis.

COGNITIVE VOCABULARY UNIFIED. Perception = synthesize a representation program.
Reasoning = search/execute programs under current representation. Theorizing =
propose executable generative programs explaining observations. Planning =
execute candidate causal programs under hypothetical actions. Experimentation =
choose real observations that discriminate programs. Dreaming = internally
execute candidate programs/counterfactuals. Learning = update task programs/
search priors. Abstraction = alter the language to amortize recurring
computation. Insight = discover a representation that sharply reduces
description/search cost. Intuition = formerly expensive reasoning compiled into
a cheap reusable program.

LITTLE SCIENTIST WORLDS. The next benchmark, not ARC yet. Generate tiny worlds
from hidden programs where solving requires progressively more of the loop:
Rung A (infer one transformation), B (compose known primitives), C (choose
among competing perceptual decompositions), D (two theories fit passive
evidence; perform an experiment), E (invent a primitive), F (compile a
reasoning pattern), G (novel composition of perceptual+causal+reasoning
primitives), H (transfer to a new generator family). Every rung has an oracle,
so independently measure program recovery, representation recovery, search cost,
prediction, intervention efficiency, library growth, prospective learning
savings.

FAILURE MODES TO ACTIVELY SEEK. Neural non-compositionality (A,B good ≠ B∘A
good). Ontology lock-in (early abstractions prevent better representations;
maintain competing representations). Self-confirming dreams (imagined evidence
cannot validate its generator). Search explosion (expressive but intractable).
Overcompression (MDL prefers simple theory that misses rare important structure;
error code must price those failures). Single-use abstraction theater (named
modules never causally reused). Apparent structural intelligence that disappears
against the correct cheap counterfactual (V4's lesson).

DON'T FORCE A SINGLE ONTOLOGY. The library should contain multiple
transformations R_1,...,R_n and program synthesis chooses the useful view.
Abstraction means learning new lenses, not discovering the one correct latent
space. A way of seeing is useful insofar as it makes successful theories/
programs cheaper.

LANGUAGE EVOLUTION RULES (FROM V4). A proposed way of seeing must beat KEEP,
COMPRESS, and existing compositions. A primitive must demonstrate functional
substitutability. A schema must beat matched-budget private encoding. A retained
concept must repay prospective cost. Decisions interact through future library
evolution, so the outer loop is not merely compression but planning over changes
to the model's own language.

THE GRAND HYPOTHESIS CRYSTALLIZED. Intelligence may consist in continually
searching for programs that explain and control experience, while continually
inventing representations and primitives that make future program search
cheaper. Most compactly: reasoning searches within a language; learning improves
the search; abstraction improves the language. Dreaming/theorizing allows
programs in that language to be executed as hypotheses before reality settles the
question.


================================================================================
THE VERSION ROADMAP: V5 THROUGH ARC (NOT EXECUTED)
================================================================================

Treat V4 as the end of "can neural abstractions exist and have sensible
economics?" The next phase turns the library into a learned language for solving
novel problems. Sequence: economic abstractions → composable language → program
synthesis → language improvement → theorizing/planning → world learning →
ARC-like transfer.

V5 — REPRESENTATION ECONOMICS (close to V4).
V5.0: finish coding-frontier audit — establish actual rate-distortion cost D*(R)
of private residuals, shared operators, routes/references, arguments (not
arbitrary 8-bit). This is the currency for everything downstream.
V5.1: test amortization law causally — manipulate D(A) and s independently,
predict H* = λD(A)/s̄, check proportional and inverse-proportional movement.
If H*∝D and H*∝1/s, we have a genuine quantitative law, not a one-world
observation.
V5.2: abstraction-over-abstraction — build a world with controlled meta-recurrence
(A_i = C + Bα_i + ε_i), hold support per family fixed while increasing library
size, find the COMPRESS→FACTORIZE phase boundary. Tests whether learned
abstractions can become material for higher abstractions. If not, the recursive-
language story is in trouble.

V6 — MAKE THE LIBRARY COMPOSITIONAL. Library elements become instructions. Tiny
fixed substrate {CALL, COMPOSE, BIND, READ, WRITE, BRANCH, HALT} (LOOP later);
everything interesting (object, symmetry, search, planning, counting) is learned.
Compositional closure gate: train at depth 3, test d=4,5,6,8 and unseen
recombinations — does A,B,C individually useful imply C∘B∘A stable? If neural
operators don't compose reliably, solve that before synthesis (adapters, typed
interfaces, normalization, workspace contracts).

V7 — PROGRAM SYNTHESIS. Freeze library, infer P̂ ∈ Programs(L) from examples
(x_1,y_1)...(x_k,y_k). Oracle enumerator → beam search → learned proposer.
Measure separately expressibility / search / execution — never collapse into
task accuracy. C_synthesis becomes a new measurable quantity.

V8 — SUCCESSFUL PROGRAMS IMPROVE THE LANGUAGE. Promote M = A∘B∘C only
prospectively: D(M) + E[C_solve | L+M] < E[C_solve | L]. This upgrades MDL from
storage cost to thinking cost. Key experiment: measure C_synth^before, allow
sleep to invent a macro, present structurally related novel tasks, measure
C_synth^after. Strongest result: C_synth^after << C_synth^before on tasks the
macro was never trained to solve — an invented abstraction changes the search
landscape of future reasoning.

V9 — DIFFERENT WAYS OF SEEING. Tiny ambiguous visual worlds. Grid = cells /
objects / tiles / symmetry groups. Perceptual circuits R_i: x→W. Jointly infer
(R*,P*) = argmin_{R,P} [L(R) + L(P) + L(D|R,P) + αC_synth]. Two task families
over identical inputs where Task A is simple under connected components and
Task B under periodic tiling — same input produces different task-conditioned
perceptual programs. Perception isn't a frozen embedding.

V10 — HYPOTHESES, DREAMING, EXPERIMENTS. Once programs represent dynamics
P:(s,a)→s', synthesis becomes theorizing. Maintain H={P_1,...,P_k}: hypothesize
→ dream (P_i(s,a)→ŝ') → compare (find disagreements) → experiment
(a* = argmax_a Disagreement(...)) → observe → update. Test in worlds where
passive observation is insufficient — two hidden rules generate identical
observations, only a diagnostic action reveals which is true.

V11 — REASONING BECOMES LIBRARY MATERIAL. Collect search traces; repeated
backward-chaining → BACKWARD_CHAIN. Test causally: does using M reduce search on
novel problem families? If yes, search→compiled reasoning is experimentally real.

PROCEDURALLY GENERATED GAMES (after V11). Not Atari or chess — generated ecology
of small compositional worlds (Objects + Perceptual rules + Dynamics + Goal +
Hidden variables). Training objective: J = Σ_t [L_prediction + C_exploration +
C_synthesis + C_planning + C_execution] + λD*(L_T). Key test: E[C_solve(G_t)] ↓
even though each G_t remains novel — becoming better at understanding new worlds.

LEARN THE SYNTHESIZER. Search traces train q_φ(P|D,L). Library learning reduces
search depth (A∘B∘C→M); proposal learning reduces search breadth (10^5→100).
Experience improves both the language of thought and the process of searching it.

ARC AS EXTERNAL EXAM. Freeze slow library, give ARC problems. Observe: search
perceptual interpretations → generate candidate programs → execute on
demonstrations → reject inconsistent → prefer compact → execute survivor on test.
Dream result: on an unseen task, a seven-step program with three perceptual
primitives, two transformation primitives, and one reasoning macro, none seen
during training.

FOUR MILESTONES. I — Language: primitives compose reliably (if not, stop).
II — Synthesis: novel programs inferred from sparse evidence (if not, solve
search). III — Self-improvement: solutions cause useful language changes that
reduce future synthesis cost. IV — Theorizing: programs as executable hypotheses
for prediction, experimentation, planning.

AVOID THE SEDUCTIVE SHORTCUT. vision model → symbolic ARC DSL → massive hand-
written synthesizer → ARC could perform well while teaching nothing. Our claim
requires the computational vocabulary itself be learned from experience; provide
only the minimum universal substrate.

THE COMPLETE LOOP (if V8 works): solve → discover recurring computation →
invent abstraction → change language → solve novel problems more easily.
Everything after V8 adds richer programs and environments to the same loop. The
ultimate demonstration: a neural system learns its own compositional language of
thought, uses that language to synthesize solutions to novel problems, and
improves the language itself from the solutions it discovers. If we get from V4
to that, ARC is a scaling and transfer problem, not a wild conceptual jump.
