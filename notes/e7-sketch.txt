E7 SKETCH -- parameterized macros, and the inference problem E5 did not test
Written 2026-08-29, after the E6 line closed. NOT a plan and NOT registered;
a plan must be frozen before any code, as always.

================================================================================
1. THE CRITIQUE E6 MAKES OF ITSELF
================================================================================

H39's confirmed central lesson is that the useful abstraction is

    A(alpha) + eps

-- parameterized, made during learning, with cheap coordinates for novelty --
and NOT a rigid `A`. That was established on tensors, sealed on seeds 700-729.

The entire E6 line then tested `A`. A macro is a CONSTANT fragment: `M := A B C`
is a fixed symbol sequence with no argument. Read in that light, E6's results
stop looking like facts about macros and start looking like a rediscovery, in
linguistic currency, of what H39 already established about tensors:

    E6A  a rigid macro pays in description, but only ~3-7x its crossing
    E6D  its creation cannot be timed from a retrospective code
    E6.2 it cannot be compiled at matched budget at all

We should not have expected otherwise. The object under test was the one the
project's own confirmed result says is the wrong object.

================================================================================
2. THE NUMBER THAT MAKES THIS CONCRETE
================================================================================

E6 step 0 measured MOTIF SURVIVAL: the fraction of planted recurrences that
produce the SAME learner gram.

    world 0   7/32 = 22%
    world 1  14/32 = 44%
    world 2  13/32 = 41%
    mean            ~36%

We treated the missing ~64% as gauge noise that limited the rung. That reading is
available but it is not the only one. Those are recurrences of the SAME
COMPUTATION written as DIFFERENT SYMBOL SEQUENCES -- which is exactly what a
parameterized macro should capture and a rigid one structurally cannot.

    A rigid macro collects only exact repeats.
    A parameterized macro `M(alpha)` should collect the family.

QUANTITATIVE PREDICTION, to be registered before it is measured: a parameterized
macro's effective use count is ~2-3x a rigid one's on the SAME corpus, because it
collects the near-misses. E6A's realized 22-56 uses against a crossing of 7.44
becomes a materially different economic picture at 60-150.

It also attacks E6.2's capacity obstruction from the right side. A single
composition needed ~4x a library slot to represent. But a FAMILY of related
compositions may share a low-dimensional argument even when no individual member
is representable at matched budget -- the family can be cheaper per member than
any one member is alone.

================================================================================
3. THE FREE PRE-CHECK, WHICH DECIDES WHETHER TO OPEN THE LINE AT ALL
================================================================================

Before building anything: measure, on the EXISTING E6 corpora, whether the ~64%
of non-matching recurrences form a LOW-DIMENSIONAL FAMILY in function space.

    - take the planted sites whose learner gram is NOT the modal image
    - compute each one's effective task-conditioned operator on a common probe
      (E5's lesson: two functions can only be compared at the SAME inputs)
    - ask whether those operators lie near a low-dimensional subspace, scored
      LEAVE-ONE-OUT (V5's lesson: never fit and score on the same objects)

This is arithmetic over cached routes. No new compute, no lifetimes.

    structured   -> `M(alpha)` has a real economic case; open the rung
    unstructured -> the argument channel buys nothing, `M(alpha)` collapses to
                    `M`, and the line is closed before it is built

This is the opportunity gate applied to a language question: measure that the
opportunity EXISTS before building the machine to exploit it. It is the same
discipline that should have preceded two dormancy designs and the B2 gate.

================================================================================
4. THE INFERENCE PROBLEM E5 DID NOT TEST
================================================================================

The thesis says adaptation is IDENTIFY-PRIMITIVE + INFER-ARGUMENT + SMALL PATCH.

E5 asked for `q(p | D)` -- write the whole program -- and it failed on quality
while search stayed cheap. But that is not the decomposition the thesis predicts.
The narrower object is

    q(alpha | D, p)

given the program's structure, infer its argument. That is a far smaller
inference problem, continuous rather than combinatorial, and it is the half the
H39 line actually confirmed works. E5's negative does not touch it.

If amortization pays anywhere in this substrate it should be here. And if it does
NOT, that is a much more damaging result for the thesis than E5 was -- which is
precisely why it is worth running. E5 tested a decomposition the thesis never
claimed; this tests the one it does.

Note the asymmetry to register in advance: `q(alpha | D, p)` is given the
program. That is a real information advantage over E5's writer and must be
disclosed as such -- the comparison to E5 is NOT apples to apples, and the rung
should carry a control where `p` is given to the E5-style writer too.

================================================================================
5. TWO CHEAPER DIAGNOSTICS, BOTH FROM E6.2's LEFTOVERS
================================================================================

CLOSURE. E6.2 found that one operator at matched budget cannot represent three
composed, with a converged fit -- so the function class is NOT CLOSED UNDER
COMPOSITION at fixed capacity. The vocabulary can EXPRESS compositions it cannot
INTERNALIZE. That is a structural bound on what primitive invention could mean
here, and it does not depend on the coding scheme.

There is a hint in the data: world 1 was the ONLY world that passed at matched
budget, and its macro `(8,8,5)` contains a REPEATED operator. Test: does the
effective rank / spectral profile of `P_c o P_b o P_a` predict matched-budget
compilability across many fragments? If it does, we get a FUNCTIONAL criterion
for which fragments are worth compiling -- a property of the function rather than
of the accounting. Hours, no lifetimes. Feeds directly into section 3.

ATTRACTOR COLLAPSE. At depth >= 8 all three worlds converge on a single
per-library slot (2, 11, 7) and the inferred program stops describing the
computation while the executor still solves the task. This was found incidentally
and labelled a scope limit. It deserves its own rung: it marks where the PROGRAM
abstraction fails even though the EXECUTOR does not, which bounds the whole
export-branch story, and nothing currently measures it directly.

================================================================================
6. A DEFLATIONARY READING THAT BELONGS IN THE PAPER
================================================================================

The standard motivation for library learning is that libraries make search
tractable. E5.1 killed that here: `C_find` is linear in program length and
logarithmic in program count, and route optimization matched or beat the oracle
in a 2.99M-program space. E6 then showed the description saving is small and the
creation timing undecidable from the past.

So in this substrate the value of the learned vocabulary is that it EXISTS and
TRANSFERS, not that it makes anything cheaper. That is the honest summary and it
should be stated rather than left implicit, because it is a real negative result
about WHY one would build a library at all.

================================================================================

    (a) the free pre-check of section 3          -- decides whether to proceed
    (b) the closure diagnostic of section 5      -- hours, feeds (a)'s reading
    (c) parameterized macros `M(alpha)`          -- the main line
    (d) `q(alpha | D, p)`                        -- the thesis's own adaptation claim
    (e) attractor collapse                       -- bounds the program story

        macro invention, tested on branching, loops and reusable subgraphs

(a) and (b) cost almost nothing and can reorder everything after them. Neither
should be skipped on the grounds that the direction already looks right.

8. METHODOLOGICAL, CARRIED FORWARD
================================================================================

Five estimands or controls in the E6 line turned out unable to fail, each caught
before compute: an algebraic crossing, a definitional substitutability test, two
arithmetic refusal controls, and a tolerance that a random operator passed. That
is past the point where a written rule is the right fix.

PROPOSAL: the estimand analogue of `src/row/arm_provenance.py`. Require every
registered estimand to ship with a NULL CONSTRUCTION under which it must return
the negative, and assert it as a test. One mechanism would have caught all five.

9. CENSUS RESULT (2026-08-29, run same day) -- SECTION 2's RATIONALE IS REFUTED
================================================================================

Ran `src/row/experiments/audit_e7_census.py`. Report `reports/e7_census.json`.
CENSUS ONLY: no verdict, no registered hypothesis, ~zero compute.

The question was whether the ~64% of planted recurrences that do NOT produce the
modal learner gram form a low-dimensional family in function space. If they do,
`M(alpha)` collects them and the economics of E6A change materially.

    world  modal share  distinct non-matching grams
      0        34%              19
      1        83%               1   -- UNINFORMATIVE, tight gauge, nothing to collect
      2        41%              17

Leave-one-out subspace capture, common probe, against two nulls:

    world 0   dim  random null        learner-written null
               3   +0.214              +0.203
               4   +0.230              +0.064
               6   +0.246              +0.103
               8   +0.125              +0.116
    world 2    3   +0.166              +0.148
               4   +0.131              +0.036
               6   +0.222              +0.121
               8   +0.259              +0.158

AGAINST RANDOM GRAMS the family looks clearly structured (+0.13 to +0.26).
AGAINST GRAMS THE SAME LEARNER WROTE AT UNPLANTED SITES the motif-specific excess
collapses to +0.03 to +0.15, crossing 0.15 at ONE dimension per world, and at
DIFFERENT dimensions in the two worlds (3 in w0, 8 in w2).

CONCLUSION: the near-misses are barely more of a family than any other gram this
learner writes. **Section 2's rationale does not survive.** A parameterized macro
would not collect the missing 64%, because those 64% are not a motif-specific
family -- most of the structure they have is structure that learner-written grams
have in general.

WHAT THE NULL DID. The first null was RANDOM grams, and it would have opened the
line (+0.21 to +0.25, "structured"). The second null -- same author, same corpus,
no planted structure -- removed most of it. Random grams answer "is this a
subspace at all"; only the learner-written null answers "is this subspace ABOUT
THE MOTIF". Ask what a null actually controls for. This is the contaminated-null
family again, in a new costume: not a null estimated from the effect, but a null
too weak to isolate it.

WHAT SURVIVES. Section 2 is dead; sections 4, 5 and 6 are untouched:

  * `q(alpha | D, p)` -- the inference problem E5 never tested. H39's confirmed
    success parameterized an IN-BASIS argument inside a routed slot, NOT a
    collection of surface near-misses. That is a different construction and this
    census says nothing against it.
  * the CLOSURE obstruction, and whether effective rank predicts compilability.
  * the ATTRACTOR COLLAPSE rung.
  * the deflationary reading for the paper.

WHAT THIS COST. About twenty minutes and no lifetimes, to close a line that would
have taken days and produced a null with an appealing story attached. That is the
opportunity gate working exactly as the constitution intends -- and it is worth
noting that the gate only worked because the SECOND null was built. With the
first null alone this note would now be recommending the rung.

10. E8D WITHDRAWN BEFORE FREEZING, AND THE REFRAME IT FORCES (2026-08-30)
================================================================================

Section 4's successor -- a decomposition ladder asking what share of adaptation
`p`, `alpha` and `eps` each carry -- was drafted as `E8D_DECOMPOSITION_PLAN.md`
and WITHDRAWN before a freeze hash was recorded. Its own non-vacuity gate fails
against E1's existing numbers:

    route-only inference is at or better than EVERY other arm in 6/6 cells,
    including the oracle program, and library fine-tuning (the intended
    ceiling) is ~10x WORSE because it overfits the support.

So on the export branch's held-out programs there is NO adaptation gap to
decompose. `p` alone is sufficient -- which is exactly what the sealed block
established, seen from the other side.

THE REFRAME. The decomposition question is only contentful where the language
does NOT already cover the task. So the experiment is not a ladder at one
operating point; it is a ladder AS A FUNCTION OF COVERAGE:

    coverage knob  ->  share(P), share(P+A), share(P+A+E)

At full coverage the answer is already known and verifiable from E1: `share(P)`
is everything. The question is what picks up the slack as coverage degrades --
the ARGUMENT (thesis-supporting: novelty is a coordinate in the existing basis)
or the PATCH (thesis-undermining: novelty is unstructured residual).

A COVERAGE KNOB ALREADY EXISTS. E1-R used DONOR-WORLD libraries as its recurrence
control -- a library trained on a different world is precisely a partially
covering language, and E1's report records `donor_world` per cell. Grading donor
mismatch gives coverage without inventing a new generator, and it reuses
machinery that is already sealed-adjacent and understood.

Registered before any such plan is written: at full coverage `share(P) ~ 1` and
the other terms are undefined-by-saturation; that anchor point must be VERIFIED
from E1 rather than re-run, and any coverage sweep must include it so the
saturated end is visible in the same figure as the informative end.

WHAT THIS COST. Twenty minutes of checking a drafted plan's own gate against data
already on disk. What it avoided: a multi-arm rung returning `share(P+A) ~ 0`,
which without this check reads as evidence against the project's central sentence
when it only says the tasks were too easy. Second time in two days that a
pre-check has killed a plan before it was frozen.

11. THE COVERAGE REFRAME DIES TOO, AND WHAT THAT SAYS ABOUT THE TESTBED
================================================================================

Section 10 proposed running the decomposition ladder as a function of COVERAGE,
with recurrence `rho` or donor libraries as the knob. Checked against E1-R's
existing nine cells before drafting anything.

  regime          O         R         S      best-other minus R
  rho = 0.0     0.0386    0.0368    0.0379        +0.03
  rho = 0.9     0.0239    0.0231    0.0396        +0.03
  rho = 1.0     0.0067    0.0068    0.0421        -0.01

Across ALL NINE cells route-only inference is within +/-0.07 log units of the
best available arm in 8 of 9. There is no coverage regime with a decomposable
gap.

THE STRUCTURAL FACT, which is bigger than the dead plan:

    `p` IS SUFFICIENT WHEREVER ANYTHING IS SUFFICIENT.

Where the library covers the task, route inference reaches the oracle. Where it
does not, nothing reaches anything -- at `rho = 0` the oracle, the inferred route
and a from-scratch learner all sit at ~0.038. This generator produces tasks that
are either IN-LANGUAGE (solved by a route) or OUT-OF-LANGUAGE (solved by
nothing), and nothing in between.

CONSEQUENCE. The decomposition question -- what share of adaptation `p`, `alpha`
and `eps` each carry -- CANNOT BE ASKED on this testbed as constructed. It needs
tasks a program gets PARTWAY, and this generator does not make them. Any rung
that tries will return `share(P) ~ 1` for a reason that has nothing to do with
the thesis.

TWO HONEST OPTIONS, and they should be chosen deliberately rather than drifted
into:

  (a) BUILD A PARTIAL-COVERAGE GENERATOR. Tasks whose teacher program is mostly
      in-language with a controlled component outside the library's span. This is
      a NEW GENERATOR: it breaks comparability with every existing artifact, and
      the project's own history says a spec that changes its generator must be
      re-read for everything downstream. Substantial commitment.

  (b) REPORT THE FINDING AS THE ANSWER. In this substrate, adaptation IS
      identify-program. That is a clean positive statement about the export
      branch and the honest joint reading of E1, E1-R and E5 -- and it is a
      NEGATIVE for the three-term thesis in this testbed, since two of its three
      terms have nothing to do here.

THREE PRE-CHECKS, THREE KILLS, IN THREE DAYS: the E7 census (near-misses are not
a family), E8D's gate (no gap on covered tasks), and this one (no gap at any
coverage). None of them cost more than half an hour. That rate is not bad luck --
it is a signal that the development testbed has been exhausted for THIS class of
question, and that the next real move is either a new generator or consolidation.

12. LOOP OPPORTUNITY CENSUS (2026-08-31): LOOPS DO NOT PAY WHERE THEY WOULD MEAN
    ANYTHING
================================================================================

Review 82 places branching/loops after macro invention. Loops are censusable on
existing data, so they were checked before any plan was drafted. CENSUS ONLY, no
verdict, no compute: arithmetic over 1,152 cached E6 routes.

A loop encodes a run of `k` identical adjacent operators as `(LOOP, op, count)`:
2 symbols plus a count, `~10.7` bits against `k * 3.70` bits plain. **A run only
pays at `k >= 3`.**

    depth  routes  runs k>=2  runs that PAY  gross  alphabet tax    NET
      6      384      340          91         182       266        -84
      8      384      527         199         512       355       +157
     10      384      722         271         635       443       +192

**At depth 6 -- the only regime where E6A found macro meaning intact (53% motif
survival) -- a loop construct is NET NEGATIVE.** It pays only at depths 8 and 10,
where motif survival is 13% and 7% and the routes have collapsed onto per-library
attractor slots. The repetition there is the attractor artifact, not structure a
loop deserves credit for.

CONCLUSION: loops are answered, and the answer is NO, for the same reason macros
barely paid. The programs are too short and the repetition too shallow for
control flow to amortize.

A CORRECTION TO OUR OWN FIRST PASS. The first census counted every run of `k >= 2`
as saving `k - 1` symbols and reported ~9,073 bits of opportunity. That is wrong
twice: a `k = 2` run CANNOT pay (the loop encoding costs more than writing the
pair), and the alphabet tax was omitted. 1,028 of the 1,589 runs are `k = 2`. The
gross figure would have justified building a loop construct; the corrected one
kills it.

BRANCHING IS DIFFERENT AND CANNOT BE CENSUSED. It is the one missing CAPABILITY
rather than a missing economy: every program here is a fixed pipeline, the route
is per-task and not per-input, so the program performs no computation -- it is a
constant that gets executed. Branching would be the first construct whose
execution depends on the data, and that is where this substrate would start
computing rather than composing.

But the generator never produces input-dependent programs, so NOTHING IN EXISTING
DATA CAN REVEAL A NEED FOR IT. There is no opportunity gate available. Building
it means committing to a new world blind -- the shape that produced E9 and, before
it, two dormancy designs. That is not an argument against doing it; it is an
argument for deciding it deliberately, with the missing gate acknowledged, rather
than drifting into it as the next rung.

13. CORRECTION TO SECTION 12 (2026-08-31): CATEGORY ERROR
================================================================================

Section 12 concluded "loops are answered, and the answer is NO". That is an
overclaim and is corrected here.

The census asked whether a loop AMORTIZES in this testbed. It was read as
answering whether iteration is NEEDED for program synthesis. Macros, loops and
branching are constitutive of a programming language, not optional economies --
if the goal is a substrate that writes programs, they are what "program" means.

The census negative is a fact about the GENERATOR, which has no iterative and no
conditional structure. It composes a fixed number of primitives in sequence, so
there is nothing for a loop to iterate over and the census measured the absence
of iteration in the world rather than the uselessness of loops.

CORRECTED READING OF ALL FOUR CENSUSES (near-miss families, coverage, decomposition
gap, loops): the development testbed cannot evaluate this class of question. That
was already the conclusion of section 11 and it should have been applied here.

CONSEQUENCE. The successor is a generator whose tasks REQUIRE what a fixed-length
program cannot express. Two candidate constructions:

  * ITERATION-NECESSARY. The target applies an operator a number of times that
    depends on the input, so no fixed-depth composition can be correct on all
    inputs. Verify by construction: exhibit two inputs requiring different depths.

  * BRANCH-NECESSARY. The target applies one of two operators according to a
    predicate on the state. Verify by construction: no single fixed program
    matches the target on both branches better than chance.

THE GATE MOVES INTO THE GENERATOR. The necessity above must be verified BEFORE any
learner is trained -- a fixed-length program provably cannot win -- which is a gate
we CAN build, unlike the ones the current testbed refused to supply, because the
world is being constructed rather than inherited.

This is also what review 82 reserved the graph-rewrite substrate for: branching,
recursion, loops, macros, structural rewriting and language growth, tested on a
world that needs them.
