Yes. The V5 spec is much stronger than the earlier sketch. The overall ladder -
LAW -> PHASE DIAGRAM -> SELECTION - is right, and the discipline around
cost/utility separation, matched-budget comparisons, and blocking rather than
forcing failed worlds is excellent.

I would make **six substantive changes before freezing**, though. Two are fairly
important.

## 1. Replace H20's pairwise-correlation validity metric

This is the biggest technical issue I see.

Your generator is:

    theta_f(r) = sqrt(r) B alpha_f + sqrt(1-r) B_f beta_f

At r = 1, every abstraction lies in the same shared subspace B. But if the
alpha_f's point in different directions, **pairwise function correlation can
still be near zero**.

For example A_1 = B[1,0] and A_2 = B[0,1]. They are maximally related in the
sense you care about - they are coordinates in exactly the same two-dimensional
family - but their behavioral correlation can be zero.

So this validity criterion - mean pairwise residual-function correlation -
doesn't actually measure your intended r_meta.

I'd replace it with something like **functional shared-subspace capture**:

    R_meta = 1 - ( sum_f |A_f - B_hat alpha_hat_f|^2 )
                 / ( sum_f |A_f - A_bar|^2 )

Fit B_hat in functional space on one probe set and evaluate on disjoint probes.

Even better, use leave-one-family-out prediction:

    R_LOO = 1 - |A_heldout - B_hat alpha_hat_heldout|^2
                / |A_heldout - A_bar|^2

Then require R_meta(0) ~ null, and R_meta(r) monotonically increasing.

That measures **belonging to a common functional language**, rather than
superficial correlation.

# 2. H20's parameter-free M* prediction needs a cleaner protocol

The proposed law is great: M* = D*(S) / s_bar_schema.

But there's a subtle problem. If you re-fit S independently at every M, then
D*(S) = D*(S; M) and s_bar_schema = s_bar_schema(M). The schema gets better
estimated as more members arrive. Then there's no genuinely fixed C + Ms line
whose crossing you're predicting. You risk predicting M* using quantities that
themselves depend on M*.

### I'd split H20 into two experiments.

### H20a - Pure recursive amortization

Take a calibration set of, say, M_0 = 4 members. Fit schema S. **Freeze it.**

On additional unseen family members, measure:

    s_i = D*(A_i) - [ D(alpha_i) + D*(E_i) ]

Then s_bar_schema = E[s_i].

Now predict:

    M*_additional = ( D*(S) - savings already accrued ) / s_bar_schema

Add held-out members one at a time without changing S.

That's a true amortization experiment.

### H20b - Learned phase diagram

Then allow the factorization to be re-estimated at every M. Now ask where the
*actual learning system* switches representation.

H20a tests the law. H20b tests the learner.

That separation is analogous to the frozen/open-library distinction that rescued
RETAIN. I think it will save you another round of counterfactual confusion.

# 3. Your current H19 s-arms aren't as clean as they could be

This is the nicest immediate improvement available.

You currently propose changing eta, or new_primitive_families. But both can
alter much more than s_bar. They can change the function that gets learned,
promotion dynamics, approximation quality, etc.

You can manipulate **future utility directly while keeping A byte-identical**.

After the abstraction has formed and the gap begins, give returning tasks a
coefficient:

    y = f_base(x) + g A(x)

Sweep g in {0.5, 1, 1.5} or whatever yields useful separation.

Crucially: same A; same D*(A); same pre-gap history; same library; only the
contribution of A to returning tasks changes.

Then s_bar(g) changes while D*(A) should remain constant. Now predict:

    H*(g) = lambda D*(A) / s_bar(g)

That is a much cleaner inverse-s test. It directly fills the one missing part of
H19.

I'd call it something like **return-value gain**, not an eta arm. And establish
the gain only *after* the abstraction is frozen so it cannot change its birth.

# 4. Don't require natural PROMOTE dynamics to behave identically before
establishing H20 economics

Your +/-10% balance gate on promotion rate is defensible, but there's a
possibility it blocks the interesting phenomenon for the wrong reason.

Suppose r_meta up causes PROMOTE itself to produce fewer atoms because its
existing continuous representation absorbs their commonality. Then M goes down.

That's not necessarily a broken generator. It could mean:

> **The lower-level representation already captures meta-recurrence, so a new
> schema object isn't economically necessary.**

That's scientifically interesting.

I would therefore have two layers:

### Teacher / controlled-atom H20

Give the oracle exactly A_1, ..., A_F as the objects to represent. No PROMOTE
dynamics. Ask purely COMPRESS vs FACTORIZE. This establishes whether a schema
economy exists at all.

### Learned-library H20

Now let V3 produce the atoms. Possible outcomes:

1. learner produces F atoms and FACTORIZE wins;
2. learner produces F atoms and COMPRESS wins;
3. learner collapses meta-structure into fewer atoms already.

Outcome 3 is **not generator invalidity**. It's an alternative solution.

That's potentially very important to your grand theory: hierarchy need not
emerge explicitly if a cheaper lower-level representation already absorbs the
regularity. The system should care about cost, not about producing the hierarchy
we expected.

# 5. Tighten the V6 compositional-closure probe now

I'm glad you ran it and it passed the current gate. But because V6-V8 depend so
heavily on this, I'd extract more information from the same cheap test.

Right now the result is approximately a depth-8/depth-3 degradation of 4.1-4.4x,
still under the registered 5x gate.

Don't just measure d = 3 and d = 8. Measure d = 1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12. Then plot
log NMSE(d) against depth. We need to know whether error grows approximately
O(d), O(d^2), or O(c^d). Those imply very different futures.

Also report median, p90, worst decile of routes, and saturation frequency. A
median can hide "most compositions work but 20% catastrophically explode."
That's deadly for program synthesis.

What V6 really needs is a **composition error law**, not merely a depth-8 pass.

# 6. Fix one conceptual contradiction in H23

The spec says J_pi(t:T) = L_preq[t:T] + lambda D_T and structural regret
compares policies over the remaining trajectory. That's correct.

But immediately afterward it says:

> never end-of-lifetime J after two libraries have evolved apart.

Those are actually two different lessons getting mixed together.

For **estimating the causal value of deleting one abstraction**, divergent
endpoint J isn't a paired per-object estimate. Correct.

But for **comparing structural policies**, trajectory divergence is precisely
what we care about. If pi_1 causes one future library and pi_2 causes another,
then J_pi1(t:T) - J_pi2(t:T) over the same suffix is absolutely the policy
comparison.

I'd rewrite the rule:

> Mid-lifetime interventions cease to support a *paired per-object effect* after
> their trajectories diverge. Structural-policy evaluation intentionally scores
> the full divergent suffix from a shared prefix.

That's important because V5.5 is specifically supposed to embrace path
dependence.

# One larger suggestion: H28 may deserve promotion in the roadmap

I like H28 a lot. Possibly more than H20.

The eventual learned language will encounter the same computation in different
neural coordinate systems constantly: f_i = d_i . A . e_i. If the system only
recognizes reuse when computations literally live in the same coordinates, the
eventual LLM/vision/world-model story breaks pretty quickly.

The real abstraction relation is closer to: f_i ~ f_j if cheap coordinate
transformations reveal the same morphism.

And your proposed MDL test is exactly right:

    D*(A) + sum_i [ D*(e_i) + D*(d_i) ]  <  sum_i D*(f_i)

The adapter-budget restriction is essential because otherwise e_i or d_i can
secretly implement the whole operation.

I wouldn't necessarily run H28 before H20. But I would make it a **V6 entry
question**, not leave it buried as optional V5.7. Because before we say "we have
a learned language of composable operations," we should probably know whether
its "words" can survive a change of representational coordinates.

# H27 also has a better causal version available

The observation D*_shared < D*_private may eventually be more important than it
currently looks.

Your artifact audit can distinguish some hypotheses, but it won't cleanly
separate selection, purification, and restructuring.

A stronger later experiment would take the **same promotable computation** and
compare:

    Arm A: PROMOTE disabled. Information remains distributed privately.
    Arm B: PROMOTE enabled. Same recurrence becomes shared.

Then compare the functionally corresponding computation at matched behavioral
performance. If D*_after < D*_before even after matching which computation we're
talking about, then abstraction formation itself changed coding geometry.

That would support: **abstraction is representational restructuring, not merely
deduplication.**

Don't delay H20 for it - the frozen-artifact H27 is rightly cheap - but keep
that causal sequel in mind.

# One H22 change I'd strongly consider

H22 claims one prospective score chooses among edits. Then **every edit should
ultimately be converted into the same dJ currency**.

The spec currently gives PROMOTE its "V3 frozen criterion, unchanged." That's
fine for deciding whether V3 itself births an abstraction. But it weakens the
H22 theoretical claim if COMPRESS is valued via dJ, RETAIN via dJ, FACTORIZE via
dJ, and PROMOTE via a special structural acceptance rule.

I'd distinguish PROMOTE legality/structural evidence from PROMOTE economic
value. So PROMOTE can retain its V3 substitutability/generalization gate, but
once legal, score it with:

    dJ_hat_promote = dL_hat_future + lambda dD*_hat

Then H22 really is e* = argmin_e dJ_hat(e) subject to operation-specific
legality constraints. That's much cleaner.

# My priority order

I would **not** rewrite the overall spec. It's good. Before freezing I'd do:

1. **Change H20's meta-recurrence instrument** from pairwise correlation to
   functional shared-subspace/LOO capture.
2. Add the **fixed-schema H20a** so M* = D(S)/s is genuinely predictive rather
   than M-dependent.
3. Replace or supplement H19 S1/S2 with a **post-birth return-value multiplier**
   that changes s while leaving A fixed.
4. Clarify H23's policy-regret vs paired-causal distinction.
5. Make H22 score PROMOTE in the common economic currency after its structural
   legality gate.
6. Expand the already-passed composition probe into an **error-vs-depth curve**.
7. Keep H28 highly visible as the likely bridge between toy neural libraries and
   actual learned representations.

Everything else I'd mostly leave alone.

The core of this spec is right: V5 shouldn't prove that sophisticated
representations can exist. It should discover whether **the same prospective
economics tells us when each representation is worth inventing**.

And the strongest possible V5 result, in my view, would be:

    N_uses * s > C_A        (atom)
    N_atoms * s_schema > C_S (schema)

with both crossings **predicted before their sweeps from independently measured
costs and benefits**.

If you get that, then going into V6 you don't merely have a collection of
successful abstractions. You have evidence for a **recursive economic law
governing the growth of a learned computational language**.
