I agree with your recommendation, with one important refinement: the next
run should be framed as a JOINT-FORMATION EXISTENCE PILOT, not as a rescue
of the failed post-hoc H39 construction.

The census has established something quite useful:

    The finished ordinary learner does not contain the nearby novel
    solutions in the affine span of the objects it retained.

The max-rank result makes that much stronger than "rank 8 was too small".
And because this is already true for the ordinary learner, the "lost
variation opportunity" problem predates the harmful V6 prospective
intervention.

What remains genuinely unknown is: can a schema preserve those directions
if it is allowed to form while the tasks are being learned? That is the
experiment I would run next.

1. One world is appropriate — but make the question binary. World 0,
explicitly exploratory, is enough for a feasibility test. Pilot hypothesis:
online factorized formation can preserve a low-dimensional space in which
an unseen sibling is cheaply expressible, even though no such space can be
recovered post hoc from the ordinary learner. Same task stream/world and
ordinary objective. Crucially, do NOT revive the prospective loss. The only
substantive intervention should be the representation architecture:
r_f = W alpha_f + eps_f, with W a slow shared schema learned jointly across
observed family operators, alpha_f a small fast argument, eps_f exact-null
private innovation. No teacher operator values. For an existence
experiment, using oracle family grouping to determine which examples update
the same structured mechanism is acceptable, as long as it is explicit:
you're asking whether the substrate CAN REPRESENT the decomposition, not
whether it can discover the grouping.

2. Make the primary pilot endpoint alpha-only future opportunity. After
training, freeze everything. For the genuinely held-out sibling, eps_new = 0
and learn only alpha_new. Then repeat exactly the abundant-support
acquisition measurement that killed the post-hoc census. Desired:
L*_alpha-only <= 1.5 L*_ordinary, using the already registered threshold
for continuity. That's the critical test. Not geometry. Not PCA capture. Not
whether W looks like the teacher. Ask: did joint formation leave the next
sibling economically reachable through a small argument?

3. Also measure whether the architecture actually uses the schema.
Otherwise it can cheat through eps. During training report D*(W), D*(alpha),
D*(eps) and functional contribution from each channel — particularly the
fraction of family computation carried by W alpha, or a causal ablation
equivalent. If current tasks are solved almost entirely through private
innovation and the held-out alpha happens to work by accident, that's not
the mechanism. Conversely, don't demand eps = 0 during ordinary training;
exact-null innovation should let the learner use private computation when
genuinely necessary.

4. Collect one extra diagnostic during the same fresh run. The census
revealed an ambiguity around retirement: the final ordinary population is
small because many residuals were retired into references. Cheaply snapshot
the HISTORICAL pre-retirement family solutions. Then: if the historical span
works but the final span fails, lifecycle/retirement discarded variation
directions; if historical also fails, ordinary wake never formed those
directions in the first place. My guess is the latter, given how flat the
residual spectra are, but it is worth measuring.

5. Preregister these failure branches.
A. Joint schema works: current tasks competitive and L*_alpha-only,new near
   the ordinary free-residual endpoint. Major positive: variation
   opportunity must be preserved during representation formation; it cannot
   necessarily be reconstructed afterward. Then full H39 is licensed; next
   compare against an equally sized generic task-local channel.
B. Current tasks fit, but alpha-only future still fails. Most informative
   negative: W alpha + eps can describe the observed population but creates
   no useful coordinates for nearby novelty. Do not proceed to H40-H44; look
   seriously at nonlinear parameterized operators / conditional primitives.
C. Future works only with a large eps_new. W alpha is not genuinely fertile;
   the architecture merely preserved the ability to relearn privately. A
   failure of the schema hypothesis even if total adaptation is good.
D. The factorized learner cannot even match current-task performance. We
   have not tested fertility; we have built an overly restrictive ABI.
   Points toward a richer schema family S_theta(alpha) rather than linear
   W alpha.

6. My prediction has shifted. I am less confident than before this census
that a simple linear schema will work. The strongest warning is the full
available affine span at 2.2-3.1x the ordinary endpoint: the ordinary
learner is not merely choosing the wrong low-rank projection; its solutions
occupy a representation whose observed directions do not extrapolate to
nearby novelty. Rough priors: jointly trained W alpha + eps materially
improves alpha-only novelty, ~50%; helps but leaves a substantial gap,
~30%; simple linear schema fundamentally insufficient, ~20%. Joint
formation has a real chance because it changes the optimization trajectory
rather than compressing an already-degenerate cloud. But I would not assume
success.

7. This illuminates the program-synthesis goal. The census is the neural
analogue of a language whose existing programs cannot span the next natural
program: compress everything seen, and the next nearby concept still lies
outside closure(L) under the cheap transformations supported. No post-hoc
compression of L fixes that. A future computational language must preserve
PRODUCTIVE DIMENSIONS, not merely summarize realized programs. A learned
primitive P(alpha) should not just compress observed instances; its
parameter space should contain useful nearby instances that have not yet
occurred — systematic generalization in the primitive itself.

8. Two kinds of compression. EXTENSIONAL: compactly encode the computations
actually observed (the ordinary learner / post-hoc schema). INTENSIONAL:
learn a rule or parameterization describing a family of possible
computations including unobserved ones — P(alpha); rotate(angle) rather
than {rotate10, rotate20, rotate30}. The census suggests extensional
compression alone is insufficient for the grand project. The held-out
sibling tests whether the representation learned rotate(angle) or merely
stored several rotations.

9. If the pilot works the roadmap is clean: observed related computations
-> jointly form P(alpha) -> new sibling => infer only alpha_new ->
P_i(alpha_i) o P_j(alpha_j) -> program synthesis over parameterized
primitives -> discover recurring program families -> M(beta), where even
macros become parameterized abstractions.

So yes: write and freeze a small one-world jointly-trained schema pilot
answering exactly one question: can forcing a parameterized family
representation to form online preserve a useful direction for a sibling
that does not yet exist? Save every historical task solution during that
same run. If yes, H39 gets a genuinely earned second life. If no, stop
trying to extract linear schemas from these residuals and move toward a
richer learned parameterized-operator representation.
