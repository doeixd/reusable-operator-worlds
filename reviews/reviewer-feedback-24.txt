# Audit the reuse procedure very carefully

This is probably the most important technical audit before sealed confirmation.

You say:

> “at a fixed point in its own examples a task may select any existing abstraction or none.”

### 4. Selection/search isn't being smuggled into description

# There is one surprising result I would investigate

The two-part gain is enormous:

[
\sim57,000\text{ nats}.
]

But prediction also improves:

[
\sim982\text{ nats}.
]

Normally compression introduces some fidelity tradeoff.

Here you get:

[
\text{smaller AND better}.
]

Why?

My leading hypothesis is that promotion is acting as a **regularizer**:

[
\text{many noisy private residuals}
\rightarrow
\text{better-estimated shared family function}.
]

Pooling across tasks gives you a better estimator.

That's important because abstraction is doing two things:

### Compression

[
D\downarrow.
]

### Statistical denoising

[
L\downarrow.
]

This could become a central theoretical result:

[
\boxed{
\text{abstraction pays twice:
once through amortized description,
and once through improved estimation from pooled evidence.}
}
]

You can test this.

Compare the promoted family function against individual residual functions on a large oracle evaluation set.

If the promoted (A) is actually **closer to the teacher family function** than the median private residual, promotion is denoising repeated estimates.

That would explain the simultaneous improvement elegantly.
