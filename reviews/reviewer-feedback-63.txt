Reviewer feedback 63 — received 2026-08-22, on H39_NEXT_STEPS_PLAN.md
(Stage A frozen; Stages B and C as designs).

----------------------------------------------------------------------

The sequencing is good: audit the interpretation -> isolate membership
discovery -> only then cardinality. Keep Stage A, make two interpretive
cautions, and make more substantial changes to B/C before they are frozen.

STAGE A. Good, but be precise about what A1 can establish. M's learned W
is itself part of the finished model; the post-hoc census intentionally
excludes that online-created schema and asks whether the realized
task-object population (private residuals / promoted objects) contains
coordinates from which the same opportunity can be mined. If A1 passes,
the strongest defensible statement is: useful coordinates were not
recoverable from the final extensional task-object population; they had
to be maintained in an explicit intensional channel during learning. Do
not say "nothing useful can be mined from the finished M learner" — W is
sitting in it and is useful. The distinction is: mine coordinates from
realized solutions versus preserve a parameterization while solutions are
formed. That is arguably an even better claim.

A2 has one possible weakness: if it passes you want to conclude
representational non-substitutability, not merely that the standard
optimizer could not find compensation. Given H37, the compensation fit
should satisfy the operational-envelope discipline: abundant support,
convergence plateau, preferably a second optimizer or restart robustness
if the result is close to 1.25. A compensated ratio near 2x is no
problem; at 1.28 I would be much less comfortable on one finite
optimizer. Since A is frozen, do not rewrite it unless the protocol lacks
a convergence guard; interpret a marginal pass cautiously.

STAGE B is the right next question, and there is an important conceptual
fact: M has already demonstrated discovery of membership well enough to
work. So H47 is not "can membership be discovered" but "what economic
price do we pay for discovering membership rather than being told it".

Tighten L. A +4 correct-family bias with frozen logits is not necessarily
a pure membership oracle; depending on the other logits it may not enforce
membership and its initialization could matter. Define L semantically:
p(designated parameterized slot) = 1 at the family step, or an explicit
route mask; keep all other trainable components identical; verify
mechanically that the correct-slot fraction is 1.000 for all trained
family tasks. No magic +4.

Keep teacher ARI non-load-bearing. If M reaches ARI 0.6 with J_M ~ J_L and
R_alpha,M ~ R_alpha,L, the conclusion is "a different but equally useful
membership structure", not "discovery failed".

Add one control to the hard-commitment question eventually: H_early vs
H_late, both ending equally hard, H_late committing only after much more
evidence. Prediction H_early < H_late ~ M: discreteness is not expensive;
committing before sufficient evidence is. Not needed for the first
three-world run, but do not interpret M > H as proving premature
commitment without it.

Tolerances should be relative where possible (e.g. |J_M - J_L| normalized
by |J_L - J_O|); absolute ones become historical accidents as world size
and family count vary. For worlds 0-2 the proposed thresholds are fine as
provisional gates.

STAGE C has a more important issue: do not freeze C as sketched. Before
implementing cardinality discovery, run a fixed-cardinality opportunity
census (the V4 lesson): for every regime evaluate fixed K in {1,2,4,6,...}
under the same coding currency and determine K*_oracle = argmin_K J(K).
Only after an economic optimum is known should you ask whether a learner
discovers it. The current scoring assumes K* = F, but H39 itself warns
against that: four teacher families were mapped onto two parameterized
slots ({0,1} -> S1, {2,3} -> S2), so the number of observed families is
already not the number of useful schema objects. Optimum cardinality is
an economic property, not a teacher-count property. Stage C should ask
K_discovered ~ K*_economic, not K_discovered = F_teacher.

Ceiling issue: K_max = 6 with F in {1,2,4,8} — if fixed-correct ever means
one slot per family, F = 8 cannot be represented by six slots. Raise
K_max >= 8, change the grid, or define explicitly why F = 8 does not imply
eight candidate objects.

Charge the whole marginal representation cost: activating a schema also
creates references, routing complexity, argument code, presence metadata.
The marginal decision should approximate Delta D* = D*(S_k) +
D*(references) + D*(alpha channel) + D*(routing overhead) minus whatever
disappears elsewhere; otherwise the learner over-activates because
schemas are underpriced. V5 makes this quantitative.

Preferred Stage C ladder: C0 opportunity census (fixed K per F, matched
D*; if K*(F) does not change across F, stop — there is no cardinality
problem); C1 oracle cardinality (K* given, membership learned: the
ceiling); C2 discover cardinality (K_max > K*, sleep birth/retire; ask
J_discover ~ J_K* and K_discover ~ K*); C3 generalization of the economic
law (vary recurrence/evidence; K_discover should change when
N_k s_bar_k > D*(S_k) changes — much stronger than recovering a teacher
count).

PREDICTIONS FOR B: J_M ~ J_L and R_full,M ~ R_full,L; the largest
discovery tax in R_alpha, because imperfect membership can be repaired
through eps, which helps full-interface but not alpha-only acquisition —
"innovation buffers imperfect abstraction discovery", another reason E4
matters. M > H; stronger prediction H_early < M ~ H_late, showing soft
routing buys time to gather evidence.

PREDICTIONS FOR C: substantially harder than membership. Overbirth
(K_discovered > K*: local specialization gives immediate gains while
schema cost amortizes slowly) or underbirth (existing schemas stretch to
absorb heterogeneous families); both pressures have appeared in ROW.
Guess: overbirth early, consolidation pressure required to shrink — the
successful mechanism may be soft overcomplete WAKE -> economic
consolidation during SLEEP, a satisfying reappearance of the original
architecture.

If B + C work: the learner autonomously determines which experiences
belong to a reusable parameterized primitive, how many primitives, their
directions, their arguments, and when private innovation is still
necessary — a learned vocabulary L = {P_1(alpha), ..., P_K(alpha)}.
Composition P_i o P_j o P_k is then the natural next question and program
synthesis becomes inferring (i, alpha_i), (j, alpha_j), (k, alpha_k) from
examples.

RECOMMENDATION. Run Stage A as frozen, with narrower A1 wording and a
cautious reading of a marginal A2. Freeze Stage B after making L an exact
membership oracle, keeping ARI diagnostic, and not calling M > H proof of
premature commitment without the late-hardening control. Redesign Stage C
before freezing: census fixed cardinalities first -> identify K*_economic
-> then test whether the learner discovers K*. The grand thesis is about
a learner discovering the economically useful language, not
reconstructing the hidden ontology used to generate the world.
