My call is to **change the substrate**, but I would sharpen H53 considerably. I would
*not* merely add more soft provisional slots. M already taught us that overcomplete
soft capacity can collapse into one distributed channel.

The next hypothesis should be:

    Useful alternative organizations must COEXIST DURING FORMATION, not merely
    remain reconstructible afterward.

H51 has now earned that hypothesis.

## What H51 actually killed

We have now tried three increasingly generous post-formation stories:

    R_0:   ordinary state
    R_1b:  retained/recombinable provenance
    R_2:   explicitly decomposable state

None gave C_restructure < infinity. And none recovered anything like the L_4
separation.

The especially revealing result is R_1b: reacquisition cost down 18-38% while TRUE
discrimination ~ 0 and WRONG actually wins. So:

    useful retained information is not necessarily information about how that
    computation should be reorganized.

That's a strong separation between REUSE and ABSTRACTION DISCOVERY. Likewise R_2 says
simply storing learning in more decomposable pieces isn't enough.

So I would consider the following family substantially falsified:

> Solve normally, preserve enough pieces/history, then discover the right abstraction
> by rearranging them later.

At least on this substrate.

# What seems to matter instead

Compare M_4 and L_4. They don't differ primarily in what information exists at the
end. They differ in the LEARNING TRAJECTORY: M_4's computation develops in a
distributed organization; L_4's develops inside the group-conditioned organization
from the beginning. And only the latter develops LOO discrimination,
substitutability, and future fertility.

So the live hypothesis is now:

    some abstractions are DEVELOPMENTAL OBJECTS. Their usefulness arises from
    learning under them, not from applying them afterward.

That's much stronger than "path dependence" in the vague sense. The path
*constructs* the abstraction.

# So H53 shouldn't be "more components"

It should be MULTIPLE PROVISIONAL ORGANIZATIONS.

Instead of: WAKE one representation -> later consider TRUE/WRONG/RANDOM, maintain
R^(1), R^(2), ..., R^(H) representing several candidate organizations DURING THE SAME
LIFETIME. Conceptually: WAKE maintains a small version space over representational
organizations.

Each hypothesis gets enough candidate-specific state that its schema can develop
under that hypothesis from the moment evidence arrives. Then sleep doesn't have to
manufacture R -> R_h. It just evaluates already-developed alternatives:
{R^(h)} -> score -> retain/prune.

# The cheapest decisive experiment

Still use the G = 2, K = 4 world. Externally supply candidate organizations: TRUE,
WRONG-A, WRONG-B, RANDOM-A, RANDOM-B, SHAM/distributed. No learned proposer yet.

But instead of six separate lifetimes, train them CONCURRENTLY. Share whatever
genuinely organization-independent substrate can safely be shared, while giving each
candidate its own small schema/argument state: B = shared generic computation, and
S_h, alpha_h for structural hypothesis h. During wake B_t -> B_{t+1} once, while
candidate heads each receive their hypothesis-appropriate updates.

Then after the lifetime use only past-task statistics (C_LOO, D*, substitutability)
to choose a hypothesis. Future sibling performance remains a held-out diagnostic.

# The experiment is not asking whether TRUE can work

We know that. The key question becomes:

    Can formation under multiple candidate organizations be amortized enough that
    structural search is cheaper than retraining once per hypothesis?

That's new. Measure C_candidate -- the incremental compute/state cost of maintaining
another organization. Compare H * C_lifetime for literal retrain-and-select against
C_shared-wake + H * C_candidate. If C_candidate << C_lifetime while TRUE develops the
same retrospective signal as L_4, you've solved H50's fundamental problem in a useful
way.

# I would want four outcomes preregistered

A. PARALLEL FORMATION WORKS CHEAPLY. TRUE becomes distinguishable from every wrong
   hypothesis, perhaps recovering most of L_4's signal, while incremental head cost
   is small. Then maintaining representational optionality during wake works. Major
   result. Next step: learn to propose/prune hypotheses.

B. WORKS, BUT COSTS ~A LIFETIME PER HYPOTHESIS. The mechanism exists but
   C_search ~ H * C_lifetime -- retrain-and-select implemented concurrently.
   Scientifically useful, algorithmically disappointing. Next question: how much
   computation candidate organizations can share.

C. SHARING CAUSES HYPOTHESES TO COLLAPSE. TRUE/WRONG heads all become distributed and
   indistinguishable. Then organization-specific learning must extend deeper into the
   substrate. Becomes an architecture-localization experiment: shared depth up =>
   structural discrimination down.

D. EVEN INDEPENDENTLY MAINTAINED PROVISIONAL HYPOTHESES DON'T DISCRIMINATE. Then
   something is seriously inconsistent with the L_4 result, or candidate formation
   needs essentially the complete L_4 lifecycle. Honest conclusion: structural
   alternatives cost approximately full retraining on this substrate. And I'd stop
   pushing this particular neural architecture.

# There may be another phase transition here

Let s be how much state/compute candidate organizations share. s = 0 is separate
retraining; s = 1 is basically M plus post-hoc bookkeeping, which we've shown fails.
There may be a boundary s* such that s < s* allows distinct organizational hypotheses
to develop, while s > s* causes them to collapse into the same distributed solution.

    abstraction search has an AMORTIZATION FRONTIER: how much computation can
    alternative representations share before they cease being genuinely alternative?

That is much more informative than simply trying another module.

# Why this is relevant to program synthesis

This result pushes the project closer to SEARCH, not away from it. A program
synthesizer doesn't fully commit to P_1, compile all knowledge under P_1, then later
mutate the compiled system into P_2. It maintains multiple partial hypotheses -- beam
search / version space / particles -- and develops them as evidence arrives. H51 is
beginning to suggest the representation learner may need the same structure.
Representation level {R^(1)...R^(H)}; program level {P^(1)...P^(H)}. Evidence updates
them; bad hypotheses die; promising ones specialize; common pieces can be shared. That
makes representation learning and program synthesis look increasingly like instances
of the same algorithmic pattern.

# It also changes wake/sleep again

WAKE: solve the present while maintaining a bounded population of plausible
representational explanations H_t = {h_1, ..., h_k}.

SLEEP: evaluate accumulated evidence
Q(h) = -D*(R_h) - C_reacquire(R_h) - C_carry(h), then PRUNE / MERGE / FORK / PROMOTE.

So sleep may not chiefly invent structure from one finished representation. Instead:
WAKE GROWS POSSIBILITIES; SLEEP PERFORMS SELECTION AND CONSOLIDATION AMONG THEM. That
is a substantially different -- and I think better -- model.

# This also resembles biological/developmental learning more

> Once a representation has committed to one coordinate system, alternative
> factorizations may become extremely expensive.

A sensible learner therefore maintains uncertainty not just over VALUES, but over
REPRESENTATIONAL STRUCTURE. That means our old distinction REFERENCE / ARGUMENT /
INNOVATION may need another type: HYPOTHESIS / PROVISIONAL STRUCTURE, whose semantics
are: this computation is maintained because it represents a plausible future
organization, not because it is currently the single best explanation.

# My updated theoretical picture

A strong learner may need to optimize not just accuracy + compression + fertility +
reorganizability, but OPTION VALUE:

    V(R_t) = current utility + value of still-accessible representational futures.

H50/H51 are telling us that once an option is discarded, reconstructing it can be
extremely expensive. So sometimes the efficient strategy is to carry several cheap
possibilities forward until evidence resolves them. That's precisely what a good
search algorithm does.

## My recommendation

I would NOT run retrain-and-select as the main successor -- L_4 already supplies the
crucial oracle existence result. And I would NOT do another provenance/decomposition
bookkeeping variant. I'd draft one very specific H53:

    Can multiple candidate organizations be allowed to develop concurrently during
    wake, such that the correct one becomes retrospectively distinguishable at
    substantially less than one full-lifetime cost per candidate?

Start with externally supplied TRUE/WRONG/RANDOM hypotheses. No learned proposer.

If that works, the next rung is enormously clear: generate hypotheses -> maintain a
small beam -> update with experience -> prune during sleep. And that begins to look
like the architecture we'd eventually want for program synthesis and self-improving
computational-language discovery: not repeatedly rewriting one committed model, but
maintaining, testing, and consolidating competing computational organizations as it
learns.
