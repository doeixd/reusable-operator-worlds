Yes. After reading the actual V5 spec, I think it's in very good shape
methodologically. My suggestions are mostly about **where to spend the next
units of research effort**, and a few places where I think the current gating
could hide an interesting result.

The biggest one: **I would seriously consider prioritizing H28 ahead of
H22-H24.** The spec currently makes H28 "registered, not scheduled," and says
it is the natural redesign if H20 is blocked. To me, H28 is much closer to the
motivating research thesis than learned library-management policy. H22-H24 ask
whether the learner can choose/edit representations; H28 asks whether two
apparently different neural computations can be recognized as **the same
abstract computation under different coordinates**. That is exactly the problem
you expect in real LLMs.

More specifically, I'd suggest these changes/experiments:

1. **Split H20 into "factorization economics" and "can promotion supply the
   atoms."** Right now H20's balance gates require D*(A_f), per-use saving,
   behavioral contribution, and **promotion rate** all to remain within 10%
   across r_meta. I agree with the first three. I'm less convinced promotion
   rate belongs in the same hard gate. Promotion rate is a learner response to
   the structure you are manipulating. It may legitimately rise because the
   family became easier to discover.

   I'd run an exogenous-library version first:

       A_1, ..., A_M are supplied/frozen as learned atoms

   and ask only:

       COMPRESS individually   vs   FACTORIZE into schema.

   That tells you whether the **representation class actually has an economic
   region**. Then separately ask whether the existing promoter can get you into
   that region.

   Otherwise H20 can fail because:

   > factorization is worthwhile, but the upstream abstraction-birth mechanism
   > changes with r_meta.

   That's a very different failure from:

   > higher-order factorization isn't worthwhile.

   I would want those separated.

2. **Turn H27 into a causal decomposition, not just a correlational mechanism
   study.** H27 is potentially one of the most interesting results in V5:
   shared abstractions cost ~3.9 bits/scalar while private residuals cost ~5.0,
   and the spec asks whether this comes from purification, effective dimension,
   selection, or restructuring. But the spec itself notes that noise
   purification and restructuring may be impossible to distinguish from frozen
   artifacts alone.

   There is a nice intervention that could separate them. For exactly the same
   cluster of private residuals, compare:

       P_0:  the private residuals before promotion
       P_1:  one functionally fitted shared residual, no further training
       P_2:  that same shared residual after post-promotion SGD

   Also compare selected residual clusters against equally sized residual
   clusters that PROMOTE rejected.

   Then you get:

       P_0(selected) < P_0(unselected)  in compressibility -> SELECTION effect
       D*(P_1) < D*(P_0)                -> PURIFICATION / consolidation effect
       D*(P_2) < D*(P_1)                -> REPRESENTATION RESTRUCTURING caused
                                           by continued learning

   That would be much stronger than a spectral correlation. If P_2 < P_1 < P_0,
   you could legitimately say abstraction formation changes the coding geometry
   of computation.

3. **I would not make H28 logically dependent on H20 succeeding.** The current
   spec says H28 is gated behind H20 producing a factorization class, but also
   says H28 is the natural redesign if H20 is blocked. Those two ideas pull in
   opposite directions.

   H20 asks roughly A_i = S(alpha_i) + eps_i in common coordinates.
   H28 asks f_i = d_i . A . e_i in different coordinates.

   H28 can pay even if H20 does not. In fact that's partly the point. So I would
   make the branch:

       H20 pays
          v
       same-coordinate higher-order abstraction exists

       H20 does not pay
          v
       try H28
          v
       perhaps abstraction only exists modulo coordinate change

   That is scientifically very interesting either way.

4. **Strengthen the compositional-closure test before V6.** The current probe
   passed: depth-8/depth-3 median NMSE ratios were ~4.1-4.4 and there was no
   saturation. That's enough as a "not obviously broken" gate, but I wouldn't
   treat it as evidence that the learned operators are truly closed under
   composition.

   In V6 I'd measure E(L) for composition depths L = 1,2,3,4,5,6,8,12,16. Then
   ask what law describes error propagation: E(L) ~ L versus E(L) ~ e^{cL}.

   Also measure error **after every individual call**. That tells you whether
   one operator is systematically pushing the state off the distribution on
   which later operators were learned.

   This could expose an important new concept: **interface stability**. Two
   morphisms may type-check mathematically, A: X->X and B: X->X, while
   nevertheless being poor neural compositional partners because A(x) falls
   outside the representational distribution B was trained on.

   That issue is going to become enormous once you leave synthetic (d=16)
   worlds.

5. **Make V6's first macro experiment distinguish three meanings of "macro."**
   I would not simply compare "ABC" against a macro M. Use at least:

       M_alias     = C . B . A, storing no new neural parameters and merely
                     naming the composition
       M_compiled ~= C . B . A, distilling the composition into a new learned
                     circuit
       Q           = a matched-capacity arbitrary new primitive

   That separates naming/compression vs search-space reduction vs execution
   compilation vs extra neural capacity.

   The spec already correctly requires macros to beat simpler compressed
   alternatives and a matched-budget search-space control. I would build the V6
   experiment around that distinction.

6. **In H21, add one systematic-generalization holdout.** Currently H21 holds
   out a whole abstraction and asks whether learning only alpha_new is cheaper
   than learning a whole operator, which is exactly the right test. I would add
   a harder held-out member whose parameter combination is deliberately novel.

   For example, if the schema has two latent directions A(alpha_1, alpha_2),
   train family members occupying (+,+), (+,-), (-,+) and hold out (-,-), or
   otherwise create a combination not observed during schema fitting.

   Then success can't be explained quite as easily as interpolation among
   nearby atoms. It starts to look like the learner has captured the
   **generative structure of a family of computations**.

7. **Decompose H22-online failures into valuation failure versus forecasting
   failure.** The spec already separates H22-oracle from H22-online, which is
   excellent. But if online fails, you will want to know why.

   Record separately: H_R_hat - H_R, s_hat - s, dD_hat - dD, and finally
   dJ_hat - dJ.

   That turns a failed selector into useful science:

   > the economic objective was right but recurrence forecasting failed,

   versus

   > recurrence was predicted correctly but the representation-value model was
   > wrong.

   Those imply completely different next steps.

8. **Be willing to end V5 earlier than the spec's maximum scope.** This is
   probably my strongest project-management suggestion. V5.0-V5.3 + H27 could
   already be an excellent coherent block:

       real code currency -> causal amortization -> higher-order phase boundary
       -> prospective schema transfer -> mechanism of compression

   H22-H24 - selection, rollout planning, learned restructuring policy - are
   interesting, but they form almost another research project. The spec itself
   already says H19/H20 are the only rungs presently deserving sealed
   confirmation and that a learned policy isn't required for V5 to count.

   If H20/H21 work, I might be more excited to spend the next research budget on
   **H28 and V6 composition** than on training q_phi to manage the library.

The overall sequence I'd favor now is:

    finish H19 repairs
      v
    H20: does higher-order representation have an economic region?
      v
    H21: does it transfer prospectively?
      v
    H27: why does abstraction change code efficiency?
      v
    H28: same computation modulo representation
      v
    V6: compositions become reusable morphisms

with H22-H24 available as a side branch if representation-management itself
becomes important.

The reason I like that ordering is that it keeps marching toward the original
scientific question rather than getting trapped optimizing the management system
around the current toy library. V5 has already produced an interesting
surprise - **cost and utility co-vary, so abstraction value is C/s, not simply
size**. The next surprises I'd go hunting for are whether **abstractions
themselves form reusable families**, whether **abstract computation survives
coordinate changes**, and whether **compositions recursively become new
primitives**.

Those three results, if they work, would make the project feel like one
continuous attack on the composition/efficient-scaling hypothesis rather than a
sequence of increasingly elaborate library-management experiments.
