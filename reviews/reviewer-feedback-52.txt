Yes. I think V6 is potentially the most important version yet, because it asks
whether we can move from "we know the economics of good representations" to "we
can cause the learner to form good representations."

My central hypothesis would be:

    Ordinary task loss underdetermines representation. Prospective learning
    pressure breaks that degeneracy in favor of representations that make
    related future learning cheaper.

But there are several ways we could fool ourselves.

## The main problems I would expect

1. FUTURE-TASK CHEATING RATHER THAN FERTILITY. In the oracle experiment, if we
   tell the learner which sibling belongs to which family, it could encode
   family/task IDs or particulars of that sibling rather than discovering
   reusable structure. Fix: strict support/query split, random opaque family
   relabeling, multiple possible future siblings, and ultimately evaluation on
   family members and worlds never used for the prospective gradient.

2. GENERIC PLASTICITY MASQUERADING AS STRUCTURE. Maybe prospective training just
   makes the network easier to fine-tune on anything. Useful, but a different
   claim. Always measure related AND unrelated future tasks. Report
   Phi_specific = Phi_related - Phi_unrelated. We want cheap adaptation
   specifically where shared computation exists, not universally larger learning
   rates or more unused capacity.

3. THE OPTIMIZER IMPROVES, NOT THE REPRESENTATION. A meta-gradient could learn
   curvature, step sizes, initialization. For V6.1, hold the adaptation
   algorithm FIXED at evaluation: take the two frozen representations and adapt
   both with exactly the same standardized optimizer. If the prospective
   representation still learns siblings more cheaply, the advantage lives in the
   representation.

4. TEACHER-GEOMETRY RECOVERY BECOMES ANOTHER FALSE TARGET. Make R_effective a
   mechanistic SECONDARY endpoint, not the primary pass. A prospective learner
   might find a representation unlike the teacher's yet superior for future
   learning. The primary result should be C_adapt(prospective) <
   C_adapt(standard) at matched current behavior and resource cost. Then ask
   what geometry caused it.

5. OVER-ALIGNMENT / COLLAPSE. Prospective pressure may force every task into one
   common subspace and destroy useful task-specific distinctions. Expect a
   tradeoff: shared structure up while private expressivity down. Monitor
   current loss, unrelated-task transfer, residual uniqueness, catastrophic
   interference. I'd predict a non-monotonic optimum rather than "more
   prospective pressure is always better."

6. SHORT-HORIZON MYOPIA. Optimizing for the next sibling might yield a
   representation excellent for T_{t+1} and poor for T_{t+5}. Train and evaluate
   across multiple future horizons; better, sample the horizon.

7. WRONG FUTURE DISTRIBUTION. Conceptually huge. Fertility only makes sense
   relative to plausible futures, Phi(R; p(T_future)). If future tasks are
   unrelated, paying to organize today's representation around a nonexistent
   family should be wasteful. V6 should eventually sweep the probability of
   recurrence.

8. PROSPECTIVE LOSS BECOMES AN ARBITRARY REGULARIZER. Avoid a free eta if
   possible. The clean version puts future adaptation loss in the same
   lifetime-prequential currency: J = L_now + L_future_after_adaptation +
   lambda D*(R). Then the relative pressure is economically interpretable rather
   than tuned until geometry appears.

9. CAPACITY CHEATING. The prospective learner may simply reserve more unused
   capacity. Compare at matched parameter budget and D*, and include a "larger
   ordinary learner" or extra-slots baseline.

10. ORDINARY MULTITASK TRAINING MAY ALREADY SOLVE IT. Important baseline.
    Compare against ordinary replay/joint training over related tasks. If simple
    joint training gives the same fertility, we learned something useful but
    don't need the fancy meta-objective.

11. META-OVERFITTING TO THE ROW GENERATOR. A meta-learned updater could memorize
    what ROW families look like. Eventually test on held-out generator families:
    different bases, ranks, family counts, coordinate transforms, perhaps a
    slightly different functional form.

12. COMPUTE / DIFFERENTIATING THROUGH LEARNING. Full meta-gradients through many
    updates may be expensive and unstable. Start with tiny exact unrolls. Only
    if the existence result works, explore truncated BPTT, first-order
    MAML/Reptile-like approximations, implicit gradients, or learned critics of
    future adaptability.

# The hypothesis ladder I would use

H30 — FERTILITY EXISTS. At matched current-task performance and D*, explicit
prospective pressure reduces adaptation cost on unseen related tasks:
Phi(R_prospective) > Phi(R_standard). This is the load-bearing claim.

H31 — FERTILITY IS STRUCTURALLY SPECIFIC. The gain is substantially larger on
related than unrelated futures: Phi_related >> Phi_unrelated. If both improve
equally, we've probably discovered generic plasticity instead of abstraction.

H32 — PROSPECTIVE PRESSURE RESTORES ACCESSIBLE HIGHER-ORDER STRUCTURE.
R_effective(prospective) > R_effective(ordinary) ~ 0.19. I'd predict a
meaningful increase but not necessarily anything close to 1; my guess is a
representation only partially teacher-aligned.

H33 — FERTILITY MAKES SCHEMA ECONOMICS REALIZABLE. The big bridge from V5, where
teacher FACTORIZE pays but learned FACTORIZE = 0/6. V6 predicts prospective
formation turns at least some cells into FACTORIZE > COMPRESS at matched
frontier coding. If H30 passes but H33 fails, prospective representations help
adaptation without producing an explicit higher-order library — still important,
but it weakens the recursive-language story.

H34 — THERE IS AN ECONOMIC THRESHOLD FOR PROSPECTIVE STRUCTURE. Let p =
Pr(future task belongs to a reusable family). Prospective organization has a
cost C_F and expected future saving p H s_bar, so predict p H s_bar > C_F before
fertile organization becomes worthwhile. Even learning a good way of looking at
things should obey the same amortization law. That would extend V5 another
level.

H35 — TOO MUCH FERTILITY PRESSURE HURTS. Expect a U-shaped/Pareto frontier: eta
= 0 gives good present fit and poor fertility; eta* gives both; eta >> eta*
gives an over-shared, inflexible representation. If this exists, the
representation economy is genuinely balancing specialization against future
option value.

H36 — META-LEARNED UPDATES REDISCOVER PROSPECTIVE ORGANIZATION. After oracle
prospective training works, remove family labels and train an updater across
lifetimes. On held-out worlds U_phi should form representations with greater Phi
than SGD despite never seeing future tasks at update time.

# A measurement I think could be extremely useful: the transfer kernel

Instead of asking only whether the learned geometry resembles the teacher,
construct K_ij = saving when adapting to task j after learning task i. This is a
functional transfer matrix. If tasks i, j truly share reusable computation, K_ij
should be high. A fertile representation should organize itself so that its
internal structure predicts this matrix. Ask whether some distance or
shared-subspace relation d_R(i,j) correlates with K_ij. That gives a much more
principled definition of representation geometry: RELATED TASKS SHOULD BE CLOSE
BECAUSE LEARNING ONE MAKES LEARNING THE OTHER CHEAPER — not because the teacher
assigned them the same label.

# In fact, maybe that is what an abstraction really is

V5's law suggests reuse is economically defined by saved future computation. So
perhaps the natural equivalence relation between tasks is not "same teacher
family" but "learning one materially reduces the cost of learning the other."
Then concepts/families are clusters in TRANSFER SPACE. That's much closer to the
ultimate project because we won't have teacher families in real data. You could
eventually discover abstractions from the transfer kernel itself.

# A really clean V6.1 design

Four arms. ORDINARY WAKE (current learner). REPLAY/JOINT-TRAINING BASELINE
(tests whether merely seeing related tasks produces the geometry).
ORACLE-PROSPECTIVE WAKE (future adaptation loss backpropagates into current
representation formation). SUPERVISED-FAMILY UPPER BOUND (explicitly encourage
same-family structure; not a candidate architecture, just tells us whether the
substrate can express the desired organization).

If even supervised-family fails: substrate problem. If supervised works but
prospective fails: objective/optimization problem. If prospective works but
replay doesn't: future adaptation pressure matters specifically. If replay works
just as well: simpler continual multitask learning was enough. All are useful.

# I would also use branching futures

Instead of one sibling, clone the learner after current training and adapt the
clones to several possible futures including an unrelated one. Prospective loss
is the expected cost across branches. This prevents overfitting to one specific
next task, and conceptually it is exactly the option-value story: which
representation leaves me well positioned across several plausible futures? Later
this maps onto dreaming — real future branches become model-generated
hypothetical branches.

# Dreaming should come only after the oracle version

Eventually reality won't hand us future siblings. Then generate hypothetical
continuations. Major danger: bad world model -> bad dreams -> representation
optimized for imaginary regularities. So have dreams PROPOSE a representation
but never VALIDATE it: past data -> infer possible regularities -> dream
plausible extensions -> choose representation with good option value -> future
reality measures whether that choice paid. Over many lifetimes the dream
generator itself can be calibrated by realized future usefulness.

# Another possibility: prospective pressure changes WHERE learning happens

Instrument this. Ordinary wake may learn task-family information across routes,
basis and residual in a messy distributed fashion. Prospective wake may discover
that the cheapest way to prepare for siblings is to move recurrent information
into a more explicit location. Measure D*_route, D*_basis, D*_innovation and
R_effective before and after. Perhaps fertile representations spontaneously
become more MODULAR — modularity emerging because modular information is easier
to reuse and adapt, rather than because we rewarded modularity.

# And I would not force explicit abstraction too early

If prospective wake gives Phi up sharply but R_effective ~ 0.2 and FACTORIZE
still doesn't pay, it found some other mechanism for making future learning
cheaper. Don't call that failure. Ask what changed: better initialization,
reusable features, a low-dimensional adaptation subspace, better conditioning.
That could tell us the library model is too restrictive. The grand objective is
to make future programs/learning cheap, not to force everything into the
abstraction hierarchy we imagined.

# What I personally predict

Oracle prospective pressure will work; I'd put reasonably high probability on
related future adaptation becoming substantially cheaper. R_effective should
improve but less dramatically than the economic endpoints — maybe 0.19 -> 0.4-0.6,
not 0.19 -> 1. Ordinary replay will recover some but not all of the effect. The
prospective learner should encode more family regularity in shared/effective
computation rather than private residuals. H33 is genuinely uncertain: it may
improve transfer without creating a clean schema that beats COMPRESS, which
would tell us meta-learning and explicit language formation are distinct steps.
I strongly expect a tradeoff with unrelated futures.

The most exciting possible V6 result isn't "prospective beats standard." It's a
phase diagram: specialize <-> preserve flexibility <-> form reusable abstraction,
depending on the expected economics of the future. That would extend the
representation-economics story from whether to keep an abstraction to how to
shape the representation before you even know exactly what abstraction will be
needed.
