# Reviewer feedback 01 — Reusable Operator Worlds

Scope: full review of the repository as of commit e6f25e3 plus the uncommitted
tuning work (`extended_diagnostics`, `--fast-tuning`, `tune_development.py`).
Context assumed: discovery is the primary goal, compute is extremely limited,
results may go to arXiv if significant, and the repository will be public.
Priorities below are ordered by information gained per unit of compute.

---

## 1. Overall assessment

The methodological hygiene is well above the norm for this literature:
predict-before-update prequential scoring, a passing positive control (oracle)
and a passing negative control (rho=0 endpoint flip), scratch-difficulty
validation before interpreting model differences, paired worlds, a
development/confirmatory seed firewall, symmetric tuning frozen before
confirmation, and full per-run provenance artifacts. The discrete result kept
as an informative decomposition (good library, expensive online route
inference) rather than buried is the right instinct.

The replicated effects (3/3 worlds, consistent signs, both the prequential and
novel-composition metrics) mean the main residual risk is no longer sampling
noise. It is confounds. Under limited compute, the plan should be rebalanced:
fewer replications, more controls. The items in Section 2 are the ones that
could overturn the headline conclusion, and they are all cheap.

## 2. Highest-priority scientific concerns

### 2.1 Teacher/learner functional-family inheritance (most important)

`ContinuousBasisLearner` and `DiscreteLibraryLearner` build their operators
from the same parametric family as the teacher primitive: rank-8 residual
tanh blocks — and they literally inherit the teacher's residual scale, since
`learned_lifetime._build_model` passes `alpha=config.world.alpha` into the
learner. The dense baseline gets a generic GELU MLP block. A skeptical reader's
first objection will be: "the reusable model wins because it is the teacher's
architecture, not because it reuses computation."

The rho=0 result is a genuine partial defense (same family, no recurrence,
advantage reverses), and it should be presented that way. But before any
public claim:

- Stop plumbing `world.alpha` into learners. Make alpha a learner
  hyperparameter (ideally learnable). Any shared constant between teacher
  config and learner config is a leak in a benchmark whose whole premise is
  that the learner knows nothing about the teacher.
- Run the family-mismatch controls already listed in the robustness queue,
  but promote them to FIRST, ahead of the tuning grid and the full rho sweep:
  (a) teacher rank 16 vs learner rank 8; (b) learner with a different
  activation or block form vs the same teacher. Seed 0 only is enough for a
  first read. If the continuous advantage survives mismatch, the reuse claim
  is strong; if it collapses, that is essential knowledge obtained for two
  runs.

### 2.2 Discrete temperature annealing is global, not per-task

`DiscreteLibraryLearner.set_training_progress` anneals temperature by global
example count. A task beginning at lifetime index 60 starts with a nearly hard
softmax: gradients to non-argmax slots are almost zero, so late tasks must
infer routes through a frozen relaxation. The reported "route inference is the
bottleneck" conclusion may therefore be an optimization-schedule artifact
rather than an inherent cost of discreteness. This matters because that
conclusion is carrying a lot of narrative weight (it is the stated reason not
to interpret discrete prequential loss as evidence against reusable
computation).

Cheap test (one run, seed 0): re-anneal temperature per task, over each task's
128 examples, against the mature shared library. Either outcome is
informative. If per-task annealing rescues discrete prequential loss, the
storage/inference/learning-cost triangle changes shape substantially.

### 2.3 Softmax mixing cannot express identity or attenuation

Continuous coefficients are a convex combination over 8 operators at every one
of the 3 steps: the model cannot skip a step, scale a step down, or realize an
effective depth other than 3. Adding an explicit identity operator to the
basis (or allowing unnormalized coefficients) is a one-line change and one run.
It also provides the cheapest possible first datapoint on the variable-depth /
loops question raised in `on-loops.txt`, without building any HALT machinery.
Recommendation on loops generally: hold the line the spec draws. The
recurrent-router-with-HALT executor bundles three new hard problems (variable
depth, learned halting, per-step route inference) before the V1 go/no-go gate
has been passed.

## 3. Compute-allocation advice

Given the discovery goal and the compute budget, ordered by information per
FLOP:

1. Family-mismatch controls (2.1) — two runs, seed 0.
2. Per-task discrete annealing (2.2) — one run, seed 0.
3. Identity-operator ablation (2.3) — one run, seed 0.
4. Sparse rho sweep: both endpoints already exist on world 0; add rho=0.5 and
   0.75 there (four runs total for both models) to get the shape of the
   headline curve before committing to the six-point multi-world version.
5. Shrink the tuning grid. The staged 36-run symmetric grid mostly purchases
   tuning-fairness optics. A defensible alternative: tune only task-code LR
   (the one hyperparameter demonstrated to matter) for both models on worlds
   0-1, freeze everything else at current values, and document the reduction
   in EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md. If the full grid is kept for publication optics,
   run it after items 1-4, since items 1-2 could invalidate the
   configurations being tuned.
6. The 30-world confirmatory ladder is publication armor, not discovery. Keep
   the seed firewall (it costs nothing), but do not spend confirmatory compute
   until the Section 2 controls have settled the interpretation.

Engineering item with better ROI than any experiment:
`forward_tasks` runs one forward pass per sample in a Python loop, and the
continuous/discrete forward evaluates all operator slots for every sample at
every step. Grouping the minibatch by task ID (current example plus same-task
replay hits) is roughly an hour of work and plausibly doubles or triples
effective compute. Under this budget that is worth more than any single run.

## 4. Presentation and reporting (for arXiv / public release)

- Normalize effect sizes. "Wins by 3,135 nats" is unreadable without the
  denominator (8,192 online examples x 16 dimensions). Report per-example nats
  (or bits via the quantized-target form, which already exists) alongside
  totals, and give deltas as a fraction of total prequential loss.
- Do not report bootstrap intervals over n=3 worlds; the interval is just the
  range of three numbers and will draw criticism. Show the three paired
  deltas directly and label the stage exploratory (PROGRESS.md already does
  this correctly — carry the same discipline into any write-up).
- Compute matching: the 6,528 vs 6,144 multiply-add match is inference-side
  only. Backprop through the continuous model touches all 8 operators at
  every step, so training compute is not matched at the same ratio. Either
  report training FLOPs separately or state explicitly that "compute-matched"
  means inference multiply-adds. Also state whether Dense-C width 32 was
  re-derived after the mixture-evaluation accounting or inherited from
  earlier parameter accounting.
- Replay deviation: the effective minibatch is 2 (1 current + 1 replay), not
  the spec's suggested 8. It is symmetric across models so the comparison
  stands, but document it in EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md before a reviewer finds it.
- The discrete zero-shot degradation over lifetime (0.103 -> 0.160, because
  tie-broken default routes become less generic as operators specialize) is a
  nice honest detail — keep it in the write-up; it preempts an obvious "why
  is zero-shot worse" question.
- For public release: `README.md` references the spec file but the repo will
  be read cold. Add a short top-level statement of the question, the headline
  metric, and the current status; link PROGRESS.md as the running lab record.
  Consider committing `on-loops.txt` and the reviewer-feedback files under a
  `notes/` or `reviews/` directory with one line of framing each, or exclude
  them — as loose root-level .txt files they will confuse readers.
- The provenance discipline (config/metrics/model/world-programs/git-commit
  per run) is a genuine strength; say so explicitly in the paper's
  reproducibility statement and publish the artifact directory layout.

## 5. Process observations

- The 54-minute commit history (11:15-12:09 on 2026-08-17) will be visible in
  a public repo. That is fine — arguably a feature — but the write-up should
  be transparent that this is agent-executed research with a human PI, since
  readers will notice.
- AGENTS.md as an accumulating "Implementation learnings" log is working
  extremely well and is the reason the repo reads as mature. It lacks one
  section: standing scientific doubts. Agents optimizing against PROGRESS.md
  milestones will sprint past unresolved concerns unless they are written
  where the loop reads them. Seed that section with: the family-inheritance
  confound (2.1), the global-anneal question (2.2), the convex-mixing
  constraint (2.3), the batch-size-2 deviation, and the training-vs-inference
  compute-matching caveat.
- Keep the rule that confirmatory seeds 100-129 remain uninspected. If the
  Section 2 controls change the model configurations, that firewall is what
  preserves the right to call the final numbers confirmatory.

---

## 6. Bottom line

The benchmark works, both controls behave, and the exploratory effects are
replicated and internally consistent. The result is not yet safe from the
"matched inductive bias, not reuse" objection, and the discrete story rests on
a possibly artifactual annealing schedule. Both can be resolved for about four
runs on seed 0. Do those before the tuning grid, the full rho sweep, or any
confirmatory spending. If the continuous advantage survives family mismatch
and the rho curve keeps its shape, this is a publishable, well-defended
result even at modest world counts.
