This is exactly the kind of V6 development sequence I was worried about, and the
good news is that the experiment is getting cleaner rather than merely more
complicated. The current status suggests three things very strongly.

First, H30 HAS NOT FAILED YET. The low-weight prospective intervention was
simply too weak to count as a meaningful causal test: a 54-nat change against
roughly 192k nats of lifetime loss is about 0.03%. You were right to distinguish
"no downstream effect" from "we actually changed the representation and
fertility did not improve." The weight sweep is therefore justified, especially
because H35 already registered that prospective pressure should have an optimum
rather than being monotone.

Second, REPLAY IS NOW A VERY SERIOUS BASELINE. It is the only arm positive in
all three worlds at the current operating point, with mean Phi ~ 0.072 and
relatively small dispersion. If that persists while stronger prospective
pressure fails, the scientific conclusion should probably become: ordinary
continual exposure to related tasks is sufficient to produce most available
fertility in this substrate — rather than trying to rescue the meta-objective.
That would still be useful, because it would tell us the missing V5 pressure was
not necessarily an explicit "future adaptability" objective; it may simply have
been continued shared-representation plasticity across related tasks.

Third, the frozen/unfrozen contrast has exposed a major architecture-level
interaction: freeze basis => M ~ 7 but prospective gradient dead; unfreeze basis
=> M ~ 2-5 but prospective gradient live. That is probably not a nuisance. It is
telling us that WHERE PLASTICITY IS ALLOWED DETERMINES WHERE RECURRENCE GETS
STORED.

## The next experiment I would prioritize

The partial-freeze experiment is the right next move, but I would treat it as
more than an H33 workaround. Sweep the number of trainable shared slots,
n_free in {0, 1, 2, 3, 6} or an equivalent grid. For each cell record M, Phi,
R_effective, J_COMPRESS - J_FACTORIZE, and perhaps the fraction of family
computation carried by basis, routes, and promoted atoms.

You may get a genuinely interesting phase diagram: plastic shared basis <->
explicit promoted library. Too much frozen capacity forces recurrence into
explicit objects. Too much trainable capacity lets the shared substrate absorb
everything continuously. Some intermediate point may be where prospective
gradients can reshape representation, enough discrete abstractions still form,
and higher-order factorization becomes testable. That could be much more
important than simply finding a configuration where H33 runs.

## I would preregister three possible outcomes

1. STRONGER PRESSURE IMPROVES Phi BEYOND REPLAY. Then H30 becomes interesting
   again. The strongest result would be Phi_prospective > Phi_replay > 0 across
   worlds with current-task performance and representation cost matched —
   evidence that seeing related experience helps but explicit future-learning
   pressure adds something above ordinary continual learning.
2. REPLAY REMAINS BEST. Then don't fight it. The next question becomes why
   replay forms more fertile representations, which points toward an
   optimization-path explanation rather than a special prospective objective:
   repeated gradients across related tasks may align shared computation enough
   to break representational degeneracy on their own. Elegant and simpler.
3. STRONG PRESSURE HURTS. Fairly plausible. Then H35 becomes the main result:
   Phi rises then falls with eta, because aggressive prospective training forces
   unrelated computations into an overly shared geometry. That gives the
   specialization-fertility frontier we expected.

# One thing I would change about the supervised upper bound

Your corrected supervised arm is better than the no-op, but I would be cautious
about calling it a SUBSTRATE UPPER BOUND. "Make sibling predictable from
relative's route without adaptation" is stronger and more specific than "can the
substrate represent family structure?" So if it fails, that does NOT mean the
substrate cannot support fertility. I would label it EXPLICIT-FAMILY-SHARING
PRESSURE instead.

A true architecture capability bound would be more direct: give family labels
and explicitly optimize a functional shared-subspace criterion,
min sum_f |F_f - S_hat(alpha_f)|^2, while retaining task performance. If even
that cannot raise R_effective, you really do have an architectural bottleneck.
But I wouldn't block the current work to implement it.

# The "novel family" choice deserves careful framing

The adaptation probe now evaluates a family unseen during the lifetime because
members of seen families were almost zero-shot already. That's useful, but it
changes the interpretation. Originally: does learning family f make another
member of f cheaper? Now: does the representation learned from some families
make a NEW FAMILY drawn from the same meta-distribution easier to acquire? That
is a more ambitious and arguably more interesting notion — META-FERTILITY rather
than within-family transfer.

Preserve both eventually: Phi_within for unseen members of seen families, and
Phi_meta for unseen families. The fact that Phi_within has ceiling effects is
itself informative: the unfrozen basis already generalizes so well within family
that there is little economic room left. That may be telling you the true useful
future is indeed the NEXT ABSTRACTION FAMILY, not the next member.

# This could change our interpretation of V5 too

V5 found higher-order teacher structure that learned promoted atoms did not
expose. But fully unfrozen learning gives M ~ 2-5 and seen-family held-out tasks
nearly solved zero-shot. That suggests some of the "missing explicit schema" may
not be missing computation at all — it may have been absorbed into a CONTINUOUS
SHARED SUBSTRATE.

This connects back to V1/V2: partial recurrence favors a continuous operator
manifold; exact recurrence favors explicit atoms. Perhaps V6 is rediscovering
the same phenomenon one level higher: a continuous shared representation may
absorb higher-order recurrence before discrete schema birth becomes economical.
That would explain why H33 and H30 naturally want different freeze regimes. If
true, it is not a tooling inconvenience. It is a representation phase
transition.

## That suggests a new measurement

For each freeze setting, measure where the family/meta-family signal lives:
R_basis, R_route, R_residual, R_promoted, R_effective. You may see: more plastic
basis => R_basis up, M down; more frozen basis => R_promoted up, M up. Then the
deeper question becomes not "did FACTORIZE appear?" but AT WHAT LEVEL DOES THE
LEARNER CHOOSE TO STORE RECURRENT STRUCTURE AS THE ECONOMICS AND PLASTICITY
CONSTRAINTS CHANGE? That is very much in the spirit of the whole project.

# I also like the stricter H30 criterion

Changing the scorer after seeing that a weak positive mean with huge variance
printed PASS was necessary, and recording the change was the right way to do it.
For development, use same-sign-in-every-world plus |mean Phi| > SD(Phi) as a
DEVELOPMENT GATE, not a final inferential statistic. For any eventual sealed
claim, freeze a proper criterion separately. The important lesson is that n = 3
is a diagnostic, not evidence by itself.

# My updated hypothesis probabilities

Replay gives meaningful fertility: high probability. Strong prospective pressure
beats replay: maybe 40-50%. Prospective pressure improves geometry but not
downstream fertility: fairly plausible. An intermediate partial-freeze sweet
spot where H33 becomes positive: maybe 50%. Fully unfrozen basis makes explicit
schema unnecessary because continuous sharing already solves the useful
transfer: also quite plausible.

That last one would be scientifically valuable. It could mean the hierarchy
isn't residual -> atom -> schema at every stage. It may be: continuous shared
manifold -> discrete atom only when recurrence becomes sufficiently
exact/economical -> higher-order schema only when the next level also crosses
its own amortization boundary. Which is remarkably consistent with V1-V5.

# So my immediate recommendation

Finish the registered weight sweep. Then, regardless of whether prospective
weight 10/100 works, run the PARTIAL-FREEZE SWEEP AS A REPRESENTATION-ALLOCATION
EXPERIMENT, not merely an H33 repair. Central table: free shared capacity vs
Phi, R_effective, M, FACTORIZE margin, current loss, for ordinary/replay/
prospective.

If a coherent frontier emerges, V6 may become less about "prospective objective
yes/no" and more about something deeper: PROSPECTIVE LEARNING PRESSURE AND
ARCHITECTURAL PLASTICITY JOINTLY DETERMINE THE LEVEL AT WHICH REUSABLE STRUCTURE
BECOMES EXPLICIT. That would unify several otherwise disconnected observations
from the entire project.

And keep the same discipline: each apparent null needs to survive a gradient
check, a dynamic-range check, a future-task validity check, and a non-vacuity
check before it gets to become a scientific result. The run log is basically a
case study in why that rule matters.
