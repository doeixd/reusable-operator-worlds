Reviewer feedback 62 — received 2026-08-22, on the sealed H39 confirmation
(`reports/h39_confirmation.json`, CONFIRMED under Amendment 1).

----------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a genuinely strong confirmation, and I agree with your
interpretation of what it licenses. The most important result is not the
borderline E2. It is the JOINT PATTERN: learned online parameterization ->
better present performance + better future full-interface performance +
causally used argument channel, across 30 sealed worlds. That is much
harder to explain away than any single ratio.

MY OUTSIDE READ. I would describe the result as: a parameterized
representation, when built jointly during learning, preserves useful
future variation that cannot be recovered by post-hoc factorization of the
finished residual population. That claim seems earned. The strongest
pieces are E1 (learned directions beat frozen random directions, so this
is not merely "two extra slots help"), E3 (every world improves present-
task cost, so the architecture did not purchase future flexibility by
sacrificing the present), E4 (the complete schema+innovation interface
beats ordinary future acquisition by about 18.5% in 28/30 worlds; this may
ultimately matter more than alpha-only), and E5 (zeroing alpha hurts
2.9-4.3x in all 30 worlds, so the representation really uses the
mechanism). Those four together establish existence and causal use very
convincingly.

E2 is real but should remain modestly stated: geomean 1.636 with CI ending
at 1.7997 against the frozen ceiling 1.8 — "confirmed at the registered
bound, not beyond it" is the right sentence. Do not write anything like
"alpha alone nearly recovers ordinary" without immediately giving 1.64x.
It preserves substantial future opportunity, not all of it. Recording the
original-rule verdict is excellent practice; the amendment was justified
before sealed data, and sealed data then demonstrated why the change
mattered, which strengthens the case for the amendment.

THE CENSUS + CONFIRMATION PAIRING is probably the deepest result: post hoc
R ~ 2.2-3.5 even at maximal span; online formation R ~ 1.64. Same broad
problem, radically different result depending on WHEN the coordinates are
created. Scientific phrasing: in this testbed, useful parameterized family
coordinates were substantially more recoverable when formed jointly with
task learning than when extracted afterward from the final learned
population; "made, not mined" as the shorthand. Consequence: abstraction
may not be reliably discoverable by train -> inspect finished network ->
extract latent program. The learning dynamics themselves may have to
maintain the degrees of freedom from which future abstractions can be
constructed.

DISCOVERY IS THE EARNED NEXT RUNG, in three separated problems, not one
giant learner.

D1 — Discover membership. Keep the number of parameterized slots fixed and
correct; remove family labels; can the learner discover which tasks share
a slot? Primary endpoints are the economic ones (J_present, R_alpha,
R_full); teacher membership recovery is diagnostic. Prediction: likely
solvable; soft routing with MDL/usefulness pressure should beat hard early
commitment (V2: the discrete tax came largely from premature commitment).

D2 — Discover cardinality. Give a larger candidate pool K_max and charge
for active parameterized objects; objective J = L_preq + lambda D*(active
schemas) + lambda D*(routes/arguments/innovation). Predict birth when
N s_bar_schema > D*(S). The interesting result is that the active count
tracks the economic recurrence conditions, not exact teacher count.
Controls: fixed too-few, fixed correct, fixed too-many, discovered.
Discovery wins only if it approaches the oracle architecture without
simply using all available capacity.

D3 — Discover representation type. Let the learner choose between an
ordinary private object, a parameterized schema + argument, and private
innovation; does it autonomously decide which recurring computations
deserve parameterization? Do not start here; membership, then cardinality.

SAFEGUARD: do not reward teacher-looking grouping; reward economy and
future usefulness. A grouping that differs from the teacher but yields
J_present <= J_oracle and C_future <= C_oracle is a valid discovery. ARI /
NMI / route recovery are secondary. Functional/economic equivalence before
ontology recovery.

GRANULARITY: is the useful decomposition really 2 schemas x arguments, or
just the architecture supplied? A developmental sweep with F_schema in
{1,2,4,8} at constant support per schema, asking whether discovered object
count tracks F, directly tests whether the mechanism scales.

PREDICTIONS FOR DISCOVERY: membership probably works moderately well
(economics near oracle even with imperfect teacher recovery); cardinality
harder (too many objects because local specialization is easy, or too few
because a flexible schema absorbs several families — both pressures have
appeared before; the description-length charge should matter a lot);
fully autonomous type discovery much harder, probably requiring a
sleep/consolidation step rather than pure online gradient descent (WAKE:
solve with soft structure; SLEEP: decide which recurring variation
deserves a named parameterized object).

PATH TO PROGRAM SYNTHESIS: P(alpha) with a causally used argument channel
is much closer to a language primitive than an opaque promoted residual.
Parameterized primitive exists -> discover which tasks share it ->
discover how many -> P_i(alpha_i) compose -> infer routes + arguments for
novel programs -> program synthesis; the synthesizer searches over
(primitive identity, argument) rather than thousands of opaque objects.
Concrete composition experiment after discovery: train on some
combinations P_1(a) o P_2(b), P_1(c) o P_3(d), test held-out crosses
P_1(a) o P_3(d) and novel arguments P_2(b') o P_3(d'), decomposing
generalization into primitive identity reuse, argument reuse, new
composition, new argument + new composition. The grand prize: infer the
correct primitive sequence and arguments from a handful of examples.

PRESERVE E4: R_full = 0.815 means the best representation is not alpha
alone but schema + argument + innovation. Future "programs" probably have
a reusable skeleton + bound arguments + a small novel patch; a continuum
private solution -> schema + large innovation -> schema + small innovation
-> pure argument specialization, along which the learner may migrate as
recurrence accumulates — essentially a compilation pathway.

BEFORE DESIGNING THE DISCOVERY PLAN, get another outside read on the
confirmation report. Points to attack: (1) is the post-hoc census versus
online formation comparison apples-to-apples enough to support "formed
online"? (2) does E3 accidentally benefit from a capacity or optimization-
budget difference between O and M? (3) does E4's full-interface gain
survive a matched total task-local parameter/bit budget? (4) is the
alpha-zero ablation the correct causal-use test, or can other pathways
compensate asymmetrically? (5) is the amended E2 verdict defensible and
sufficiently transparent? (6) does any part of the result rely on oracle
family grouping strongly enough that "parameterized representation" should
be qualified as supervised/oracle-structured? Unless one reveals a serious
confound, the discovery stage is earned.

The big conceptual result: useful abstraction is not merely the discovery
of structure already latent in finished solutions; learning can create a
coordinate system in which future variation becomes expressible. That is
exactly the kind of phenomenon the eventual self-improving computational
language needs.
