----------------------------------------------------------------------

We just learned something fairly central to the whole program: the
useful abstraction is not sitting inside the finished collection of task
solutions waiting to be extracted. The learner has to create and maintain
the right coordinate system while it is learning. That is much stronger
than the original "reuse is valuable" result. The sequence now looks
like: recurring computation exists -> sharing is economically valuable ->
post-hoc solutions do not expose it well -> ONLINE LEARNING CAN CREATE A
USEFUL PARAMETERIZED REPRESENTATION. Stage A makes the last step much
harder to explain away.

WHAT WE LEARNED EMPIRICALLY. R_alpha,online = 1.64x versus post-hoc
reconstruction R_census,O = 2.59x and, strikingly, R_census,M = 6.33x.
The 6.33x is especially revealing: once the learner has an explicit
parameterized channel W alpha, the reusable family information gets
routed into that channel; the private residual population becomes LESS,
not more, suitable for reconstructing the family afterwards. The
architecture induces a division of labour: W, reusable family structure;
alpha, task-specific variation within that structure; eps, genuinely
private innovation. That is very close to the semantic decomposition we
have been trying to get to for a long time. And A2 says this is not
cosmetic: removing alpha and giving route + private residual 2,000
updates to compensate costs ~2.01x. Alpha is used and operationally
non-substitutable by the other task-local channels. That makes H39 much
more than a probing result.

THE DEEPER FINDING: INTENSIONAL VERSUS EXTENSIONAL REPRESENTATION. An
extensional representation stores the solutions actually encountered,
r_1 ... r_n, and hopes to discover a common abstraction afterwards — what
the census tries. An intensional representation stores P(alpha), a rule
describing an entire family of possible computations; observed tasks
instantiate P(alpha_1), P(alpha_2), ... but the representation also
provides coordinates for P(alpha_new) that has never occurred. That is
exactly the capability H39 is beginning to demonstrate. Compression of
observed objects and learning a generative coordinate system are
different things; the second is much closer to abstraction in the sense
we ultimately care about.

THIS RESOLVES AN AMBIGUITY FROM V5 AND V6. V5: the learner allocated
recurring information correctly but the higher-order structure could not
be recovered afterwards; we wondered whether the extraction method was
wrong; H29 showed much of it was not even in the effective span. V6
tried to force the right representation by training for future
adaptation; that hurt. H37 showed it was not just bad optimization
(0.018 -> 0.030 under high-budget fitting). Now H39 says the idea that
representation formation matters was right, but the naive prospective
objective was the wrong mechanism. The successful intervention was not
"care more about the future"; it was architectural: give recurring
structure, variation, and innovation different representational roles.

IT CHANGES OUR CONCEPTION OF ABSTRACTION. The MDL story — create A if
repeated savings exceed D*(A) — remains correct economically but is
incomplete. A good abstraction also needs an internal parameterization
of variation: not A = one reusable function but A(alpha), with a cheap
escape hatch + eps. A better definition: an abstraction compresses what
is invariant while exposing useful coordinates for what may vary. This
explains V6: one can increase measured common structure while making the
coordinates for variation worse.

DIRECTLY RELATED TO PROGRAM SYNTHESIS. The eventual learned language
should not consist mostly of opaque neural macros P_1, P_2, ... but of
parameterized primitives P_i(alpha), with composition P_3(alpha_3) o
P_2(alpha_2) o P_1(alpha_1). Otherwise every new variant requires a new
primitive or a patch to an opaque one; with parameterized primitives
adaptation becomes identify primitive + infer argument — a dramatically
nicer synthesis problem. Rotate(37 deg) is a better primitive than
Rotate37. H39 is essentially a neural demonstration of why.

EPS MATTERS TOO. R_full = 0.815 while R_alpha = 1.64 means the right
system is not "everything must fit the abstraction" but reusable skeleton
+ bound argument + optional novel patch — P(alpha) + Delta P, or
macro(alpha) + a small local subprogram. New knowledge need not be
completely known or completely new; it can be mostly an instance of
something known plus a small innovation.

IT VINDICATES WAKE/SLEEP MORE PRECISELY. Pure post-hoc sleep may be too
weak: if wake solves each task in arbitrary private coordinates, sleep
may not have enough structure to reconstruct the abstraction. The
architecture probably needs interaction between timescales — WAKE: solve
while maintaining candidate shared coordinates; SLEEP: decide which
coordinates deserve persistent objects; WAKE: use those objects to
structure subsequent solutions — rather than solve arbitrarily first and
compress later. Representation formation has to be prospective in
ARCHITECTURE even though the explicit V6 prospective loss was harmful.

WHAT REMAINS MISSING. Existence and use are demonstrated; discovery is
not. We supplied the form (two parameterized slots, K = 32, argument
matrices, private innovation channel); the learner learned the directions
and used them, which is substantial. A fully autonomous learner must
answer: should there be an abstraction here at all; which tasks belong to
it; how many should exist; which dimensions deserve to become arguments;
when should private innovation become another reusable primitive. Those
are the next research problems. [See annotation above regarding the
"M already discovers membership" paragraph.] Roadmap: reuse economics ->
online parameterized abstraction -> membership discovery -> cardinality
discovery -> parameterized primitive vocabulary -> composition -> program
synthesis -> language improvement.

THE BIGGEST THEORETICAL UPDATE. Originally: intelligence progressively
compresses recurring computation into reusable abstractions. Now:
INTELLIGENCE LEARNS PARAMETERIZED COMPUTATIONAL REPRESENTATIONS THAT
COMPRESS WHAT RECURS WHILE PRESERVING CHEAP COORDINATES FOR NOVELTY. Then
reasoning / program synthesis searches over those coordinates and their
compositions. And eventually: learning improves not only what the system
knows, but the language in which future learning and reasoning take
place. H39 is the first result in the project that looks distinctly like
evidence for that latter idea rather than merely for parameter sharing or
compression. The system did not just store reusable computation more
cheaply. It created a new representational degree of freedom — alpha —
that changed how future computations could be learned. That is very close
to the phenomenon we have been trying to isolate from the beginning.
