## 1. A retraction, on the record

Reviewer-feedback-05's author (this reviewer) previously flagged the transfer
dissociation — "abstraction quality improves before it pays for itself"
(learnings.txt s4) — as the most novel empirical nugget and a headline-figure
candidate. It failed replication: at rho = 0.25/0.5/0.75, Continuous wins
novel transfer on only one of three worlds and its mean novel NMSE is worse
at all three points. The retraction is recorded here so the write-up cannot
inherit the earlier enthusiasm by accident.

Two consequences:
- Adopt the simpler picture: weak recurrence -> sharing is harmful bias;
  strong recurrence -> sharing is useful inductive bias. One crossover
  affecting both lifetime cost and transfer, not a three-regime story.
- The rho=1 checkpoint learning-to-learn result (32-shot novel NMSE gain
  ~6.4x vs ~2.3x for Dense-C) is ALSO still primarily a world-0 result and
  now deserves proportionate suspicion until replicated. Both this reviewer
  and reviewer-02 were seduced once by a world-0-only effect; do not let it
  happen twice. Prioritize checkpoint replication across worlds 1-2 before
  citing that ratio anywhere public.

The falsification itself is an asset: state it explicitly in the report
("the proposed early-transfer intermediate regime was not supported"), as
notes/crossover.txt recommends. It will buy reviewer trust.

## 2. The crossover result is strong; two analyses will make it stronger

Crossings at configured rho of 0.811 / 0.852 / 0.851 across three
independently generated worlds, with unanimous sign patterns on both sides,
is not architecture noise. Endorsed as-is: "crossover," not "phase
transition"; interpolation stays descriptive; expand to worlds 3-9 with
frozen configurations before anything new.

### 2.1 Re-coordinate the crossover in measured recurrence space (free)

Per notes/crossover.txt: interpolate each world's crossing in measured
functional recurrence r rather than configured rho. The diagnostics already
exist per run; this is pure analysis. If r*_W aligns more tightly than
rho*_W across worlds despite world-to-world variation in the rho -> r
mapping, the claim upgrades from "our knob has a crossover" toward "an
invariant of the learning problem." Report both coordinates regardless.

### 2.2 Crossover vs lifetime length (the decisive mechanism test, ~free)

The amortization account N * s(r) > C makes a falsifiable prediction the
static bias-variance account does not: the crossover must MOVE with lifetime
length. More tasks to amortize against -> rho* declines with N.

Half of this test costs nothing: prequential loss is logged per example, so
the Dense-minus-Continuous cumulative difference can be computed at
truncated lifetimes (t = 2048, 4096, 8192) from EXISTING runs, yielding
rho*(N) at three N values per world with zero new compute. If rho*(N)
declines monotonically, "amortization threshold" graduates from
interpretation to demonstrated mechanism — the strongest figure the paper
could have. If it does not move, that is equally important: the crossover is
then about representational bias, not amortization, and the theory section
changes.

Caveat to note in the analysis: truncated-lifetime curves reuse the same
online trajectory (shared parameters at t=2048 have seen only 16 tasks), so
this is a valid reading of "a lifetime that ended at N" — it is not an
approximation. Confirmatory-grade versions can later run genuinely shorter
and longer lifetimes (e.g. 32 and 128 tasks) at two bracketing rho values.

### 2.3 Optional grid refinement

All three crossings land between rho = 0.75 and 0.9 — the widest gap in the
grid. If worlds 3-9 reproduce the pattern, two added points (0.80, 0.85) at
those seeds only would localize rho* properly. Do not spend this before the
3-9 expansion confirms the sign pattern.

## 3. Unify the GELU result with the crossover (one cheap prediction)

The two main findings — family-mismatch shrinks the advantage (~3,100 ->
~441 nats), and a recurrence crossover exists — may be one finding. If
family mismatch raises the effective cost C of acquiring/using the shared
representation, the amortization model predicts the GELU learner's crossover
shifts RIGHT (more recurrence needed to pay off) rather than the advantage
simply vanishing. Test: run the GELU-operator continuous learner at the two
bracketing rho points (0.75, 0.9), worlds 0-2 — six runs. If the crossover
exists but displaced, prior quality becomes just another term in the same
economics, and the paper's two headline results fold into one framework.
That unification is worth six runs.

## 4. Phase II update: promote the mixed-rho world above Benchmark C

The heterogeneous-reuse world proposed in notes/crossover.txt — per-primitive
reuse levels within ONE world (e.g. rho_i = 1.0, .95, .8, .5, .2, 0) — is a
better bridge to dynamic restructuring than fork/merge under nonstationarity,
and it should replace Benchmark C as the first Phase II target
(amending reviewer-feedback-04 s1.2):

- It creates the situation where neither fixed architecture is correct and
  the learner must discover the FACTORIZATION STRUCTURE — the actual
  research question — in a static world.
- It needs almost no new machinery: a per-primitive rho vector in the
  existing generator, existing validity gates. No regime switching, no
  nonstationarity controls, no new statistical unit.
- It gives copy-on-write / residual specialization (spec Model 5) a concrete
  measurable job: residuals should concentrate on the low-rho primitives.
  That is a sharp, falsifiable signature of selective sharing.
- The motivation statement is now empirical, not aesthetic: the experiments
  exhibit regimes where opposite structural biases are optimal; a learner
  that chooses sharing granularity per-computation has a demonstrated
  opportunity that neither fixed architecture can take.

Hierarchical recurrence (global + family + task deltas) is the natural
second step; fork/merge under nonstationarity moves to third, inheriting
whatever consolidation and descriptor machinery exists by then.

## 6. Bottom line

The project now has a replicated, quantitative crossover in preferred
representation as a function of latent recurrence, a falsified secondary
hypothesis honestly recorded, and a theory (amortization) that makes two
cheap, decisive predictions: the crossover tightens in measured-recurrence
coordinates, and it moves with lifetime length. Run the free analyses before
any new training. If both predictions hold, the paper's center of gravity is
no longer "a reusable architecture wins" but "the economics of when
abstraction pays" — with the crossover surface (recurrence x lifetime x
prior quality) as its object of study. That is a contribution no amount of
architecture benchmarking can reproduce.
