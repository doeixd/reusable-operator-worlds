# One subtle issue I'd check: what distribution is (A) being fit on?

Even with fixed primitive position, the residual at that step may receive different incoming hidden states across tasks.

So there are two notions of “same function”:

### Common-domain functional similarity

Evaluate every residual on the same probe distribution:

[
z\sim Z_{\rm common}.
]

This asks whether the operators themselves are equivalent.

### On-trajectory substitutability

Evaluate on states actually arriving at that step:

[
z\sim Z_\tau^{\rm trajectory}.
]

This asks whether substituting the abstraction preserves the computation people actually care about.

I would measure both.

A candidate could look different globally but be perfectly substitutable on the relevant manifold.

For PROMOTE, **substitutability should ultimately win**.

## What I expect the post-hoc functional fit to show

My prior would be that it improves **dramatically** over the parameter mean.

Why?

You already have:

* perfect family recovery;
* load-bearing residuals;
* a deliberately shared teacher primitive;
* fixed program position.

Those are exactly the circumstances where parameter non-identifiability can explain a disastrous arithmetic mean.

If the functional optimizer is given enough probes, I wouldn't be surprised by a jump from ~12% capture to something like **60–90%**.

If that happens, I'd consider the new-primitive/fixed-position world basically validated.

Then the V3 question becomes very clean:

[
\boxed{
\text{Can PROMOTE discover the functional representative that the oracle fit shows exists, pay for it once, replace repeated private residuals with references, and help future tasks?}
}
]

And if the functional fit **doesn't** make that jump, that's equally useful: you've learned that even a deliberately repeated primitive becomes represented as a *family of context-dependent neural implementations*, and V3 needs to promote a parameterized equivalence class rather than a fixed operator.

Either outcome moves the project forward.
