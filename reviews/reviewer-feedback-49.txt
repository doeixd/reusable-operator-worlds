Yes. H29 is a much better localization result than "PROMOTE fragments things." But
I would make one correction to the interpretation before building the adapter
experiment.

The conclusion I think you have earned is:

    the learner's RESIDUAL OBJECTS contain little of the teacher's meta-family
    geometry before PROMOTE.

You have NOT quite established:

    the learner as a whole has already lost 90% of that information.

That distinction matters because the wake learner has more places to encode a
family than the private residual.

## The missing possibility: the family is distributed

A task is being solved through something closer to

    shared basis + task-specific route + private residual.

The teacher says T_f(z) = B_teacher(z) + A_f(z). But the learner is free to
implement the same thing as T_tau(z) = sum_j r_tau_j P_j(z) + Delta_tau(z).

So two tasks sharing the same teacher family may have residuals Delta_1,
Delta_2 that look unrelated because the common computation has been distributed
differently between r_tau and Delta_tau.

If so, the R_pre = 0.095 result doesn't mean the family structure vanished. It
means THE RESIDUAL TENSOR IS THE WRONG COMPUTATIONAL UNIT to call "the task's
innovation." And that would be extremely consistent with lessons you've already
learned elsewhere in the project.

# I would therefore put one cheap audit before the adapter experiment

Construct the EFFECTIVE task-conditioned operator at the family step. For each
task tau, define functionally F_tau(z) = the actual learner transformation
applied at that step, including basis selection/mixture, route, residual, and
any adapters already involved. Use the correct on-trajectory state distribution,
as your latest probing error just reinforced.

Then extract the task-conditioned innovation relative to an appropriate
shared/default computation: I_tau(z) = F_tau(z) - F_0(z), where F_0 is the
learner with the relevant task-specific information nulled — not an arbitrary
parameter baseline.

Then run exactly H29's family-capture instrument on {I_tau} rather than only
{Delta_tau}. Call the result R_effective. Now you get a very informative fork.

## Outcome 1: R_effective is high

Suppose R_residual ~ 0.095 but R_effective ~ 0.7 or 0.9. Then the information
was never lost. It was DISTRIBUTED across the wake representation. And the V3
PROMOTE mechanism has been promoting the wrong thing. That would be a major
finding: abstraction boundaries should be discovered functionally, not inherited
from parameter boundaries. PROMOTE currently says "here is the private residual
object; maybe it deserves to become shared." The better operation would say
"here is a recurrent causal transformation, even though its implementation
currently spans routes, basis calls, and residual state — compile that
transformation into one reusable object." A compiler shouldn't care which
registers happened to implement a computation before compilation.

# Outcome 2: effective operator is still low, but cheap adapters recover it

Then your current Hypothesis C is probably right. You have I_i that look
different directly, but there exist cheap e_i, d_i with d_i . I_i . e_i ~
S(alpha_i). This is H28, but now at exactly the stage where the problem
originates. Test adapters in an explicit complexity ladder: identity -> scalar/
diagonal -> orthogonal linear -> low-rank linear -> full linear. Stop as soon as
structure recovers. For each adapter family require D*(e_i) + D*(d_i) <<
D*(I_i), and evaluate on disjoint interventions. If only full expressive
adapters work, don't count it — they can simply hide the computation. The
interesting result is: a tiny coordinate change reveals a large shared
computation.

# Outcome 3: effective operator is low and cheap adapters fail

Then test your registered B next: the learner has chosen a different
basis/decomposition. But this likely requires a GLOBAL rather than
per-abstraction coordinate transformation. Imagine the teacher basis is
A_1..A_4 while wake represents B_1 = A_1 + A_2, B_2 = A_1 - A_2, etc. No
individual adapter between B_i and A_j will solve this. But a global rotation of
the computational dictionary might: A = Q B. So search for a low-cost global
change of basis over the library/task innovations that maximizes family capture
while preserving all task behavior. Critically, charge D*(Q). If one global
cheap Q turns R ~ 0.1 into R ~ 0.8, then the meta-structure exists only at the
level of the population, not in individual learned atoms. That's a different
form of canonicalization, and arguably even more interesting.

# Outcome 4: none of those recover the structure

Then the interpretation becomes much deeper: the wake objective simply does not
identify the fertile representation. The learner has enough capacity to solve
all the tasks. Teacher meta-structure exists. A schema would be economically
useful. But nothing in the current objective rewards representing related tasks
relatedly. Therefore SGD is free to choose any one of a huge equivalence class
of task-solving implementations. Most of those implementations may destroy the
possibility of cheap higher-order abstraction. That would mean post-hoc REFACTOR
is not sufficient. You need some prospective pressure during representation
formation.

# This would reveal a very important distinction

There may be three levels. BEHAVIORAL EQUIVALENCE: both solve current tasks.
CURRENT ECONOMIC EQUIVALENCE: J_now(R_1) ~ J_now(R_2), both encode current
experience equally cheaply. PROSPECTIVE EQUIVALENCE: E[C_future | R_1] ~
E[C_future | R_2] — this need not hold. And the whole project may ultimately
care about the third. Two representations can encode today's experience equally
well while one exposes A_i = S(alpha_i) and the other hides it. When a new
family member arrives, the first learns alpha_new while the second learns a
whole new operator. The first representation is more FERTILE.

# That suggests an experiment more important than ordinary refactoring

Obtain two behaviorally/current-MDL-matched representations L_A, L_B where A has
high schema accessibility and B doesn't. Give both exactly the same unseen
family member. Measure L_preq, N_shots, and Delta D* for each. If J_now(A) ~
J_now(B) but J_learn-new(A) << J_learn-new(B), you've directly demonstrated that
representations can have OPTION VALUE for future learning. That's an enormous
conceptual bridge from V5 toward the ultimate system.

# It would also tell us what "sleep" should actually optimize

Our original sleep picture was: compress current representation. V4 taught us to
compare against COMPRESS. V5 now suggests sleep might instead need to optimize

    J_sleep = D*(L) + L(D_past | L) + eta E_{T~p(T|H)}[C_adapt(T | L)].

The first two terms say don't make the past worse. The third asks which equally
good way of representing the past makes plausible future learning cheapest. That
is PROSPECTIVE REPRESENTATION LEARNING. And now "dreaming" has a concrete job:
during sleep, sample hypothetical future tasks from the regularities the model
believes exist, try adapting to them under candidate representations, and let
representations that make those hypothetical extensions easy acquire option
value. Importantly, dreams don't count as evidence that the world works this
way — they evaluate the CONSEQUENCES of representations under plausible futures.
Reality still decides whether those future distributions were useful.

# This connects extremely directly to program synthesis

A good programming language isn't merely one in which the programs you've
already written are short. It is one in which the programs you're likely to want
next are short and easy to discover. Wake can produce some arbitrary basis
{B_1..B_8} that solves everything, but another vocabulary {A_1..A_4, S} may make
the next family member S(alpha_5) almost trivial to express. So abstraction
fertility is basically: how good is this representation as a language for future
programs? That is much closer to our ultimate goal than teacher recovery.

# So I would revise the immediate experimental sequence

Don't jump straight from H29 to adapters. Do: (1) effective-operator H29 —
measure family structure over the full task-conditioned computation, not
residuals alone; (2) cheap coordinate alignment, if effective structure remains
low; (3) global basis alignment, if local adapters fail; (4) current-economy
comparison at matched behavior and D*; (5) prospective fertility — give them
unseen family members and measure adaptation cost. Only after that decide
whether the remedy is: promote the functional unit, canonicalize coordinates,
globally refactor, or change the wake objective itself.

The key thing H29 has done is eliminate a tempting but shallow story: "PROMOTE
makes bad abstractions." The result is more interesting. It looks increasingly
like: the representational choices made while solving tasks determine whether
later abstraction is even visible. That is exactly the issue we'll face when we
eventually ask the system to invent concepts, perceptual decompositions,
reasoning primitives, and programs. A learner can solve a problem without
learning it in a FORM WORTH THINKING WITH LATER. The next stage is figuring out
how to distinguish — and eventually prefer — the representations that are.
