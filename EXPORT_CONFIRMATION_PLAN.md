# The thesis

> At exact reuse, lifetime learning produces a compact learned PROGRAM LANGUAGE
> whose frozen operations systematically compose on unseen programs and unseen
> execution lengths.

Its components are preregistered INDEPENDENTLY so that a mechanistic miss cannot
erase a direct behavioural result.

# Protocol

One lifetime per world: the **support-split discrete learner** at depth 3,
`rho = 1`, `configs/v1.yaml`, `--model discrete`, generated by
`support_split_world.py` with `SupportSplitSpec()` defaults. That single artifact
supports every estimand: its trained tasks carry C1, its three held-out strata
carry C2 and C3, and depth-2/4 programs over the same teacher library carry C4
and C5.

Consequence, stated because it changes which development numbers set the
intervals: C2's interval is derived from **E2's H1 stratum** (a support-split
lifetime) rather than from E1's opportunistic holdouts, since the sealed protocol
uses support-split lifetimes throughout.

Sample: 8 held-out programs per stratum and per depth condition, seeded and
performance-blind. The replication unit is the WORLD (30 of them), so the
per-world sample is smaller than development's 12-24 and the number of worlds is
ten times larger.

Arms, unchanged from E1/E2/E8: **O** (teacher program through the E0.1 functional
assignment), **O-W** and **R-W** (another sealed world's library), **R** (route
inferred from 128 support examples, 2,000 Adam steps at lr 0.01), **S** (scratch).
Interface E1-P throughout. The variable-depth executor is used for C4/C5 and must
pass its bitwise depth-3 equivalence control on each sealed artifact.

# Estimands

Intervals are derived MECHANICALLY from the development statistics they
correspond to, and are stated as one-sided lower bounds where the development
effect is large and one-sided, because the scientific risk is shrinkage rather
than sign.

## C1 - PROGRAM REPRESENTATION (confirms E3)

- **C1a syntax sufficiency.** Storing only `(z_1..z_D)` reproduces the model
  BITWISE. Registered: 64/64 tasks in >= 28 of 30 worlds. Development: 64/64 in
  3/3.
- **C1b two-part economy.** `Q_D = log(D_private / D_program)` at behavioural
  rates. Development: 2.646 / 2.732 / 2.693 (mean 2.690). **Registered: mean
  `Q_D` >= 2.0 with >= 28 of 30 worlds individually above 1.5.** Secondary, and
  a genuinely independent quantitative prediction: the amortization point
  `N* = D*(L) / (D_private/task - D_program/task)`; development 4.32 / 3.96 /
  4.11 (mean 4.13). **Registered: mean `N*` in [3.0, 6.0].**
- **C1c causal semantics.** The gauge permutation preserves behaviour BITWISE in
  >= 28 of 30 worlds; wrong-route, shuffled-library and wrong-depth each degrade
  by >= 1.0 log unit in >= 28 of 30. Development: bitwise 3/3, degradations
  1.72-2.16.

## C2 - FROZEN EXPORT (confirms E1)

`G_export = (L_S - L_R)/(L_S - L_O)` on the H1 stratum, reported with raw
`L_O`, `L_R`, `L_S`, and with the oracle and inference legs SEPARATED so a
degradation in `R` cannot be confused with a failure of the vocabulary.

Development `G_export` spans 0.983-1.039. **Registered: mean `G_export` in
[0.75, 1.15]**, and separately **`log L_S - log L_O` >= 1.5 in >= 28 of 30
worlds** (the vocabulary-expressibility leg; development 2.04-2.79).

## C5 - COMPOSITIONAL DRIFT (mechanism, scored separately)

Development, oracle per-step errors at depth 4, fitted `log e_t = a + b t`:
`b` = 0.661 / 0.566 / 0.566 (mean 0.598). Continuation ratio
`q = (e_4/e_3) / mean(e_2/e_1, e_3/e_2)`: **0.611 / 0.745 / 0.687 (mean 0.681)**.

Review 79 proposed predicting `q ~ 1` ("the fourth call behaves like another
ordinary application"). **Development says otherwise and the interval is derived
from the data, not the proposal**: the step ratios DECELERATE (2.99 -> 1.74 ->
1.45 in world 0), so the fourth call adds proportionally LESS than the early
ones. Registering `q ~ 1` would have preregistered a prediction our own
development data contradicts.

**Registered: mean `b` > 0 with `b` in [0.3, 0.9]; mean `q` in [0.45, 0.95]; and
NO discontinuity — `q < 1.5` in >= 28 of 30 worlds.** The scientific statement is
that depth-4 degradation is a CONTINUATION of ordinary compositional drift, in
fact a decelerating one, rather than a novel-position failure.

# Fatal structural assertions (they fail the run, they do not warn)

- every held-out program is absent from the training set;
- every H2 program contains an adjacent pair absent from training;
- every H3 program places a primitive in a `(primitive, position)` pair absent
  from every training program;
- the split satisfies its registered coverage, balance and context constraints;
- `D_train = 3` and `D_test in {2, 4}` for C4/C5;
- the variable-depth executor reproduces the shipped one BITWISE at depth 3;
- `R` and `S` each reduce their own training objective by > 1%
  (mode-consistent, E1 Amendment 2);
- every behavioural rate is non-saturated at the 12-bit ceiling.

# Registered predictions

Ours: C1a, C1c and C3 pass comfortably (the development effects are 10-20x the
registered floors). C1b passes with `Q_D` near 2.6-2.7 and `N*` near 4. C2 passes.
**C4 is the one we would bet against most readily** — its development minimum
(1.761) is the closest of any estimand to its floor, and depth 4 is the only
condition asking the vocabulary to leave the distribution it was fitted on.
**C5's `q` is the estimand where a sealed miss would be most informative**: if `q`
comes back near 1 rather than near 0.68, the deceleration we measured was a
three-world accident and the drift model needs rebuilding.

Review 79's: expects the pattern to replicate; the stated risk is shrinkage
across thirty untouched worlds rather than absence of the phenomenon.
